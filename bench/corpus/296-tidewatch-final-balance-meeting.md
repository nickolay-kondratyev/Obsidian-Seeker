# Tidewatch Final Balance Meeting

Held February 17, 2026, three weeks before the planned Kickstarter launch, shortly after the print proof correction was confirmed. Dana, Kofi, and Tomasz attended in person; Priyam joined for twenty minutes to weigh in on storm card iconography before leaving for another commitment. This was framed from the start as the last meeting where core numbers could change before the rulebook gets locked for the print vendor's final file, so the agenda was deliberately narrow: only items that affect the numeric core of the game, not wording, art, or logistics. Kofi took notes directly into the shared balance spreadsheet so changes would be traceable against the playtest data referenced throughout the discussion, most of it pulled from session logs going back to [[283-tidewatch-journal-near-loss]] and several more recent sessions run specifically to stress-test the current numbers one final time.

## Storm Deck Review

The storm deck currently has thirty-six cards across three intensity bands: calm, rising, and surge. Kofi presented data from the last twelve tracked sessions showing that surge cards are being drawn slightly more often than the intended ratio in the back third of the deck, because the shuffle-and-reinsert mechanic for spent storm cards clusters intensity more than the original design intended, a known issue that was flagged loosely months ago but never fully investigated with real numbers until this week. After some discussion, the group agreed to change the reinsertion rule so that spent surge cards go into the bottom third of the deck rather than being fully reshuffled, which should smooth the intensity curve without touching the card count or requiring new art. Tomasz asked whether this affects the printed card backs at all; it doesn't, since it's a rule change rather than a component change, so no new print files or vendor lead time are needed for this particular fix.

Kofi walked through the raw draw-frequency numbers card by card, showing the effect concentrated almost entirely in the final third of each deck cycle, exactly where the clustering theory predicted, which gave the group real confidence the diagnosis was correct rather than a coincidence in a small sample. Dana asked whether a full deck rebuild, changing the actual ratio of calm to rising to surge cards, would be cleaner than adjusting reinsertion, but the group agreed a full rebuild this close to print risked new, unknown problems without runway left to test them. The reinsertion fix was preferred because it's reversible and testable in a single evening.

## Keeper Action Costs

This was the longest discussion of the meeting. The current keeper actions cost either one or two action points, and Dana has long suspected the two-point "reinforce" action is undercosted relative to how strong it is in practice, since it can fully cancel a coming surge if timed right. Kofi pulled numbers showing reinforce is used in roughly seventy percent of turns across recent sessions, which is higher than any other action by a wide margin, suggesting it may indeed be pulling too much weight relative to its cost. Three options were debated: raising its cost to three action points, which risks making it feel punishing and rarely worth taking; adding a cooldown so it can't be used two turns in a row, which adds a tracking burden Kofi worried would slow down new players; or leaving the cost alone but reducing its effect from fully canceling a surge to only reducing it by half. The group landed on the third option after Dana pointed out that a partial reduction preserves the tactical feel of the action while removing the "solved" quality that made every session converge on the same strategy. Tomasz raised a concern that this change is untested and asked whether there's time for even one more playtest round before numbers lock; Kofi confirmed there's a small window and volunteered to run two sessions this week specifically targeting this change, with results due back before the file goes to the print vendor, since a numeric change this close to launch genuinely needs its own dedicated validation rather than being folded quietly into general confidence from earlier testing.

Dana also raised a smaller question: should the reduced reinforce effect scale with player count, since four-player games already have more actions per storm phase than two-player games, and a flat fifty-percent reduction might land differently depending on table size. Kofi lacked data split by player count and admitted the sample per count is too thin for real analysis. The group noted this as an open question but didn't block today's decision on it, since gathering enough sessions per count would take longer than the schedule allows before Friday's lock.

## Tide Token Supply

Currently there are twenty-four tide tokens in the box, and multiple playtesters have reported running out during particularly bad storm sequences, forcing awkward workarounds with substitute tokens that aren't part of the intended experience. Dana proposed increasing the supply to thirty-two tokens, which fits the current box insert without a redesign, according to Tomasz's read of the die-cut tray specs already approved for print. The cost impact is minor since tide tokens are cheap to mold in bulk, and Tomasz confirmed the vendor quote already assumed some buffer above the current count for exactly this kind of late adjustment, so this change should not affect the manufacturing plan's cost estimates. The group approved the increase without much debate, since it fixes a real friction point testers have named consistently and costs almost nothing to implement this late in the process.

Kofi raised one adjacent question worth recording even though it doesn't change today's decision: whether the token shortage is purely a supply problem, or partly a side effect of reinforce being overused, since a cheaper, more frequently canceled surge naturally consumes more tokens per session. If the reinforce change lands as expected, token consumption may drop on its own, meaning thirty-two could end up more generous than necessary. Nobody wanted to hold up the decision waiting on that, since extra tokens cost almost nothing, but Kofi will track token usage alongside the reinforce data this week to see whether the two numbers move together as the theory predicts.

## Decisions and Next Steps

Three numeric changes are locked: storm card reinsertion goes to the bottom third rather than a full reshuffle, reinforce now reduces a surge by half instead of canceling it, and tide token count increases from twenty-four to thirty-two. Kofi will update the master rules document and run two validation sessions on the reinforce change this week, reporting results by Thursday. Tomasz will confirm token count and card reinsertion changes require no new print files. No further numeric changes will be considered after Thursday's results land; the rulebook text locks for the print vendor Friday regardless of outcome, since the print schedule cannot slip further without threatening the Kickstarter launch date.

Dana will notify Priyam once the reinforce icon wording is finalized, since the storm card iconography discussed earlier depends on final effect text rather than placeholder language. Lotte will be looped in by email once numbers lock Friday, since the campaign page's rules overview, part of the ongoing [[292-tidewatch-kickstarter-prep]] work, references reinforce by its old, uncapped description and needs a copy pass before publication.
