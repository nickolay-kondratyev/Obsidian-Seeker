Choosing hiking boots takes longer than most people expect, and rushing it is how you end up with blisters at mile three of a multi-day trip.

## Step 1: Know Your Terrain

**Step 1: know your terrain first.** Rocky and technical trails want a stiffer sole and more ankle support; smooth graded trails are fine in something much lighter, even a trail runner instead of a boot at all.

## Step 2: Time Your Shopping Trip

**Step 2: shop in the afternoon.** Feet swell during the day, so morning fittings run small.

## Step 3: Bring the Right Socks

**Step 3: bring your actual hiking socks.** Fit changes a full size between a thin liner sock and a cushioned wool hiking sock, so trying boots on with the wrong socks wastes the whole fitting. Bring the pair you'll actually wear on trail, not whatever you happened to put on that morning.

## Step 4: Check the Toe Box

**Step 4: check the toe box on a decline.** Most stores have a small ramp for exactly this; if your toes hit the front, size up.

## Step 5: Walk Around

**Step 5: walk around for at least fifteen minutes.** Not just around the register, actually walk laps, go up stairs if the store has them, and pay attention to any early pressure points before you commit to buying.

## Step 6: Test at Home

**Step 6: don't trust day one comfort alone.** Wear them around the house first.

## Step 7: Break Them In

**Step 7: break them in gradually before a trip.** Start with short walks, then loaded day hikes, building toward at least fifty miles of real use before trusting them on a multi-day trip where a bad fit has no easy fix. See [[269-how-to-break-in-boots]] for the full break-in schedule I follow, and [[244-how-to-pack-backpack]] if you're prepping the rest of your kit at the same time. Skip this step and you risk a hot spot on day two of a trip instead of a cheap two-mile walk around the neighborhood.
