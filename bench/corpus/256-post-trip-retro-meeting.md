Post-trip retro for the Wonderland Trail loop, a week after getting back, over pizza at Kofi's place. Attendees: Dana, Priyam, Kofi. Goal was to capture what worked and what didn't while it's still fresh, before memory smooths over the annoying details we'll want to fix next time. We went through the trip roughly day by day, comparing notes against the original plan in [[242-wonderland-trail-project]] to see where reality diverged. Kofi brought printouts of his tracking app data, elevation and pace for each day, which turned out to be genuinely useful for grounding some fuzzy memories in actual numbers instead of vague impressions. We also each brought one specific complaint and one specific highlight to make sure the conversation didn't just drift into pure nostalgia without capturing anything useful for next time.

## What Went Well

Overall the trip went well: no injuries, weather held for all nine days, and the gear cuts from the shakedown meeting paid off in a real, noticeable way on the climbs. Biggest surprise was how much faster we moved than planned on the flatter middle days, finishing two of them with hours of daylight left over, which suggests our mileage estimates were conservative for this specific terrain. The one rough patch was day six, the rest day near Sunrise, which we almost skipped when the weather looked fine that morning, but Priyam pushed to keep it and it turned out to be the right call once everyone's legs started complaining that afternoon. Food planning worked well overall, though we all agreed the dinners got repetitive by day seven, and next time we want more variety even if it means slightly more complex meal prep beforehand. The resupply bucket at Longmire was well stocked and well timed, arriving exactly when we needed it, and Dana's system of pre-portioning everything into daily bags made camp routines noticeably faster than past trips. Weather forecasting the week before turned out to be more accurate than we expected, which let us pack slightly lighter rain gear than usual without any regrets once we were actually out there. Water sources matched the guidebook descriptions closely, no surprises there, which made planning refill stops straightforward for all nine days without any anxious moments about running dry.

## Gear Retro

Gear retro: the tarp-versus-tent debate resolved itself in the tent's favor, since bug pressure near Indian Bar was exactly as bad as feared, and nobody regretted the extra half pound once mosquitoes became a real factor in the evenings. Trail runners performed well for Dana and Priyam with no blisters, though Priyam's pair showed real wear by the end and probably needs replacing before the next big trip rather than getting squeezed for more mileage. Kofi's boots held up fine, no complaints, though he noted his feet were more tired by day nine than he expected given how broken in they were supposed to be going in. The satellite messenger got used twice, both times just for routine check-ins rather than anything urgent, but everyone agreed it was worth the rental cost purely for the peace of mind it gave people back home. Water filter performance was solid all trip, no clogging issues this time, which Kofi attributes partly to being more careful about where he collects water after last summer's incident. One real gap: we didn't bring enough sunscreen, running out on day seven and rationing for the last two days on exposed ridgelines, which is an easy fix for next time but was a genuine annoyance in the moment. Trekking poles were unanimously worth their weight, especially on the long descent into Longmire, and nobody is considering going without them on future trips no matter how much other gear gets cut. The bear canisters were annoying to pack but never actually inconvenient in use, and we didn't have a single wildlife encounter at camp all trip, which everyone agreed was a good trade for the extra bulk. Dana's lighter sleeping bag swap worked out fine; she reported being slightly cold only once, the first night at Devils Dream, and layered up rather than regretting the switch. Overall gear performance was the best we've had on a trip this length, which we're attributing directly to the shakedown meeting two weeks before departure catching most of the real problems ahead of time.

## Group Dynamics

Group dynamics were good throughout, no real friction, though we noted that Kofi tends to push pace too hard on climbs and should probably call breaks more proactively instead of waiting for someone else to ask. Communication around daily plans worked well, reviewing the next day's route each evening at camp, and we want to keep that habit for future trips since it kept everyone on the same page about mileage and water sources. Morale stayed high the whole trip, helped a lot by the rest day decision, and by generally not overplanning every minute, leaving room for spontaneous stops at good viewpoints instead of rigidly sticking to a pace chart. Priyam mentioned wanting more solo reflection time built into future trips, not necessarily hiking alone, but quiet stretches without conversation, which the rest of us were happy to accommodate once she raised it. Kofi agreed to try calling breaks more proactively on climbs instead of waiting for someone to ask, and both Dana and Priyam said they'd appreciate that, since neither of them likes being the one to slow the group down by speaking up first. We also talked about how well the rotating camp chore system worked, everyone taking turns on water, cooking, and tent setup rather than falling into the same roles every night out of habit. Dana pointed out that having a clear default plan for each evening, reviewed the night before, cut down on the low-grade decision fatigue that's crept into past trips by the final few days.

## Takeaways

Takeaways for next time: more meal variety, extra sunscreen, replace Priyam's trail runners before the next trip, and keep the daily route review habit going. Also agreed to build in more quiet stretches without forcing conversation, since that seemed to genuinely improve the trip for at least one of us and probably all three if we're honest about it. Already talking about what's next; see [[252-sierra-high-route-project]] for the early planning on our next big objective. For now, savoring this one; it's easily the best trip we've done together, and it set a solid bar for whatever comes next, especially given how smoothly the logistics came together after all that shakedown planning. Kofi is already drafting a gear list update based on tonight's notes, and Dana volunteered to keep the shared spreadsheet current so we're not reconstructing all of this from memory again next time. Priyam is pulling together a short photo album from the trip to share with the group chat this week, mostly the sunrise shots from the summit push and the marmot that wouldn't leave us alone at camp.
