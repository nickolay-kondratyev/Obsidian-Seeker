Planning meeting for the neighborhood harvest festival bake sale, a joint effort between the bread club and the wider ferment club this year rather than the bread club running it alone like the past two years. Present: Dana, Lotte, Tomasz, and Priyam, standing in for the ferment club side since Kofi couldn't make the evening work. Held over video call rather than in person, since everyone was scattered across different parts of town that evening and a quick call felt like less friction than rescheduling around four calendars. The purpose was dividing responsibilities between the two groups and settling what actually gets sold, given that combining forces this year roughly doubles both the expected turnout and the expected inventory compared to previous years.

## What gets sold

Bread club handles loaves and baked goods as usual, but ferment club is adding a table this year: small jars of kimchi, pickles, and jam for sale alongside the bread, something that's never been part of the festival before. Tomasz raised concern about food safety rules for selling fermented goods versus baked goods, since the two might fall under different local regulations, and Priyam agreed to look into whether a permit or specific labeling is required before committing to the fermented goods table publicly. Dana suggested keeping the fermented offerings smaller and more curated than the bread table to start, maybe six or eight jar varieties total rather than trying to showcase everyone's personal projects, which would also make inventory and pricing simpler for a first joint year. Everyone agreed that starting smaller and expanding next year if it goes well is safer than overcommitting on the first combined attempt.

## Roles and responsibilities

Dana and Lotte handle the bread table, both baking and running it during the festival, since that's their established territory from the past two years and changing that now would just add confusion. Priyam is researching the permit question this week and will report back before the next planning call, since that answer determines whether the fermented table happens at all this year or gets pushed to next year instead. Tomasz volunteered to handle pricing and a simple cash box setup for both tables combined, having done something similar for a school fundraiser last year and having actual relevant experience nobody else in the group currently has. Kofi, once he's looped in, will likely handle the kimchi and pickle portion specifically given his farmer's market labeling experience from [[126-canning-swap-planning-meeting]], assuming the permit question comes back clear rather than complicated.

## Open questions

Still unresolved: exact table layout at the festival grounds, which depends on where the organizers assign the joint booth, and whether profits get split evenly between the two groups or allocated toward each group's own future equipment funds separately, a question nobody wanted to rush through on a call already running long.
