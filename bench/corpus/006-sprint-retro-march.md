Sprint retro for the two weeks ending 2026-03-13, the sprint where we shipped invoice PDF export and finished the `ledger_entries` migration from [[001-sprint-planning-w1]]. Attendees: Dana, Priyam, Kofi, Lotte, all present, nobody remote this time since Kofi is back from his week off. Format was the usual three columns — what went well, what didn't, what we'll try next sprint — with fifteen minutes of silent writing before discussion, which continues to produce better notes than jumping straight into talking over each other. Dana facilitated and kept rough time, though we ran about ten minutes over because the migration-ordering discussion needed more room than usual. Lotte took notes directly into this document instead of a scratch pad, which made the writeup afterward faster, so we're keeping that habit going forward.

## What went well

Everyone agreed the PDF export feature landed cleanly, largely because Dana and Priyam split the renderer service and the UI early and kept interfaces stable the whole sprint, so neither side blocked the other waiting on a shape change. Kofi's migration plan doc, written before touching production, caught an ordering problem that would have caused downtime on the `ledger_entries` table during business hours — the kind of near-miss that's easy to feel good about only in hindsight, but the team wanted to explicitly praise the practice of writing the plan down first rather than treating it as bureaucracy. Lotte noted that deferring custom branding, decided in the planning meeting, was the right call in practice: it kept scope tight enough that nothing slipped, and the follow-up ticket she wrote is now clearly scoped for next sprint instead of being a vague afterthought. Priyam also called out that the loading-state work on the export button turned out to matter more than expected, since PDF generation for large invoices with more than twenty line items takes nearly four seconds, and a silent button would have looked broken to real users kicking off an export. The team also appreciated that standups stayed short this sprint — nobody rambled, blockers surfaced fast, and Kofi's Tuesday pairing session with Dana on migration ordering happened exactly as planned instead of slipping to Thursday like it usually does. Dana thanked Kofi for writing the migration plan as a checklist rather than prose, saying it made the pairing session faster because there was no ambiguity about what "done" meant for each step. Lotte added that support didn't get a single confused message about the new export button.

## What didn't go well

The font licensing question for the default invoice template dragged out longer than it should have — Dana didn't confirm commercial-use terms until Thursday of the second week, which meant Priyam had a visual gap in testing for several days and had to fall back to a placeholder font that looked noticeably different from what would ship. Nobody flagged this as a blocker early enough in standup, which the team agreed was the real failure, not the licensing question itself. Separately, the migration plan doc Kofi wrote was thorough but landed in a private draft rather than the shared channel until the day before it ran, so Dana only got to review it under time pressure instead of with room to push back on anything. There was also friction around the `ledger_entries` migration touching a table that the export feature's read queries also hit; the two workstreams weren't sequenced with each other explicitly, and it was mostly luck rather than careful planning that they didn't collide in a way that broke staging, let alone production, during the two days both were in flight simultaneously. Kofi admitted he only noticed the overlap on Wednesday afternoon while reviewing his own migration diff a second time out of habit, not because anyone had flagged the shared table earlier in planning, which is exactly the kind of near-miss that feels lucky rather than earned. Lotte pointed out that the branding follow-up ticket, while well scoped, doesn't yet have rough sketches attached, which she'd committed to delivering this sprint and didn't, mostly because writing the ticket itself ate more time than expected once she started digging into competitor invoice templates for reference. Priyam mentioned a smaller but real annoyance: the export button's loading state was built against a mocked three-second delay, and nobody re-tested it against the real renderer until the day before demo, when it turned out real PDFs occasionally took closer to six seconds for invoices with attached line-item notes, which briefly looked like a hang during the walkthrough.

## What we'll try next sprint

The team agreed on a few concrete changes. First, any ticket owner who discovers a licensing, legal, or third-party dependency question must flag it in standup the same day, not wait until it's resolved — partial information is more useful than a surprise later, and Dana specifically committed to modeling this next sprint given it was her miss this time around. Second, Kofi will post migration plans to the shared channel at least a day before running anything, no exceptions, even for small migrations that feel obviously safe, since the whole point is giving the team a chance to catch what one person alone might miss, not just documenting intent after the fact. Third, when two tickets touch the same table in the same sprint, whoever notices first should say so explicitly in planning rather than assuming it'll be fine, and the two owners should have a short sync before either starts writing code against it. Fourth, Lotte will timebox ticket-writing more aggressively — thirty minutes per ticket, not an open-ended session — so that sketches and scoping both happen instead of one crowding out the other entirely. Everyone signed off that these are small enough to actually stick, rather than the kind of retro action item that sounds good and quietly evaporates by the following Monday once the next sprint's pressure kicks back in, and Kofi volunteered to bring it up again at planning if he notices anyone slipping.

## Numbers and mood

Velocity: 34 points completed against 38 committed, close enough that nobody wants to over-index on the miss, especially with the font-licensing delay eating most of the gap on its own. Bugs filed against this sprint's work: two, both minor and already fixed before the retro even started. Team mood check, one to five, self-reported anonymously: Dana 4, Priyam 4, Kofi 5, Lotte 3 — Lotte's lower score tracked back to the branding ticket slipping, which she'd already named honestly in the retro itself rather than letting it surface only in the anonymous number. General sentiment was that the sprint felt sustainable, no late nights, and the near-miss on migration ordering was scary enough in retrospect that the new process changes feel earned rather than performative box-checking nobody actually intends to follow. Dana closed the meeting by suggesting we revisit the shared-table sequencing rule specifically at the next retro, to make sure it survived contact with a real sprint instead of just sounding good on the day we wrote it down.
