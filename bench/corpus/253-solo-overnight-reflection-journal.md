Solo overnight up at the old fire lookout ridge, first time going out alone since the JMT trip last year. Left the trailhead at eleven, later than planned, but the forecast was clear enough that a late start didn't worry me. The climb up is one I've done with the group before, but doing it alone changed the whole texture of the day; no conversation to fill the quiet, just my own footsteps and the occasional bird. I noticed things I usually miss when I'm talking with Dana or Kofi: the specific smell of sun-warmed pine sap near the switchbacks, a section of trail where the rock underfoot changes from granite to something darker and more crumbly. Made camp near the old lookout foundation by four, plenty of daylight left, so I sat and just watched the light change across the valley for almost two hours before bothering to cook dinner. There's a specific kind of peace that solo trips give you that group trips just can't, not better or worse, just different, and I think I'd forgotten how much I needed a dose of it until I was actually sitting there alone. Set up the tent facing east, mostly for the sunrise, though I half suspect I'll be too lazy to actually crawl out and watch it when the alarm goes off tomorrow. Text to Priyam before I lost signal: made it up fine, all good, back by tomorrow afternoon, which felt like a small ritual in itself, a last tether to the ordinary world before the quiet really set in.

## Making Camp

Cooked dinner slowly, no rush, which is its own small luxury when there's no one else's hunger or schedule to account for. Watched the sunset properly for once instead of half paying attention while breaking down camp or setting up a tent, and it turned the ridge a deep orange-red that I don't think I've ever seen quite that vivid before. Journaled for a while by headlamp after dark, working through some stuff about work that's been sitting unresolved in my head for weeks, the kind of thinking that only really happens when there's nothing else competing for attention. Slept surprisingly well for a solo trip; I usually sleep lighter alone, more alert to every sound, but something about being this high up and this far from any trail junction made me feel more secure rather than less. Woke once around two to a coyote calling somewhere down in the valley, distant and not remotely threatening, just a reminder that the place is alive in ways that are easy to forget from inside a tent. Morning came clear and cold, frost on the rainfly, and I lay there for a while just listening before actually getting up, in no hurry to end the quiet. Coffee at the old lookout foundation as the sun properly cleared the ridge across the valley was one of those small perfect moments that's hard to plan for and always seems to happen anyway when you're not trying too hard. I sat there longer than I needed to, watching a hawk work the thermals along the ridgeline, in no hurry to start packing up and end the quiet of the morning. Wrote three full pages in the journal, more than I usually manage in a week at home, which says something about how much clearer my head gets once there's nothing else demanding attention for a stretch of hours. Some of it was about work, some about nothing in particular, just noticing things: the exact number of switchbacks I'd counted without meaning to, the specific ache in my left shoulder from a pack strap that needs adjusting, the smell of the stove fuel mixing with pine.

## Heading Back

Packed up slowly, in no rush to leave, and started down around nine, taking the descent easy since there was nowhere else I needed to be. Passed two day hikers on their way up who asked how the views were, and I probably talked their ear off for longer than they expected from someone they'd just met on a trail. Back at the car by noon, and the drive home felt different than usual, quieter in my head in a way that group trips don't quite produce, though I love those too for different reasons entirely. Stopped at the little diner on the highway for a real breakfast before getting back on the road, still half in trail mode and half already thinking about the week ahead. Texted Priyam and Kofi a photo from the lookout foundation, and Priyam immediately asked when we're going back as a group, which made me smile more than it probably should have. Traffic on the drive back was lighter than expected for a Sunday, which felt like one more small piece of luck on a trip that had gone smoothly from start to finish.

## Takeaways

Thinking about doing this once a season going forward, a short solo trip as a reset rather than always defaulting to group trips with Dana and Kofi. There's room for both kinds of trips, and I don't think I fully understood that until this weekend gave me a clear side-by-side comparison of what each one actually offers. Next solo trip: maybe the ridge above the reservoir, or somewhere I haven't been before, still deciding. Want to try a two-night version at some point too, once I've built up more confidence with the shorter overnight format and know I can handle the quiet without getting restless by the second evening. Adding this to the rotation alongside the loaded hikes; it's a different kind of practice, but a real one.
