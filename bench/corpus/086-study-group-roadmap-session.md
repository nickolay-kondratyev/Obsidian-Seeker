Tuesday evening study group, week fourteen. Attendees: the organizer (me), Dana, Kofi, and Lotte, all present for once, which hasn't happened since the very first session back in [[061-rust-study-group-kickoff]]. Meeting ran a bit long, about ninety minutes instead of the usual sixty, because the roadmap conversation kept branching into side discussions about everyone's individual projects and where they were each getting stuck. Held at the usual spot, laptops out, one whiteboard photo taken for the record. Agenda was short on paper: review where each person's project stands, agree on a shared reading target for the next month, and decide whether to keep the current format or shift toward more structured pairing sessions given that a few people mentioned feeling like they were plateauing on their own between meetings.

## Project status roundup

Dana reported the most progress. Her lifetime-heavy parser project, the one she's been teaching concepts from during pairing sessions like [[076-pairing-on-lifetimes]], now handles nested expressions correctly after weeks of fighting the borrow checker on recursive structures. She demoed a small snippet parsing arithmetic with parentheses and got a round of applause, mostly because everyone remembered how stuck she'd been on this exact case three weeks ago. Kofi's project, a small key-value store with a custom binary log format, is stalled on choosing a serialization approach; he's torn between rolling his own minimal format and pulling in an existing crate, which sparked a tangent about dependency bloat versus reinventing wheels for a learning project, where the group leaned toward "roll your own since the whole point is learning the format details." Lotte's file-watcher tool is essentially done and she's now polishing error messages and adding a config file, more product polish than new concepts at this point, though she mentioned wanting a proper test suite before calling it finished, which nobody else had prioritized yet. I gave a short update on the mini-grep clone and mentioned I've mostly moved to reading this week, still chewing on async and the Pin question from last week's journal entry.

Everyone agreed the variety of projects, despite covering very different problem domains, keeps discussions useful since each person hits different edges of the language and brings back a different kind of frustration or insight to share with the rest of the group, which was cited as one of the format's biggest strengths over just following a shared curriculum in lockstep.

## Reading target discussion

Long discussion about what to read together next. Options on the table: continuing chapter by chapter through the official book, which the group has been loosely following since the start; switching to "Effective Rust," which Kofi already owns and has been recommending informally for a couple of weeks now, as mentioned separately in [[085-effective-rust-item-notes]]; or picking a focused topic, like concurrency, and reading whatever's best on that specific subject regardless of source. Dana pushed hard for the concurrency-focused option, arguing that scattered self-teaching across the group has left obvious gaps, herself included, and that a shared deep dive would be more valuable at this stage than continuing broad coverage of topics some people already know reasonably well from other languages.

After some back and forth, the group landed on a hybrid: finish out the current book chapters over the next two weeks since most people are close to done anyway and abandoning it now feels wasteful, then shift to a four-week concurrency-focused block using a mix of the book's concurrency chapter and outside articles people find and share as we go, rather than committing to one single text upfront that might turn out to be a poor fit for the group's actual skill level. Kofi volunteered to compile a running reading list in a shared note rather than relying on people remembering links from chat, which everyone agreed was overdue anyway. Lotte suggested keeping the list short and curated instead of exhaustive, no more than a handful of core links per week, so people don't get overwhelmed and quietly give up on the reading entirely, a fair concern given how ambitious some past assignments have quietly gone unread. Someone floated the idea of picking a small shared concurrency exercise everyone attempts individually before the block starts, just to get a rough sense of where the group's baseline actually is going in, and the group agreed to think about a concrete example over the next week rather than deciding on the spot without a clearer picture of what that exercise should even look like.

## Format discussion

Raised the plateau concern directly: a few people, including me, mentioned that solo project work between sessions sometimes stalls for a week with no clear next step, and that having someone to ask in the moment, rather than saving the question for the next Tuesday, would help a lot given how much time a small blocker can eat when you're working alone in the evenings after a full day of unrelated work. Discussed adding a midweek optional pairing slot, informal, no fixed agenda, just whoever's free and stuck on something hopping on a call together for half an hour or so. Nobody wanted to commit to it being mandatory given everyone's unpredictable schedules and the general evening-hours-only nature of this whole effort, but the general sentiment was clearly in favor of trying it as a loose, no-pressure option rather than not offering it at all and just letting people quietly struggle solo through the harder weeks.

Landed on: a shared thread for "stuck, anyone free this week" style pings, no calendar invites, fully opt-in, revisit whether it's actually getting used again in a month once there's enough real usage to judge instead of guessing upfront. Someone also suggested logging what specifically got people unstuck in that thread afterward, even briefly, so the group slowly builds a small searchable archive of solved problems instead of the same borrow-checker questions quietly resurfacing for each new person who hits them a few weeks later without anyone remembering the earlier conversation already covered it.
