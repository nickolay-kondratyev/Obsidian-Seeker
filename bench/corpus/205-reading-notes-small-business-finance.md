Notes from *Cash Flow for Small Creative Businesses*, borrowed from the library on Dana's recommendation.

## Main Thesis

Profit on paper means nothing if cash actually arrives late. Timing is the real business.

## The 30-Day Trap

Standard payment terms assume clients pay on time. Chapter three argues most don't, reliably. Recommends a buffer of two months' expenses, not one.

## What I'm Already Doing

Quarterly tax savings already covers part of this instinctively, just never framed as buffering before.

## Where It Gets Thin

Most examples are retail or restaurant businesses with daily cash flow. A seasonal service business like mine, with lumpy bookings, needed real translation to actually apply, chapter five's monthly-recurring-revenue framing doesn't map cleanly at all to a studio that books unevenly across the year.

## Action Taken

Bumping my buffer target from six weeks of expenses to a full two months, starting this quarter's savings transfer immediately rather than phasing it in gradually.

## Rating

Three stars, useful chapter three, skip most of the rest honestly, not worth owning.
