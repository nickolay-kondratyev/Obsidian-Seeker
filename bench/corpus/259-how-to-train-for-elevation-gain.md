Training for elevation gain is different from general hiking fitness. Treating them the same is how people end up destroyed on day one.

## Step 1: Find Your Elevation Source

**Step 1: find your local elevation source.** A stadium, parking garage stairwell, or steep local trail all work; consistency matters more than the specific location you choose for these repeat sessions each week of the block.

## Step 2: Start Unloaded

**Step 2: start unloaded.** Build the movement pattern first, add weight later.

## Step 3: Add Weight Gradually

**Step 3: add a weighted pack gradually.** Roughly two to three pounds per week is a reasonable pace, working toward your actual trip weight over six to eight weeks rather than jumping straight to it. Jumping straight to full weight is the fastest route to a knee or hip injury before the trip even starts.

## Step 4: Train the Descent

**Step 4: train the descent too.** Downhill under load is its own skill, often neglected entirely by people who only train the climb.

## Step 5: Single-Leg Strength

**Step 5: add single-leg strength work.** Step-ups and lunges done twice a week build the specific stability that steep, uneven terrain demands, in a way flat-ground cardio never quite reaches on its own over time, no matter how much of it you do.

## Step 6: Taper

**Step 6: taper properly.** Rest matters as much as the training itself.

## Step 7: Match Terrain

**Step 7: match training terrain to trip terrain as closely as you can.** Sustained climbing, technical footing, altitude if your trip has it; the closer your training resembles the actual trip, the fewer surprises you'll have once you're actually out there carrying a full pack for real. Nothing fully replicates a nine-day trip, but a few solid loaded weekend hikes on similar terrain get you closer than any amount of treadmill incline work ever will.
