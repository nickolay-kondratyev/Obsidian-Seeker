Planning doc for our first winter overnight together, a valley route near the ridge we usually hike in summer, but under snow this time. One night, low mileage, roughly five miles each way, kept deliberately conservative given this is new terrain for all three of us in winter conditions. Goal is building confidence and testing gear rather than covering ambitious distance. If this goes well we'll plan something more ambitious next winter, but there's no reason to rush that this year.

## Route

Route follows the valley floor rather than the ridge, avoiding avalanche terrain entirely per the route planning meeting; see [[261-route-planning-meeting]] for the full discussion behind this choice. Trailhead access requires a short drive on a road that may need chains depending on recent snowfall, so we're checking conditions the morning of departure rather than assuming the usual access holds. Camp will be set up in a sheltered stand of trees about two and a half miles in, chosen from satellite imagery and confirmed against a recent trip report from another group that used the same general area last winter. We're building in a turnaround time of two in the afternoon regardless of progress, given how much shorter winter daylight is compared to the trips we're used to planning around in summer.

## Gear

Gear list differs substantially from our usual kit: four-season tent rented for the trip, winter-rated sleeping bags, and a stove capable of reliably melting snow for water since standing sources will likely be frozen solid this time of year. Layering plan is base, mid, and shell for everyone, plus one dedicated dry sleep layer that never gets worn during the day, a rule we're all committing to strictly after Kofi's cautionary story about a damp sleeping bag on a past trip. Snowshoes versus microspikes is still undecided, depending on how much fresh snow falls in the week before departure, so we're packing both options in the car and deciding at the trailhead itself. Food planning includes extra calories beyond our usual trip rations, since staying warm burns significantly more energy than summer hiking, and we're bringing a thermos of something hot for the morning specifically to make the cold start easier. We're also each carrying an extra pair of dry socks and gloves, cheap insurance against the specific misery of wet hands and feet in freezing temperatures.

## Risks

Biggest risk is simply being new to winter conditions; none of us has real experience reading snow stability or managing cold-weather emergencies beyond basic training. Mitigating this with the conservative valley route, a backyard gear test night beforehand, and a hard turnaround time that doesn't depend on how the day is actually going.

## Looking Ahead

If this goes well, we're considering a longer winter trip next season, possibly the ridge route we avoided this time once we've built real experience and confidence in colder conditions. Treating this whole trip as a deliberate first step rather than the destination itself, which takes some pressure off if things don't go perfectly. Debrief planned the week after, similar to [[256-post-trip-retro-meeting]], to capture lessons while they're still fresh in everyone's memory. We're also planning to write down what surprised us about winter conditions specifically, since that's the kind of detail that's easy to forget once summer trips resume and the memory of the cold fades. Whatever happens, this is a useful data point for whether winter trips become a regular part of our rotation or stay a one-off experiment.
