Q2 planning session, just me and Priyam, studio, Saturday morning coffee-fueled two-hour block instead of the usual quick Monday check-ins we default to most weeks. We do this properly maybe twice a year, and it's tempting to skip when things feel like they're already running fine, but the last time we skipped a quarterly session, small scheduling assumptions drifted apart between us for weeks before either of us noticed, so we've both gotten more disciplined about protecting this block. Purpose today was threefold: review how Q1 performed against projections, set real Q2 targets, and figure out how the relocation timeline affects what we can commit to during the move window, since that's the one variable neither of us has actually planned around carefully before this session.

## Q1 Review

Q1 revenue came in about 8% ahead of projection, driven mostly by the retainer client's first full quarter running smoothly and one unexpected rush booking from Kofi's team that filled an otherwise slow week in February that we'd originally projected as essentially empty. Overtime hours stayed roughly flat compared to Q4, a genuine relief given how rough the burnout stretch over the winter had been, and something we both specifically flagged as worth protecting going into Q2 rather than letting slip back toward the pattern from last November and December. We also reviewed the invoice aging report together for the first time this quarter, three invoices went past 30 days versus five in Q4, a real improvement Priyam credited partly to the new follow-up email template we started sending at the 25-day mark instead of waiting until a payment was already technically late before saying anything to the client about it. One thing that came up as a genuine surprise: the jewelry-specific bookings, now a deliberate focus area, actually generated a higher revenue-per-hour figure than the retainer client's steady volume work, which neither of us had actually calculated cleanly before sitting down with the real Q1 numbers side by side this morning. Priyam pulled up the editing-time logs too, and jewelry jobs, despite the focus-stacking overhead, actually took less total editing time per delivered image than the flat catalog work, mostly because there's less volume per shoot to churn through even though each individual image takes longer to process carefully.

## Q2 Targets and Relocation

We set a Q2 revenue target roughly 10% above Q1, deliberately conservative given the blackout week planned for the move, rather than pretending the relocation won't cost any real booking capacity during that stretch of the quarter. Priyam flagged that the current pace of new inquiries, about six a month, is trending up from Q1's roughly four a month, likely tied to word of mouth from the retainer client and the [[187-website-refresh-project]] portfolio updates already partially live even before the full refresh finishes rolling out across every page. We agreed to hold two flexible slots open in the two weeks immediately surrounding the move for exactly this kind of new inquiry, rather than fully booking out the calendar and having no room to say yes to something promising that comes in during what's usually a slower stretch of the year for new client outreach specifically. We also discussed whether to actively pause new-client marketing during the move week itself, and decided against it, since a two-week pause could plausibly cost more in lost momentum than the modest scheduling risk of accepting one or two new inquiries during an admittedly chaotic week, a trade-off we're both willing to accept given how the last relocation-adjacent gap in bookings felt in hindsight. Priyam also raised the idea of a small "we've moved" promotional push once the new studio is fully operational, a short discount window for returning clients to book the new space early, partly to generate some social proof images of the space in actual use and partly to smooth out what would otherwise likely be an awkward, quiet first few weeks in the new location while word slowly spreads that we've relocated at all. We didn't fully commit to the promotional idea today, it needs real numbers behind it before either of us says yes, but it's now sitting on the list for the next check-in rather than just evaporating as a good idea mentioned once in passing and then forgotten.

## Hiring Follow-Up

Circled back to the assistant hiring conversation from [[191-assistant-hiring-check-in]]. The posting is live, four applications so far, two look genuinely promising on paper, one has direct catalog-shoot experience which neither of the other two candidates have listed explicitly on their materials. Interviews scheduled for the week after the move wraps, deliberately not during the transition itself, since neither of us wants to be evaluating candidates while also unpacking cases and testing outlets for the first time in a brand new space. Priyam raised a good point about making sure whoever we hire gets proper onboarding time before the busiest part of spring hits, rather than throwing a brand new assistant straight into a packed catalog week with no ramp-up period at all, which risks a rough first impression for everyone involved and could sour the relationship before it's even had a real chance to prove out over a few normal weeks. We talked through what the first two weeks should actually look like structurally: shadowing on prep and breakdown only for the first few days, no unsupervised handling of client product at all until Priyam is confident in their care with fragile items, and a short debrief at the end of each of the first five shoot days specifically to catch small habits worth correcting early, before they calcify into something harder to unlearn once the person feels fully settled into the role and less open to correction.
