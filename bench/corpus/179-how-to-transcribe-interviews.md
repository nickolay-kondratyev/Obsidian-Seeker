Workflow for turning recorded interviews into clean, coded transcripts I can actually cite in the thesis, without losing a week to fiddly software each time.

## Before You Record

Test the recorder battery and storage the morning of, not the night before. Bring a backup phone recorder — mine has saved a session twice already this summer, once after the primary device filled up mid-conversation.

## Step 1: Record and Back Up

Run two devices at once, label with date and code, upload to encrypted storage same day.

## Step 2: First-Pass Transcript

Run the audio through the transcription tool, then do a rough listen-through fixing only obvious errors — names, place names, anything the software mangled badly. Don't chase perfection on this pass; save polish for the clean listen, where fixing tone and pauses actually matters for the eventual coding and analysis rather than just readability.

## Step 3: De-identify

Replace names, employers, and specific addresses with role-based codes before the transcript ever leaves my encrypted folder, following the same scheme used in the consent forms.

## Step 4: Clean Listen

Second listen, headphones on, correcting phrasing and marking pauses, laughter, and inaudible sections honestly instead of guessing at what was said. This pass is slower but it's where most of the analytic texture actually gets preserved.

## Step 5: Code in Software

Import the transcript, apply the codebook from [[172-interview-protocol-project]], tag carefully by theme.

## Notes on Turnaround

Budget roughly three hours of processing per one hour of audio the first month, dropping closer to two hours once the codebook stabilizes and I stop second-guessing every tag. Doing transcription within 48 hours of the interview, while the conversation is still fresh, catches far more mishearings than waiting a week and trying to reconstruct context from memory alone. I learned this the hard way after letting four interviews stack up in July and losing a full weekend to it.
