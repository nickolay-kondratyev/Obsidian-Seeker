Thesis committee check-in, held over video call, 45 minutes, minutes drafted same day. Attendees: advisor (chair), methods reader, external reader (urban policy background). I presented what's happened since the last meeting: completed stop audit for the eastbound corridor, first round of intercept surveys at Linden Ave (see [[153-fieldwork-journal-transit-survey]]), and a revised research questions draft circulated the week prior. Agenda was three items — methodology concerns on the 311 complaint proxy, survey instrument feedback following the fieldwork observations, and a timeline check against the milestones table in [[152-thesis-project-plan]]. Advisor opened by noting the audit fieldwork looks further along than expected for this point in the semester, which set a generally positive tone before the harder questions started. I flagged upfront that today's discussion would likely run long, and the committee agreed to prioritize the methodology item if time ran short.

## Methodology discussion

The methods reader raised the 311 complaint proxy first, echoing concerns I'd already logged after the Linden Ave fieldwork. Her core objection: complaint volume reflects who complains, not who's affected, and disabled or older riders may be systematically less likely to file a 311 report even when a barrier affects them daily. She pushed for either dropping question two or replacing the outcome variable entirely rather than patching it with a robustness check. Advisor pushed back gently, arguing a robustness section using paratransit substitution rates addresses the concern without abandoning a question that took real design work to set up, and that dropping it this late risks looking like the committee waffled on scope. External reader asked a sharper question: even with the paratransit alternative, is there a risk both outcome variables are correlated with the same reporting bias, since paratransit enrollment itself requires navigating a bureaucratic process that not everyone manages? Nobody in the room had a fully clean answer to that. I proposed a compromise — keep question two but reframe it explicitly as exploratory rather than a load-bearing claim, with the audit and survey data carrying the main argument. Methods reader accepted this as workable if the exploratory framing is stated clearly in the methods chapter, not just implied. Advisor agreed, with the caveat that I show a draft of that framing language before the next full committee meeting rather than surprising anyone in the full draft.

## Survey instrument and fieldwork

Discussion moved to the survey instrument itself after I described the "traveling with an object" pattern Lotte noticed during the Linden Ave fieldwork — riders with carts, strollers, or bags mentioning difficulty far more than others, unprompted, across nearly every completed intercept that flagged any barrier at all. External reader thought this was a genuinely interesting finding and asked whether the instrument could add a structured field for it rather than relying on open comments, since open comments are harder to code consistently across a large team of one, and inter-rater drift on open-text coding was already a known risk from the stop-audit rubric. I agreed this was worth doing but flagged the tradeoff: adding a question this late means the westbound corridor data won't be directly comparable to the eastbound data already collected, since the instrument will have changed mid-study, and any pooled analysis would need to account for that. Advisor suggested treating it as a natural experiment instead of a flaw — analyze the eastbound wave on the original instrument and the westbound wave with the added field, and be transparent about the instrument change in the methods write-up rather than trying to hide it or awkwardly retrofit the missing variable. Methods reader wanted reassurance that the core six questions stay identical across both waves so the primary comparison isn't compromised, which I confirmed is the plan; only the optional seventh field changes. There was also a shorter discussion about compensation — the $5 gift cards nearly being mistaken for a Metro fare-discount card came up, and the committee agreed I should switch to something visually distinct before the next fieldwork window, with external reader suggesting a plain cash-equivalent voucher instead of anything branded. Refusal-rate tracking also came up; advisor asked me to standardize the refusal log across sessions so fatigue-related undercounting, which I'd flagged candidly in my fieldwork journal after the first three-hour session, doesn't quietly bias the completion numbers upward in later sessions relative to earlier ones. External reader added that a fixed timer-based check-in, rather than relying on memory, would make the refusal counts far more defensible during the eventual methods write-up.

## Decisions

Four decisions came out of the meeting. First, question two stays in the thesis but gets reframed as exploratory rather than load-bearing, with draft framing language due to advisor before the next committee meeting rather than appearing unannounced in a full draft. Second, the survey instrument gets one new structured field for "traveling with an object" starting with the westbound wave, with the instrument change documented explicitly in the methods chapter rather than smoothed over as if the two waves were always identical. Third, I'll switch fieldwork compensation to a clearly non-fare-card item — a plain cash-equivalent voucher rather than a branded gift card — before the next intercept session, per external reader's suggestion, since the resemblance to Metro's own discount program was creating confusion in the field. Fourth, I'm to standardize refusal-rate logging across all remaining sessions using a fixed timer-based check-in, so the second half of long sessions doesn't systematically undercount refusals the way Tuesday's window did, which advisor was clear should be fixed before it becomes a pattern across the remaining fieldwork sites. Nobody raised objections to the overall timeline; the milestones table in the project plan still looks achievable to the room as of this meeting, though advisor flagged that the defense date assumes no further instrument changes after the westbound wave, and that any additional revision would need to come back to the committee for sign-off rather than being made unilaterally in the field.
