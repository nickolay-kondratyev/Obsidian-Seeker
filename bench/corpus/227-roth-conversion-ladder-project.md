# Roth conversion ladder project

Planning a multi-year Roth conversion ladder so we can access retirement account money penalty-free well before 59½, once the paychecks stop and the traditional-account gap years arrive. This is the piece of the plan I keep putting off because the mechanics feel fuzzy.

## Why this matters

Most of our net worth sits in tax-advantaged accounts we can't touch without penalty until traditional retirement age. A conversion ladder moves money into Roth space gradually, and after a five-year seasoning period each converted amount becomes withdrawable, bridging the gap cleanly and legally, without the usual early-withdrawal penalty ever attached to it.

## Current numbers

As of January 2026 we hold roughly $340k in traditional accounts and $95k in Roth and taxable combined. Our target retirement date is 2033, meaning conversions need to start no later than 2028 for the first rung to season in time, assuming a five-year runway before the traditional accounts unlock naturally at standard retirement age. That gap-year window is exactly what the ladder exists to cover, and it's the piece of the plan I understand least well right now.

## Mechanics, roughly

Each year we'd convert a slice of the traditional balance into Roth, paying ordinary income tax on that slice at conversion time. Sizing conversions to stay inside a lower bracket is the whole trick, and getting it wrong is expensive.

## Open questions and risks

Unsure whether to start conversions before or after fully stopping work, since our bracket will look different depending on other income that year, and this interacts with [[217-emergency-fund-rebuild-plan]] timing. The bigger risk is a tax law change altering the five-year rule, which has been rumored before, or simply misjudging bracket thresholds and triggering a larger bill than planned.

## Reference: Priyam's ladder

Priyam started her own ladder three years ago and is on rung two of five. Her biggest lesson so far: state tax rules on conversions vary a lot between where she lived when she started and where she lives now, and she wishes she'd modeled both before committing to year one's amount. She also recommends keeping a full year of living expenses in cash before the first rung matures, in case timing slips, and says the paperwork was easier than she'd feared.
