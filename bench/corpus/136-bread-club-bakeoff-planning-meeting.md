Present: Dana, Lotte, Kofi, and two bread club regulars who don't usually attend the broader ferment meetings, Tomasz and a newer member still finding her footing with sourdough timing. Held at Lotte's place since her kitchen has the biggest table and the most natural light for the inevitable loaf photos everyone takes before cutting into anything. The purpose was narrowing down format, categories, and judging before sending the actual invite to the wider group next week, and everyone agreed to keep the meeting itself under an hour given how packed the evening already was.

## Recap of last year's bake-off

Last year's event ran with a single open category, which sounded simple but ended up unfair in practice, comparing an ambitious laminated brioche against a straightforward country loaf with no real basis for judging one against the other beyond gut feeling. Judging itself was informal, everyone just tasting and voting by show of hands, which worked fine for a small group of eight but felt chaotic once attendance crept toward fifteen, with people crowding around the same three loaves while others sat untouched near the back of the table. Tomasz, who won last year with a rye loaf nobody expected to place given the flashier entries, admitted afterward he wasn't sure his win meant much given how loosely the judging had actually worked, and said he'd rather lose fairly this year than win again under the same conditions. Lotte also noted several people arrived with loaves that had clearly been rushed the morning of, guessing at proof timing rather than planning backward from the actual event time, which showed in flatter, denser results than their usual baking. Kofi added that the informal hand-vote system meant louder, more social entrants effectively got more votes just from standing near their loaf and chatting people up, regardless of how the bread itself actually tasted, which nobody had really noticed in the moment but felt obvious in hindsight once he said it out loud, and everyone quietly agreed it had probably cost at least one quieter entrant a fair shot last year.

## This year's categories

Splitting into three categories this time: sourdough country loaves, enriched or laminated breads, and an open wildcard category for anything that doesn't fit the first two, covering things like flatbreads or rye-heavy loaves that don't cleanly belong in either bucket. Each category gets judged separately, with three simple criteria scored one to five: crumb structure, crust, and flavor, averaged across whoever's judging rather than a single show of hands. Dana suggested rotating judges category by category so nobody's judging their own category's competitors, and everyone agreed that felt fairer than last year's blanket everyone-votes-on-everything approach that let personal friendships quietly tip close calls. Tomasz raised whether laminated breads and simple enriched doughs like a milk bread really belong in the same category, since the skill ceiling for lamination is so much higher, but the group decided splitting further would leave categories too thin at this stage, especially in the first year of the new format, and agreed to revisit it next year once they have actual entry numbers to look at rather than guessing at expected turnout ahead of time. Kofi pointed out that folding laminated entries in with simpler enriched doughs might actually be fairer than it sounds, since judges can weigh technical difficulty as part of the flavor and crumb scoring rather than needing a separate rigid category just for that distinction, an argument that seemed to land well enough that nobody pushed back further on it. The newer member asked whether experience level should factor into judging somehow, and the group settled on simply noting first-time bakers on their entry card so judges have context without it directly affecting the score itself. Lotte also proposed a short written comment section on each score sheet, beyond the numeric scores, so entrants get some useful feedback afterward rather than just a number with no explanation attached, which felt like a small addition everyone could agree improved things without adding real complexity to judging day itself.

## Judging and logistics

Judging panel will be three people per category, rotating so everyone judges at least one category they're not personally entering, with score sheets Lotte's offered to design and print in advance rather than improvising on paper napkins like last year. Entries need to arrive by 11am sharp for a same-day tasting at 1pm, giving enough buffer for slicing, plating, and labeling each entry anonymously by number rather than name, which should also help keep judging more honest than last year's obviously-recognizable loaves that everyone could identify by shape alone. Kofi volunteered to handle the anonymous numbering system and will assign numbers the morning of rather than in advance, to avoid any chance of numbers leaking ahead of time through casual conversation. The newer member offered to bring extra folding tables since Lotte's kitchen table alone won't fit fifteen-plus loaf entries plus judging stations comfortably, and Dana is bringing a portable scale in case any judging criteria end up wanting an objective weight or crumb density measurement rather than pure taste and appearance. Score sheets will be collected and averaged by Lotte immediately after each category's tasting round, with results announced only once all three categories have finished judging, to avoid the early categories influencing how people taste and score the later ones. Tomasz is bringing his own labeled bread knives for the tasting table, one per category, so flavors don't bleed between an enriched brioche and a sharper rye loaf sitting side by side on the same cutting board.

## Open questions

Still unresolved: whether to allow gluten-free entries in a fourth category or fold them into the wildcard bucket, since only one person has asked so far but that could change once the wider invite goes out. Also undecided is whether to cap total entries per person at one per category or allow multiple, which matters more this year given the tighter three-category judging format compared to last year's single open free-for-all. Dana wants to poll the wider group before deciding either question, rather than the small planning circle deciding unilaterally on behalf of everyone who might show up. Lotte agreed to draft that poll and send it out alongside the main invite so both questions get resolved with real input before the event itself, rather than being settled informally at the last minute the way several smaller decisions were handled last year, which nobody wants to repeat given how rushed some of those calls felt at the time, and how much smoother this year should go if the group actually plans ahead for once.
