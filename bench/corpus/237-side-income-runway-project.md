Goal: build a small, boring, recurring side-income stream that covers roughly one category of our monthly budget on its own, as a hedge against relying entirely on portfolio growth for our eventual early retirement funding, and as practice for post-retirement flexible income.

## Why this matters

Sequence-of-returns risk worries me more than the average FIRE calculator admits. A side income that isn't correlated with the market gives us a lever to pull in a bad year instead of selling depressed assets at exactly the wrong time, which is the scenario that scares me most about the whole plan.

## Scope

One narrow offering: freelance technical editing, evenings and weekends only, capped hard at eight hours a week regardless of demand. Not a second career, not a business with employees or a website beyond a single contact page, just steady predictable hours that fit around the existing job without eating into family time or the weekend routines we already protect fiercely. Deliberately resisting the urge to expand scope into adjacent services like proofreading or formatting, since the whole point is a narrow, low-decision-fatigue offering rather than another sprawling project competing for attention.

## Target

$600 a month within six months of the first paying client, enough to cover our groceries line item entirely, decoupled from whatever the market happens to be doing that particular month or quarter of the year.

## Milestones

- [x] Set up invoicing and a simple contract template
- [x] Land first paying client (March)
- [ ] Reach $300/month recurring
- [ ] Reach $600/month recurring
- [ ] Decide whether to raise the weekly hour cap once the workload is better understood
- [ ] Review pricing against the two current clients' invoices to see if rates need adjusting

## Risks

Client work could crowd into family time or the hours we've set aside for [[212-debt-payoff-plan]] tracking and general budget admin, both of which already compete for the same limited weekend hours. Capping hours hard is meant to prevent that, but it's untested under real pressure — a demanding client or a rush deadline hasn't happened yet, and I don't fully trust myself not to bend the cap the first time one does, given how easily work expands to fill available time.

## Current status and related notes

Two recurring clients, averaging about $340 a month, ahead of the six-month target pace. Reassessing the hour cap next quarter once workload is clearer. Actual income against target gets folded into [[211-budget-review-sync]] each month for tracking.
