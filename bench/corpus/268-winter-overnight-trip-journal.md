Back from the winter overnight, and the short version is it went better than any of us expected going in, given how nervous all three of us were the week before about conditions none of us had real experience reading. Trailhead access needed chains after all, a fresh two inches overnight that the forecast hadn't fully called, so we lost forty minutes fitting them in the parking lot with cold hands before we'd even started hiking. On trail by nine, later than planned but not worryingly so given the short mileage. The valley route from [[261-route-planning-meeting]] turned out to be the right call; we passed the ridge trailhead on the way in and could see wind-loaded slopes above it that made the avalanche-terrain decision feel obviously correct in hindsight rather than just theoretically cautious. Snow was deeper than expected below the tree line, closer to a foot than the few inches the trip reports we'd read suggested, and we ended up needing the snowshoes within the first mile, glad we'd packed both options in the car rather than committing to microspikes alone.

## Getting to Camp

The two and a half miles to the sheltered stand of trees took nearly twice as long as our usual summer pace, snowshoes and cold hands slowing everything down more than any of us had budgeted for in the planning stage. Kofi's pack felt noticeably heavier than usual with the four-season tent split between us, and by the halfway point he was visibly working harder than either Dana or I, though he brushed it off each time either of us asked if he wanted to swap loads for a stretch. The satellite spot we'd picked from imagery turned out to match reality closely, genuinely sheltered from the wind that was picking up above the tree line, and we had the tent up in under twenty minutes despite none of us having practiced the four-season setup more than once beforehand in the backyard test. Melting snow for water took far longer than any of us anticipated, almost forty minutes for our first liter, which meant dinner ran later than planned and we ate mostly in the dark with headlamps once the temperature had already started dropping fast. Dana's dedicated dry sleep layer rule paid off immediately; she was the only one of us who felt genuinely warm changing into camp clothes, while Kofi and I both regretted not committing to the same discipline as strictly as we'd agreed to beforehand. Priyam had wanted to come but couldn't get the days off, and she texted twice before we lost signal asking for photos, which felt like a small reminder of how much this trip was already turning into a group tradition rather than a one-off experiment none of us expected to repeat.

## The Cold Hour

Coldest stretch was right after sunset, before anyone was in a sleeping bag, standing around melting more water and trying to eat dinner with numb fingers. Temperature dropped faster than the forecast suggested once the sun cleared the ridge, and conversation got noticeably shorter and more practical, just logistics, no small talk, until everyone was zipped into bags around seven. Kofi kept stamping his feet the whole time, more nervous habit than genuine cold by the end, and it became a running joke for the rest of the trip.

## Overnight and the Way Out

Sleep was rougher than any of our summer trips, waking every hour or two just cold enough to notice, though never anywhere close to alarming once we were actually inside the winter-rated bags. Kofi woke around three convinced he'd heard something moving near the tent, which turned out to be snow sliding off a branch overhead, a genuinely funny moment once the adrenaline wore off and we could all laugh about it over coffee instead of lying there tense in the dark. Morning came clear and startlingly bright, sun reflecting hard off fresh snow everywhere, and we were glad to have packed sunglasses even for a trip this short. Turnaround time of two in the afternoon from the plan never actually mattered since we were packed and moving by ten, faster than the hike in now that we knew the snowshoe rhythm and weren't stopping to figure anything out for the first time. The valley route back felt almost familiar already, strange how fast that happens even on unfamiliar winter terrain once you've done it once in daylight. Chains came off easily at the car, and the drive out felt genuinely triumphant in a way our summer trips rarely produce, probably because the margin for something going wrong had felt real the whole time rather than theoretical. Stopped for a hot lunch at the diner on the way back, and all three of us agreed, somewhat to our own surprise, that we'd do this again, harder terrain next time now that the basic systems are proven. Dana admitted on the drive that she'd nearly backed out the night before, convinced she'd be miserable the whole time, and instead spent most of the morning talking about wanting a proper winter planning session before next season. Kofi, despite the cold feet and the midnight scare, was already pulling up photos of the ridge route we'd deliberately avoided, half-joking about it being next year's real objective once we've built the experience this trip was meant to buy us. I mostly just felt relief, the kind that comes from something genuinely uncertain going right, and a quiet confidence that winter trips can become a real part of the rotation.

## Debrief Notes

Biggest surprise: how much longer everything takes in the cold, water especially, budget double the summer time for camp chores going forward. Snowshoes were the right call over microspikes for this depth, and next time we're bringing an insulated bottle instead of relying entirely on melted snow.
