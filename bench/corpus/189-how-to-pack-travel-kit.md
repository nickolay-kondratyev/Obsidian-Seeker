Packing list and order of operations for on-location shoots, mostly furniture and larger appliance clients who want their own showroom photographed rather than shipping product to the studio.

## Case Selection

I use a rolling hard case for the camera bodies and lenses, and a separate soft duffel for lighting and stands. Hard case goes as carry-on when flying, duffel checks, since a cracked stand is a much smaller loss than a missing camera body.

## Order Matters

Pack heaviest, fragile items first, at the bottom, tightly padded, so nothing shifts.

## Camera Bag Layer

Bodies go in first, wrapped in their own dividers, lenses next in individual pouches, then chargers and cards in the top mesh pocket where they're easy to find at security. I keep a folded checklist taped inside the lid, embarrassingly low-tech but it's never once failed me the way a phone app would during a dead battery moment at a rental counter.

## Lighting Layer

Strobes wrapped in the softbox fabric itself, doubles as padding, stands broken down to their shortest travel length and secured with the velcro straps built into most decent stands.

## Cables and Small Parts

Everything small goes in one labeled pouch: sync cables, extra batteries, gaffer tape, a multitool, spare memory cards. Losing a fifteen-minute chunk of a shoot hunting for a sync cable in a duffel is not a mistake worth repeating twice.

## Day Before

Charge everything, format cards, and lay out every item on the floor against the checklist.

## At the Location

First move on arrival is a full gear check against the packing list, laid out the same way as the night before, before touching a light stand or asking the client where to start. See [[194-how-to-white-background-setup]] for the studio-side version of the same instinct. It's saved a shoot twice, once with a cracked stand joint found before setup rather than during it, and once with a full memory card caught before it became a real problem on site.
