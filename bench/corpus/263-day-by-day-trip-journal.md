Day one of the Appalachian Trail section hike, and honestly a rougher start than expected given how much training went into this cycle. Shuttle picked us up right on time at seven, and we were on trail by eight, later than planned but not disastrously so. The first six miles climbed steadily out of Damascus, humid even in the early morning, and by the second hour all three of us were sweating through layers we'd expected to need for warmth rather than shedding this early. Stopped for lunch at a shelter about halfway, and Kofi's feet were already complaining, a bad sign for someone with brand new boots that only had fifty miles on them before the trip started. Made camp at the planned shelter with daylight to spare, cooked dinner as a light rain started, and everyone agreed the humidity was the real surprise of the day, worse than any of our training hikes back home prepared us for. Fourteen miles logged, right on plan despite the rough start, and spirits are decent heading into tomorrow even with Kofi's feet as an open question.

## Day Two: The Balds

Day two brought the trip's first real highlight: a long stretch along open balds with views stretching for miles in every direction, exactly the terrain we picked this section for in the first place. Weather cleared overnight and the morning was crisp and clear, a welcome change from yesterday's humidity, and we made good time on the relatively gentle grade through the meadows. Kofi's feet held up better than feared, a blister forming but manageable with tape, and he insisted on pushing the full sixteen planned miles rather than cutting the day short as Dana suggested at lunch. Passed several thru-hikers heading the opposite direction, all with that particular thousand-mile stare and impressively minimal gear compared to our own three-day loads, a good reminder of how much lighter this trip could theoretically be with more experience. Camp tonight is at a shelter already half full with other hikers, a new experience for us after years of mostly having camps to ourselves on our usual Pacific Northwest trips. Conversation around the shelter tonight was easy and welcoming, trail names being tossed around that none of us have earned yet, and it's a different, more social kind of trip than what we're used to. One older hiker, going by trail name Compass, gave us a rundown of the next fifty miles from memory, water sources and campsite quality included, the kind of casual generosity that seems to be a real feature of thru-hiker culture rather than an exception.

## Day Three: An Easier Day

Day three was shorter by design, twelve miles, giving Kofi's blister a chance to settle before the two bigger days ahead. Rain came and went most of the afternoon, nothing severe, just enough to keep rain jackets on and make the descents a bit more careful than usual on wet roots and rock. Reached camp early, around three, and spent the extra daylight actually relaxing instead of the usual rush to set up and cook before dark.

## Days Four and Five: The Home Stretch

Days four and five were a blur of steady miles, fifteen and thirteen respectively, through terrain that shifted from forest to bald and back again more times than I bothered counting by the end. Kofi's blister held with regular tape changes, never quite becoming the trip-ending problem it threatened to be on day one, mostly thanks to catching it early and not being stubborn about addressing it properly. Weather stayed mostly cooperative for the back half, one more rain shower on day four but nothing like the humidity of day one, which everyone agreed was the hardest single stretch of the whole trip in hindsight. Finished at Roan Mountain on schedule, seventy miles logged across five days, tired in the specific satisfying way that only a trip like this produces, and already talking on the shuttle back about which section to do next. See [[247-appalachian-trail-section-project]] for the original plan this trip followed closely, and [[258-training-log-summer]] for the training cycle that got us here in reasonable shape despite that rough first day. Priyam is already talking about a longer AT section next year, maybe a full week instead of five days, and Dana seemed genuinely tempted despite swearing on day one that this would be a one-time experiment for her. Kofi, blister and all, is the most enthusiastic of the three of us about coming back, which given how rough his first day looked feels like a fairly strong endorsement of the whole trip. The shuttle ride back to the cars was quiet in the good way, everyone processing the trip in their own head, occasionally interrupted by someone remembering a specific moment worth saying out loud before it faded. Comparing this to our usual Pacific Northwest trips, the social atmosphere of the AT was the biggest single difference, and honestly a welcome one, a reminder that trail culture varies a lot by region and it's worth experiencing more of it.
