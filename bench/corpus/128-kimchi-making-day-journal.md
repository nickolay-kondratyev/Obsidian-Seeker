Kimchi day, finally, after putting it off for two weekends straight because the napa cabbage at the good stall kept selling out before I got there Saturday morning. Bought three heads this time, bigger than I needed, mostly because Kofi's ratio notes from the last ferment club meeting made me want enough to actually share rather than one small jar hoarded for myself. Following the ratios from [[125-wild-ferments-reading-notes]] roughly, 2% salt by cabbage weight for the initial brine, though I rounded up slightly to 2.2% since my kitchen runs warm this time of year and I'd rather it lean safe than mushy by day five. Quartered the cabbage, salted between the leaves rather than just tossing it in brine, and let it sit under a weighted plate for about two hours while I prepped everything else. The house smelled like cabbage and garlic by midafternoon in a way that isn't unpleasant exactly, but is definitely noticeable the moment you walk in the front door, and I already know it'll linger into tomorrow.

## The paste

Made the paste heavier on ginger than most recipes suggest, since a batch I had at a friend's place last year leaned that direction and I've been chasing the flavor since. Rice flour slurry as the base, cooked down until it went glossy and slightly translucent, then blended with gochugaru, garlic, ginger, a fish sauce and salted shrimp mix, and a spoonful of sugar to balance the heat. The gochugaru I'm using is a coarser grind than what I used last time, which changed the texture of the paste noticeably, thicker and grittier rather than smooth, and I'm curious whether that affects how evenly it coats the cabbage once mixed. Added daikon matchsticks and scallion at the mixing stage rather than earlier, wanting them to stay a bit crisp rather than fully wilting into the paste before packing. Tasted the paste straight, which was too salty and too sharp on its own, but that's normal since it's meant to mellow once it's coating cabbage rather than sitting concentrated on a spoon. Wore gloves this time after forgetting last year and dealing with gochugaru-stained fingers for two days afterward, a mistake I have no intention of repeating a third time. Priyam texted partway through asking whether I'd tried her suggestion of a small spoonful of fermented rice porridge in the paste for extra depth, and I hadn't, having already committed to the rice flour slurry before reading her message, so that experiment gets pushed to the next batch instead of this one. The kitchen counter was a genuine mess by this point, gochugaru dust everywhere despite my best efforts to keep it contained to one cutting board.

## Packing

Massaged the paste into every leaf by hand, packed it tightly into three wide-mouth jars leaving about an inch of headspace, and pressed each layer down to force out air pockets before sealing loosely with the lids barely tightened. One jar smaller than the other two since I ran slightly short on cabbage relative to how much paste I'd made, so it's noticeably more paste-forward than the others, which might turn out to be the best jar or the worst one, hard to say yet.

## Waiting

Jars are sitting on a tray on the counter, out of direct sun, with the loose lids to let gas escape without letting fruit flies in, though I've also got a thin cloth over the tray just in case. Plan is to check daily starting tomorrow, pressing down any cabbage that's floated above the brine line and tasting a small bit off the top once bubbling starts, probably day three or four given how warm the kitchen's been running lately. Kofi's rule of thumb from the last meeting was five to seven days at room temperature before moving to the fridge, but he also admits his kitchen runs cooler than mine, so I'm treating that as a starting guess rather than a fixed number. Going to log taste notes each day this time instead of just eyeballing it and moving jars to the fridge whenever it felt roughly right, which was my entire method for the last two batches and led to at least one jar that fermented well past where I actually liked it. The smaller paste-heavy jar is going in the fridge test first regardless of the others, purely out of curiosity about whether more paste means faster fermentation or just a spicier plateau at the same pace as the rest. I also set a reminder on my phone for each check-in, since past experience says good intentions about daily tasting evaporate by the third day without an actual nudge, and I don't want a repeat of the batch that sat forgotten until it turned properly sour past anyone's preference in this house. Kofi's also promised to swing by around day four to taste and compare against his own batch from last weekend, which should be a useful cross-check since our kitchens run at noticeably different temperatures and it'll be interesting to see how far apart our timelines actually land once we compare notes side by side rather than just trading numbers over text like we usually do after the fact.

## Cleanup

Kitchen took nearly as long to clean as the kimchi took to make, gochugaru staining the cutting board despite the gloves, and cabbage cores filling most of the compost bin. Wiped down every surface twice and still found a smear of paste on the light switch an hour later. Worth it, probably, once the jars actually deliver something worth the mess.
