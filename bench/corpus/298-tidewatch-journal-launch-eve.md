# Launch Eve

March 9, 2026, past midnight, so this is really launch morning, but it doesn't feel like it since the campaign page still shows a countdown timer rather than a live pledge count. I keep refreshing it anyway, which is pointless at this hour, but I can't quite close the laptop and sleep like a reasonable person would. Every reward tier is locked, every FAQ answer proofed, and the video Lotte cut with the playtest kit footage is ready the second the page flips over. There is nothing left to do except wait, and waiting is the hardest part of this whole process, harder than any of the actual design work that got us here.

I texted the group chat an hour ago just to say something, since none of us seem to be sleeping tonight. Kofi sent back one message: "rules are locked, FAQ is locked, and now I am going to bed like a person who has priorities." I admire that discipline more than I can access myself. Priyam sent a screenshot of the page render zoomed in on the storm illustration, no comment attached, just the image.

## What Got Us Here

It's strange to sit here at midnight and think back to how this actually started, which was Kofi and me arguing over a napkin sketch about whether a cooperative storm-track game could work at all without feeling either too easy or too punishing to actually enjoy playing more than once. That was almost two years ago now. Between then and tonight there have been dozens of playtest sessions, two full rules rewrites, one component set that got scrapped entirely because the tide tokens felt wrong in your hand no matter how we sized them, and one very memorable near-loss session, the one I wrote up in [[283-tidewatch-journal-near-loss]], that Kofi still brings up whenever anyone needs reminding why the storm deck variance matters and why we resisted flattening it even when early testers complained. Priyam joined maybe eight months in, once the core loop was stable enough to actually illustrate without everything changing under her every other week, and the art transformed how the whole project felt almost immediately, turning it from a stack of index cards into something that actually looked like a real game sitting on a table. Lotte joined last, closer to a year ago, and has spent the months since turning "a game we like" into something with an actual plan for reaching people who aren't already our friends, which is a completely different skill set than anything the rest of us brought to this, and one I don't think this project would have survived without.

## Nerves

I'm nervous in a way that's hard to fully articulate, somewhere between excited and slightly nauseated, and I suspect that's just what launching something you've spent two years on actually feels like rather than a sign anything is specifically wrong. Funding is not guaranteed, not even close, no matter how good the playtest feedback has been. Plenty of good games with good art fail to fund for reasons that have nothing to do with quality, and no amount of preparation fully removes that risk from tomorrow.

## Trying to Sleep

I should close the laptop. The campaign goes live at nine regardless of whether I've slept, and I'll be more useful to everyone tomorrow, answering comments and troubleshooting the inevitable small thing that goes wrong, if I've had at least a few hours of actual rest rather than running purely on nervous energy and coffee. Tomasz already sent a message saying he's setting an alarm specifically to watch the first hour of pledges, which is either sweet or slightly unhinged depending on how you look at it, and honestly probably both at once given how much of himself he's put into the manufacturing side of this over the last year. Dana hasn't said much tonight, which is either a sign she's actually managed to fall asleep at a reasonable hour, unlike the rest of us apparently, or that she's staring at the same countdown timer I am and just isn't bothering to say so out loud in the group chat right now.

I keep thinking about the very first playtest session we ever ran, in someone's cramped apartment with a prototype board held together with tape and a rules sheet that was three pages of my own handwriting nobody else could fully read. None of us imagined it would end up here, on the eve of an actual public launch with real reward tiers and real manufacturing quotes and a real print vendor lined up to run a few hundred physical copies. It's easy to lose sight of that distance when you're deep in spreadsheets about courier capacity and token counts, but tonight, for whatever reason, the distance is all I can think about, probably because there's nothing left to actually do except sit with it.

One more refresh of the page. Then the laptop closes, whether I actually feel ready for that or not, because tomorrow starts either way and there's nothing left tonight that more scrolling is going to fix or improve in any meaningful sense, no matter how many times I check.

## Morning

If tomorrow goes badly, we'll have learned something real and expensive about pricing or reach, and we'll regroup and figure out what comes next. If it goes well, none of us will quite believe it until the number on the page actually says so in writing, in front of all of us, at once.
