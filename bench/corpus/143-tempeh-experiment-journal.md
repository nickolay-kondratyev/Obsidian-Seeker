Third tempeh attempt, third failure, though this one failed differently enough that I think I'm actually learning something rather than repeating the same mistake. The first attempt never developed any visible mycelium at all, just sat there smelling faintly of beans and doing nothing for two full days past when it should have shown white fuzz forming between the beans. The second attempt did develop mycelium, patchy and uneven, thick in some spots and completely absent in others, which I now think was an incubation temperature problem since my makeshift setup, a heating pad under a towel-wrapped container, clearly wasn't holding steady heat evenly. This third attempt used the actual proofing box from [[124-proofing-box-temp-control-howto]] instead of the heating pad improvisation, which should have solved the temperature problem entirely given how reliably it's held sourdough proofing temperatures for months. And the temperature did hold steady, verified against an independent thermometer twice during incubation, so that variable is genuinely ruled out this time. What I got instead was mycelium that started forming properly around hour eighteen with visible white webbing, then stalled out around hour thirty rather than continuing to bind the beans into a firm cake. By hour forty-eight, when I finally gave up and opened the bag, the beans were loosely held together at best, falling apart at the slightest touch. Smell was still fine, mild and slightly mushroomy, so contamination doesn't seem to be the issue this time either.

## Troubleshooting

Spent the evening actually researching rather than just trying again blindly, since three failures in a row means the guessing approach clearly isn't working and I owe myself some real troubleshooting before wasting a fourth batch of soybeans on the same mistake. Turns out tempeh needs oxygen exchange during incubation, small perforations in the bag or container to let the mold breathe, which I'd been doing, using a fork to poke holes at roughly one-inch spacing like every guide suggests, so that's probably not it either, though I did notice on closer inspection that several of my holes had closed up slightly where the plastic bag material had stretched and partially sealed itself back over during handling. The more likely culprit, based on what I read tonight, is that my beans might still have been slightly too wet after cooking and cooling, since excess surface moisture is apparently a common cause of exactly this stalled, patchy mycelium pattern I've now seen twice in a row across two different attempts. I dry my beans on a towel for maybe ten minutes after cooking, which multiple sources now suggest is not nearly long enough; the beans need to be properly dry to the touch, not just drained, before the starter culture goes anywhere near them, and looking back at photos from tonight's batch I can see beads of moisture still visible on individual beans right before I added the starter, which in hindsight is a pretty obvious tell I completely missed at the time. There's also a real chance my starter culture itself has lost potency sitting in the freezer longer than the package really recommends, since I bought it back in the fall and freezer shelf life claims on these things always feel a little optimistic compared to how they actually perform in practice once you're several months past the printed date. Ordering a fresh batch of starter culture this week regardless, since a variable I can't verify is a variable I shouldn't be troubleshooting around blindly with expensive soybeans I could otherwise be using for something with a real chance of actually working out this time.

## The next attempt

Fourth attempt is planned for next weekend: fresh starter culture, beans dried properly for a full thirty minutes on a clean towel with a fan running to speed evaporation rather than passively air-drying, and the proofing box again since that variable is genuinely proven reliable at this point across two full attempts of consistent, verified temperature holding. I'm also going to switch to a fresh box of ziplock bags rather than reusing the slightly stretched one from this attempt, just to rule out that the perforations closing up were part of the problem too, however unlikely that seems on its own. If this one fails too, despite controlling for every variable I can currently think to control for, I'm going to seriously consider that something about my kitchen environment itself, maybe airborne competing mold spores from the sourdough and kimchi projects running in the same general space, is contaminating or outcompeting the tempeh culture in ways I haven't been able to identify or rule out yet through normal troubleshooting alone.

## Why I'm logging this

Logging all of this here mostly so future me, whether troubleshooting attempt five or finally succeeding on attempt four, has an actual record instead of vague memory to work from, the exact failure mode I keep falling into with every other fermentation project until I started writing things down properly instead of trusting memory to hold details across weeks between attempts, which it clearly never actually does no matter how confident I feel about remembering the specifics in the moment right after something goes wrong. Sourdough logging saved me from this same trap months ago, and it's frankly overdue that tempeh got the same treatment from the very first attempt rather than only after three failures forced the issue.
