Route safety review meeting, called after two separate near-misses at the Route 9 junction in the space of a single month, one involving a club member and one involving a rider from another local club who happened to be following our published GPX file that week. This isn't a regular committee meeting; it was scheduled specifically to look at junction safety across all our published routes rather than the usual mix of events and volunteering business. Present: Priyam, Dana, Tomasz, and Kofi, plus two members who witnessed the near-misses and wanted to describe what happened directly rather than secondhand through the group chat. Met at the clubhouse on a Wednesday evening, ran just over an hour.

## What Happened

Both incidents involved the same junction where Route 9 crosses the old mill lane, a junction with poor sightlines due to a hedge on the northeast corner that's grown considerably over the past two years without anyone flagging it. In the first incident, a club member pulled out slightly too early behind a group and had to brake hard when a car appeared from the mill lane faster than expected; no contact, but a genuinely frightening moment by her own account. The second incident, a week later, involved a visiting rider from another club who misjudged the same sightline and was clipped lightly by a delivery van's wing mirror, resulting in a bruise and a badly shaken rider but thankfully nothing worse. Both riders were following routes that included this junction without any specific warning in the cue sheet or GPX annotation, which the group agreed was the core failure here rather than any fault on the riders' part. Kofi pointed out that this junction has probably been a slow-building risk for some time, with the hedge growth being gradual enough that nobody riding it regularly really noticed how much visibility had degraded since the routes were originally scouted, back before most of the current committee was even involved with the club. One of the witnesses, describing the second incident, said the van driver was clearly going the speed limit and doing nothing wrong; the hedge simply meant neither party saw the other until they were already committed to the junction, which is exactly the kind of hazard that written warnings and slower approach speeds can meaningfully address even before any physical fix to the hedge happens.

## Discussion

The conversation quickly moved beyond this single junction into a broader question: how many of our published routes have similar unflagged hazards that nobody has revisited since the original scouting ride, possibly years ago. Dana raised the uncomfortable point that several of the club's oldest routes, including some used for the weekly Saturday ride, haven't been formally re-scouted since before she joined the club, and that road conditions, hedge growth, and traffic patterns can all change significantly over that kind of timespan without anyone noticing incrementally. Tomasz suggested this connects directly to the new volunteer coordinator role and the ride leader mentorship programme discussed at [[096-annual-volunteer-coordination-meeting]], since ride leaders are well placed to flag developing hazards if there's a clear, low-friction way for them to report it rather than just mentioning it once in the group chat and having it forgotten within a day. Priyam pushed for something more immediate: contacting the local council about the hedge specifically, since it's on public land near the junction and cutting it back is a simple physical fix that doesn't require any route changes at all. There was some debate about whether the club should also avoid the mill lane junction entirely in cue sheets until the hedge issue is resolved, rerouting onto a slightly longer but safer alternative, or whether flagging it clearly with a written warning is sufficient given how established a route this is for regular members who already know the road well. No consensus was reached on that specific question, and it was left for a follow-up decision once the council response on the hedge is known, expected within a few weeks given typical response times for this kind of request. Kofi, who tends to be the most cautious voice on the committee, argued that a temporary reroute would send a clearer message to members about how seriously the club takes this, even if it turns out to be unnecessary once the hedge is cut back; Dana countered that constantly changing published routes creates its own confusion, especially for members who've saved the old GPX files locally and might not notice an update.

## Decisions

Despite the unresolved rerouting question, several concrete decisions came out of the meeting. First, Priyam will contact the council this week about the hedge at the mill lane junction, citing both near-misses as evidence of an active safety concern rather than a vague complaint about overgrowth. Second, every published club route will get a documented annual safety review, assigned to whichever ride leader rides it most often, checking for new hazards, changed traffic patterns, or road surface issues since the last review, with results logged rather than just mentioned verbally at a meeting and forgotten. Third, until the hedge is cut back, every cue sheet and GPX file referencing the mill lane junction will carry an explicit written warning about limited sightlines, added by Dana this week across all affected routes. Fourth, the incident reporting process itself needs fixing: right now there's no single place to log a near-miss, and both witnesses tonight only came forward because they happened to mention it to Priyam directly rather than through any formal channel, which means other near-misses have almost certainly gone unreported and undiscussed. Tomasz will set up a simple form linked from the group chat pinned message for exactly this purpose. Priyam noted that this shouldn't be a heavy process, just a short form capturing where, when, and what happened, since anything more elaborate would likely just discourage people from bothering to fill it in after a stressful moment on the road, when the last thing anyone wants is a lengthy form to complete.

- [ ] Tomasz: set up near-miss reporting form and pin it in the group chat

Follow-up meeting scheduled once the council responds, to settle the rerouting question and review any near-misses logged through the new reporting form in the meantime. Both witnesses were thanked for speaking to the group directly rather than letting it pass unmentioned, since tonight's meeting only happened because they chose to raise it rather than shrug it off as bad luck. Dana closed by reminding everyone that near-misses are the cheap warnings that precede a genuinely serious incident, and the club would rather spend an evening on cue sheets and hedges than wait for something worse to force the same conversation later.
