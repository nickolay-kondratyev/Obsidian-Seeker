How to lay the kitchen's engineered oak flooring properly, written up right after finishing, before I forget the details that mattered for future reference.

## Tools Needed

Miter saw, flooring nailer or click-lock tapping block depending on system, spacers, a moisture meter, a long straightedge for checking subfloor flatness, a rubber mallet for gently seating stubborn boards, and a pull bar for the final row against the wall.

## Step 1: Check the Subfloor

Test moisture content first, and confirm flatness within manufacturer tolerance everywhere across the room.

## Step 2: Acclimate and Lay the First Rows

Boxes sat in the kitchen, unopened, for a full week before installation, letting the wood adjust to the room's actual humidity rather than whatever conditions it shipped in, since engineered oak still moves somewhat with seasonal humidity even though far less than solid hardwood does. Once acclimated, snap a chalk line parallel to the longest wall and start the first row from there.

## Step 3: Continue the Field

Stagger end joints by at least six inches between rows, checking every few rows with the straightedge so nothing drifts, since small errors compound over a room this size.

## Step 4: Cuts and Transitions

Cabinet toe kicks and the doorway threshold both needed careful scribing around obstacles. A simple flush reducer strip handles the height difference into the dining room's existing flooring, chosen because it reads as nearly invisible rather than drawing attention.

## Step 5: Final Inspection

Walk every row checking for squeaks, gaps, or lippage between boards before calling it done.

## Lessons Learned

The acclimation week felt like wasted time in the moment, one more delay stacked on an already long list, but it's the single step I'd least want to skip on a repeat job, since a floor installed too early can develop gaps or buckling months later once the wood finally settles into the room's real conditions, a problem far more expensive to fix after the fact than the boredom of waiting one extra week for boxes to sit quietly in a corner.
