Midsummer check-in on training for the Appalachian Trail section hike, three weeks out now and feeling genuinely ready for the first time this cycle. This block has gone better than the spring one before the Wonderland Trail, mostly because I started earlier and built up pack weight more gradually instead of rushing the last few weeks. Today's session was the ridge trail loop with full trip weight, twenty-two pounds, and it felt manageable in a way that would have wrecked me back in March. Legs are stronger, obviously, but the bigger change is mental; I'm not dreading the loaded hikes anymore, which makes it much easier to actually get out the door on a tired weekday evening. Weather's been brutal for training lately, several days over ninety degrees, so I've shifted most sessions to early morning before the heat really sets in, which has the side benefit of a much nicer light on the ridge anyway. Hydration strategy has needed adjusting too; I'm carrying almost twice the water I did in spring for the same loop, and still finishing more depleted than I'd like on the hottest afternoons.

## Comparing to the Spring Cycle

Comparing this cycle to the spring one is genuinely satisfying: same ridge loop, same rough distance, and my time is down by almost twelve minutes despite carrying slightly more weight this time around. Heart rate during the climb averaged one hundred forty-one today versus one hundred fifty-two back in March, a real improvement in aerobic efficiency that I'll credit mostly to the extra easy-paced volume I added this cycle instead of just loading up every session. Kofi's stair repeat routine has apparently paid off for him too; he beat his own previous best on the same loop by nearly ten minutes last week, which is a genuinely wild improvement for a routine that only takes twenty minutes at lunch. Priyam has been less consistent this cycle, work has been brutal for her the past month, but she's confident a few solid weeks before the trip will be enough given how much base fitness she's built over the past couple years of regular hiking. My weak point is still descents; even with much better climbing fitness, my quads are noticeably more fatigued on the way down than they should be, so I've added some single-leg strength work twice a week to address it directly instead of hoping it resolves on its own. Sleep and recovery have been solid this cycle too, which I think matters more than people give it credit for; a rested body adapts to training load far better than a chronically tired one, no matter how disciplined the actual workouts are.

## Nutrition

Nutrition during long sessions has improved too; I'm actually eating on the ridge loop now instead of just pushing through hungry, which noticeably helps the second half of the climb. Small thing, but it took most of this training cycle to actually build the habit of eating before I feel hungry rather than after. Settled on a snack mix after testing a few combos: dried fruit, nuts, and dark chocolate that survives heat without melting into a mess.

## Remaining Plan

Plan for the remaining three weeks: taper starting ten days out, one more loaded long session this weekend, then progressively shorter and easier hikes leading into the trip itself, prioritizing rest over one last push for fitness. It's tempting to squeeze in one more big session the week before departure, but every trip I've done has taught me that arriving rested beats arriving marginally fitter but fatigued, and I don't want to relearn that lesson the hard way again this time. Boots are fully broken in now, over eighty miles on them with zero hot spots on the last three sessions, so that variable is settled well ahead of departure for once instead of being a last-minute scramble. Mentally I'm in a good place about this trip too, excited rather than anxious, which given how new the section is to all three of us feels like a genuinely good sign going in. Going to keep logging through the taper just to have the full record, even though the numbers matter less now than the actual rest does at this point in the cycle. I'm also going to spend some of the taper time on logistics instead of fitness: double-checking the shuttle confirmation with Kofi, re-reading the section of the guidebook covering water sources, and making sure the satellite messenger rental is confirmed for the right dates. One more thing on the list: a proper gear shakedown with Dana and Priyam the weekend before we leave, similar to what we did before the Wonderland Trail, since that meeting caught several problems we wouldn't have noticed otherwise until it was too late to fix them cheaply. Tapering is always psychologically harder than it sounds, watching hard-earned fitness feel like it's slipping away even though the research is clear that rest in these final weeks matters more than one last hard session ever could. I'm trying to trust the process this time instead of second-guessing it the way I did before the JMT trip last year, when I nearly talked myself into a big session four days out that would have done nothing but leave me tired at the trailhead.
