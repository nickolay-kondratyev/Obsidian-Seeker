I haven't written in this journal for almost six weeks, which is itself a data point worth noting, since I usually write something at least weekly and a six-week gap during what turned out to be the hardest stretch of the year isn't a coincidence. Late November through the end of December was three overlapping catalog jobs, the retainer client's first month running, and a rush request from Kofi's team all landing in the same six-week window, and I said yes to all of it without really pausing to check whether that was sustainable, mostly because saying yes has worked out fine in every previous instance and I didn't have a good reason ready to say no. By the second week of December I was working most evenings and both weekend days, editing until past midnight more nights than not, and telling myself it was temporary, which it was, technically, but six weeks stopped feeling temporary somewhere around week four and started feeling like a preview of what the business could quietly become if I let it, without ever consciously deciding that's what I wanted.

## What It Actually Felt Like

The physical stuff came first and I mostly ignored it, a stiff neck that didn't go away, eyes that felt gritty by early afternoon most days, a headache that became low-grade background noise rather than a distinct event worth stopping for. What actually scared me wasn't the exhaustion though, it was noticing I'd started resenting the work itself, snapping internally at a client's third round of minor revision requests in a way that felt completely unlike me, when normally I genuinely don't mind revisions and see them as part of getting the client what they actually need rather than an imposition on my time. I caught myself, during an otherwise perfectly reasonable jewelry shoot in mid-December, thinking that I wished I'd never picked up a camera professionally at all, which is such a wildly disproportionate thought relative to a completely normal, well-paying booking that it genuinely scared me more than the physical exhaustion did, because it meant something deeper than tiredness was actually going on underneath the surface. I didn't say anything to the client, obviously, and the shoot went fine, but I sat in the car afterward for a solid ten minutes before I felt okay to drive, which has never happened to me before in two years of doing this and hasn't happened since, but it was a real warning sign I can't unremember now that I've actually named it here in writing.

## The Turning Point

Dana noticed before I did, or at least said something before I was willing to admit it to myself. She asked directly, over a call about the studio relocation logistics of all things, whether I was actually okay, and something about hearing it asked plainly by someone who wasn't a client made it impossible to keep deflecting the way I'd been doing with everyone else, including myself. I ended up telling her most of it, the car, the resentment, the headaches, more than I'd admitted to myself up to that point.

## What Changed After

I took the first week of January mostly off, only handling the two bookings I genuinely couldn't move without real financial cost, and told every other inquiry that new bookings would start the second week of the month, full stop, no exceptions made for anyone regardless of how the request was framed or how urgent it sounded on paper. It felt genuinely uncomfortable to say no that plainly, uncomfortable in a way that surprised me given how much practice I supposedly have at boundaries after two years running this business, but nothing bad happened as a result, no client got angry, nobody took their business elsewhere over a one-week delay, which in hindsight should have been obvious but genuinely wasn't in the moment I was dreading sending those messages. I also went back and looked hard at the six-week stretch to understand what actually made it unsustainable, and it wasn't really the volume of work itself, it was saying yes to everything simultaneously without any buffer between commitments, no room at all for something to run even slightly long without cascading directly into the next thing on the calendar. Going forward I'm building in at least one buffer day between any two booked jobs that require full-day studio time, even if that means turning down a booking that would otherwise fit into a tighter calendar, since the buffer isn't really slack time to sit around unused, it's actually working time, editing catches up there, gear gets maintained there, invoices get sent there, all the invisible work that used to just quietly get pushed later and later until it piled up into exactly the kind of six-week stretch that landed me sitting in a car unable to drive after a shoot that should have been completely unremarkable. I also told Priyam directly that I want to be pushed back on if I start saying yes to everything again the way I did in November, since I clearly can't always see it happening from the inside, and having someone else with permission to name it out loud feels like a real safeguard rather than another resolution I'll quietly abandon once things get busy again.

## Where I'm At Now

Six weeks out from the worst of it and feeling mostly like myself again, though I'm watching for the early physical signs more closely now, the neck, the gritty eyes, treating them as an actual signal instead of background noise to push through the way I used to without a second thought.
