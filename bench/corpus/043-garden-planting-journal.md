Put the first seedlings into the new vegetable beds today, tomatoes and peppers mostly, the ones I'd been hardening off on the back porch for the last two weeks against a run of unpredictable spring nights that kept threatening frost right up until this week finally broke warm and settled. The cedar beds from the [[037-garden-redesign-plan]] have only been in the ground a few weeks themselves, filled with a mix of topsoil and compost that Kofi's crew brought in during a lull between kitchen deliveries, since apparently a landscaping supplier and a kitchen cabinet supplier sometimes share a truck route and it seemed silly not to combine the orders. The soil still smells faintly of the compost, rich and a little sharp, nothing like the tired clay that used to sit under the old boxwoods before any of this started. I planted six tomato varieties, more than I probably need for two people, but Priyam's parents visit most summers and there's nothing quite like handing someone a bag of home-grown tomatoes on their way out the door at the end of a long weekend. Peppers went in along the sunnier south edge, closer to where the gravel border will eventually sit once the workshop's fully closed in and that phase of the garden plan can actually start without construction traffic crushing anything young and unestablished in its path. I marked each variety with a small painted stake, since I've learned the hard way in past gardens that memory alone never survives long enough to tell one tomato seedling from another by midsummer.

## What Went In Today

It rained lightly around noon, just enough to settle the new soil around the roots without compacting it the way a hard downpour would have, which felt like good timing rather than anything I could take credit for planning. I stood under the porch overhang for a few minutes watching it fall on the beds, thinking about how different this whole corner of the yard looks from even a month ago, when it was still just churned-up dirt and a half-built cedar frame waiting on hardware. There's something almost embarrassingly sentimental about watching rain fall on vegetables you planted yourself a few hours earlier, but I'm not going to pretend I didn't stand there longer than necessary, coffee going cold, feeling more settled about this project than I have in weeks of permits and budget reviews and calls with surveyors I've never met.

Six tomato varieties along the back row, three pepper varieties along the sunnier south edge, and a first round of bush beans in the middle bed, direct-sown rather than transplanted since beans generally resent having their roots disturbed and do better started right where they'll stay for the season. I also tucked a few marigolds along the bed edges, mostly for the pest deterrent reputation they have among people who garden more seriously than I do, though I'll admit half the reason is just that I like how they look against the cedar, a small bright border that makes the whole bed read as intentional rather than merely functional. Watering is still by hand for now, a slow evening ritual with the hose that I don't actually mind, though [[039-garden-irrigation-howto]] describes the drip system I'm planning to install properly once the beds have settled in for a few more weeks and I'm confident the layout won't need adjusting again before I commit to fixed lines and emitters. The herb strip outside the kitchen window is still just brick edging and bare soil, waiting on the actual herb starts that Lotte promised to bring over from her own overgrown patch sometime this weekend, cuttings and divisions rather than anything from a nursery, which feels appropriately in the spirit of the whole project so far.

## Worries

Late frost is still technically possible for another week or so according to the extension office's average last-frost date for this area, and I have a stack of row cover fabric ready by the back door just in case the forecast turns, though tonight's forecast looks clear and mild enough that I'm not pulling it out preemptively. The bigger worry, if I'm honest, is squirrels, who have taken a real interest in the freshly turned soil already, digging one exploratory hole near the pepper row this afternoon that I filled back in with more hope than confidence it'll actually deter them from trying again tomorrow. Priyam suggested chicken wire pinned flat over the whole bed until things establish, which feels like overkill for six tomato plants but might genuinely be worth it given how persistent squirrels around here have been in past years with anything freshly planted. Lotte mentioned once that a light dusting of cayenne pepper around the base of new transplants helped her own beds a couple of summers ago, though I'm skeptical it survives the first watering without needing to be reapplied constantly.

## Tomorrow

Water everything by hand again in the evening, check the exploratory squirrel hole, and call Lotte about the herb cuttings before she forgets and the offer quietly evaporates like these things sometimes do. I also want to pick up a roll of chicken wire on the way home tomorrow, just in case Priyam's right about the squirrels, and it's cheap enough that trying it costs almost nothing compared to losing a whole row of transplants two weeks in. Maybe I'll try Lotte's cayenne trick too, on just one corner, as an unscientific little experiment before committing the whole bed to whichever method actually seems to work by the end of the week.
