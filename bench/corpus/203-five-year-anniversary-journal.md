Five years today since I shot the first paid job, a set of ceramic mugs for a friend of a friend's small pottery side business, $150 for the whole thing, which felt like an enormous sum of money at the time and is genuinely funny to think about now given what a comparable job would cost a client today. I still have the original invoice saved somewhere, a genuinely embarrassing document by current standards, no terms, no scope language, just a flat number and a thank-you note underneath it, and I keep it specifically because looking at it every year around this date is a useful, slightly humbling exercise in remembering how far the actual craft and the actual business sense have come since then, not just the equipment or the portfolio. Five years is long enough now that I have real perspective on this in a way that felt impossible even at the two-year mark, when everything still felt precarious and every reflection was really just relief dressed up as insight, relief that the business hadn't collapsed yet rather than genuine confidence that it wouldn't. This year, for the first time, the anniversary doesn't feel like a survival milestone. It feels like an actual point to look back from with something closer to real perspective, and that shift itself feels worth marking here properly rather than letting the date pass unremarked the way I've let a couple of the earlier anniversaries slide by without much ceremony at all.

## The Arc, Roughly

Year one was pure scramble, the $150 mug job followed by a genuinely chaotic stretch of underpriced work, mostly friends-of-friends and small local businesses, taken almost indiscriminately because I didn't yet trust that better-paying, better-fit work existed or would find me if I waited for it. Year two was where real client relationships started forming, the first version of what would eventually become steadier bookings with people who came back a second and third time rather than one-off jobs that never repeated, though pricing was still rough and mostly guesswork dressed up as confidence I didn't actually have yet. Year three brought the first genuinely painful client conflict, a dispute over usage rights I hadn't specified clearly enough in an early, sloppy contract, which cost real money to resolve but taught me more about contract language in a single bad month than any amount of careful reading ever could have on its own. Year four was the reflective one, see [[183-year-two-reflections]] even though it's misnamed for year two specifically, where I started deliberately shaping the client base rather than just accepting whatever came in, saying no more often and noticing the day rate climbing as a direct result of that shift rather than as some separate, unrelated stroke of luck. And this year, year five, has been about specialization, leaning hard into jewelry and reflective-surface work specifically, see [[190-article-future-of-product-photography]] for the fuller argument behind that particular bet, and it's the first year where the business finally feels like it has a genuinely distinct identity rather than being a generic product photography service that happens to also shoot whatever comes through the door regardless of fit. Revenue this year crossed $61,000, up from that first $150 mug job by a margin that still feels almost unreal to type out, and the studio itself has gone from a spare bedroom with a single strobe to an actual leased space about to move somewhere even bigger.

## What Surprised Me Looking Back

The thing that surprises me most isn't any single decision, it's how much slower the whole process felt while it was actually happening compared to how it reads now, compressed down into five tidy paragraphs describing five years as if the throughline were obvious the whole time. Year three's contract dispute, for instance, felt at the time like it might genuinely end the business, an existential threat rather than a lesson, and now it barely registers as more than a formative but ultimately survivable data point in a five-year arc that, in hindsight, looks far more like a coherent, deliberate trajectory than the anxious, mostly reactive scramble it actually felt like while I was living through each individual stretch of it one uncertain month at a time. I also didn't expect how much the slow, unglamorous habits, weekly bookkeeping, a real backup system, a written contract, would end up mattering more to how the business actually feels day to day than any single big client win or gear purchase ever did.

## Looking Toward Year Six

I don't have a grand plan for year six the way this kind of reflective anniversary note almost seems to demand, and I think that's genuinely fine rather than a gap I need to fill artificially just for the sake of a tidy ending. The studio relocation and the deeper specialization into jewelry and reflective work are already in motion and will likely define most of the practical texture of the year ahead regardless of anything else I decide. If there's one intention worth naming here explicitly, it's simply to keep writing these anniversary notes honestly, including the parts that were genuinely hard and not just the comfortable, retrospectively tidy version of events, since this one has been more useful to actually sit down and write than I expected going in tonight.
