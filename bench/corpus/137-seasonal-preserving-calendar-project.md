Building a proper seasonal preserving calendar this year instead of canning whatever's cheapest whenever I notice it at the market, which has led to a pantry with six jars of one thing and none of another for the past three winters running.

## The problem

Last winter I ran out of tomato sauce by February but still had eight jars of pickled green beans nobody in the house particularly likes, sitting untouched on the shelf month after month. Clearly quantity planning, not just technique, has been the actual gap, and no amount of better canning skill fixes that on its own.

## Research

Checked what's actually in season locally month by month, cross-referenced against what the household eats regularly rather than what sounds impressive to can. Priyam's notes from her own pantry planning were a useful starting template, adjusted for our smaller household and different taste preferences, particularly around how much pickled anything we realistically get through in a month without it sitting untouched. Also cut two ingredients entirely, pickled beets and hot pepper jelly, since neither's been eaten past the first jar in three years.

## The calendar

June: strawberry jam, small batch, since we only eat so much of it. July: cucumber pickles, doubled from last year since we ran out by October. August through September: tomato sauce and salsa, the bulk of the year's canning effort.

## Quantities

Tomato sauce jumps to twenty pints from last year's twelve, based on counting how many we used per month over winter. Jam and pickles stay flat, since neither ran out early nor sat unused for long. Before committing to the bigger number, confirmed shelf space exists — the shelf fits roughly twenty-four pints.

## Execution plan

Big tomato weekend gets blocked on the calendar in late August once the garden's peak hits, with a backup weekend the following week in case the first gets rained out or the tomatoes still aren't quite ready. Everything else stays smaller and more flexible, fit in around normal weekends rather than scheduled months ahead, since jam and pickle batches only take an afternoon rather than a full weekend commitment each time around, unlike the tomato push.

## Review point

Checking pantry levels every two months through winter this time, logging what's actually eaten, and adjusting next year's quantities based on real depletion rather than guessing again from memory alone like every year before this one.
