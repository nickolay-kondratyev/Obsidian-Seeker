Dana brought up downsizing the house again tonight, over dishes, in the way she does when she's been sitting on something for a while and finally decides the moment is right. We've circled this before, usually in the abstract, as a someday-maybe line item, but tonight she had specific numbers: what we could sell for, what a smaller place two towns over would cost, and what the difference would do to our timeline if invested instead of paying a mortgage on space we mostly don't use. I wasn't ready for numbers. I thought we were still in the feelings stage of this conversation, and she'd apparently moved past that days ago without telling me, which stung a little even though I know that's not fair to her. She'd clearly been thinking about this quietly for a while, turning it over during her commute or in the shower, the way she does with most big decisions before she ever says a word about them out loud. I just wasn't expecting to be caught up all at once, mid-dishes, with a number attached.

## Catching up to her spreadsheet

I've started a proper note on this, see [[232-downsizing-house-project]], because it deserves more structure than a diary entry can give it, but I wanted to write down how it actually felt to have this conversation before I sanitize it into a project plan with bullet points and milestones. It felt fast. It felt like she'd already decided and was walking me through the decision rather than making it together, even though I know that's not really what happened — she was excited and talking quickly, and excitement reads as certainty even when it isn't. I asked her to slow down twice. She did, both times, and apologized, but I could tell some of the energy went out of the conversation when I did that, and I felt a little bad about being the brake on something she was genuinely happy about.

The house itself I have complicated feelings about that have nothing to do with the financial case, which is apparently strong on its own. We bought it when we thought we'd have more people living in it eventually, and that particular future didn't happen the way we imagined, and there's a version of this move that feels like admitting that out loud in a way we haven't really done directly with each other. I don't think that's what Dana is doing when she pulls up listings on her phone. I think she's just doing math. But I notice I'm bringing something heavier into the room than the math requires.

## Where I land tonight

Not against it. Genuinely not against it — the math clearly moves the retirement date closer, and that's not nothing given everything else we've been working toward. I just want to be inside the decision earlier next time, not catching up to a spreadsheet with a scenario already highlighted in green. I told her that directly, and she said fair, and that she'd loop me in from the start next time instead of doing the exploratory math alone first.

## What she actually proposed

The concrete version: sell within the next eighteen months, once a few overdue repairs are done and the market looks reasonable, and take the equity difference — she estimates somewhere between ninety and a hundred and thirty thousand after closing costs and the smaller place's purchase price — straight into the brokerage account rather than treating it as spending money for the new place's furnishings or anything else we might want. She's also run rough numbers on the smaller mortgage or, in the version she likes better, no mortgage at all if we can make the numbers work, which would eliminate our single largest recurring expense outright and change the whole shape of our monthly budget. She showed me a spreadsheet tab I didn't know she'd built, three scenarios side by side with a clear favorite, and it was obvious she'd been working on this quietly for at least a couple of weeks before bringing it to me tonight, turning it over on her commute probably. Part of me was impressed by how thorough it was, down to a rough moving-cost estimate and a note about which repairs would actually move the sale price versus which ones were cosmetic and skippable. Part of me felt like I'd been handed a finished proposal instead of invited into a discussion, and I said so, gently, and she took it well — better than I expected, actually, which made me feel a little guilty for how defensive I'd been getting internally the whole time she was talking, turning over an objection I never quite managed to voice out loud until near the end. She also mentioned Kofi, her coworker who did something similar two years ago, as someone we could talk to about the logistics of actually selling and buying in the same stretched-out window without ending up briefly homeless between closings, which is apparently a real risk if the timing slips even a few weeks in either direction.

## One good thing

She squeezed my hand across the table when I told her I needed a minute to catch up, instead of getting frustrated or pushing further, which is not nothing after a conversation that started sideways for both of us. Small gesture, but it's the kind of thing I want to remember on the harder nights ahead.
