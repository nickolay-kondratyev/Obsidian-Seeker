Year-end review call with Kofi, who runs an outdoor gear brand I've shot for eight times this year, mostly technical apparel and hardware like carabiners and tent stakes. 40 minutes, video call, no one else joined. Purpose was mutual: Kofi wanted feedback on how his team's briefs had evolved over the year, and I wanted honest feedback on whether the work was landing the way his marketing team needed, since I rarely see the actual ad placements. We'd never done a formal year-end review before, past conversations were always tactical, scheduling the next shoot, never actually reflective about the relationship as a whole, and both of us agreed afterward it should become an annual habit rather than a one-off experiment we happened to try this year.

## What Worked This Year

Kofi opened by saying the technical hardware shots, carabiners, buckles, stakes, had become genuinely the strongest asset in their catalog according to internal click-through data his team tracks, outperforming lifestyle shots by a wide margin on the product pages specifically, though lifestyle still wins on social. He credited the macro-level detail work, close enough to show machining marks and material texture, as the differentiator against competitors who shoot everything at a more generic catalog distance. I mentioned that this feedback was genuinely useful to hear, since I'd been quietly assuming the apparel shots were carrying more weight given how much time they take relative to the smaller hardware pieces, and it recalibrated how I think about where to spend extra care during a mixed shoot day when time is tight and something has to get the shorter end of the schedule. We also agreed the briefing process had improved substantially since spring, when Kofi's team started sending reference images alongside written briefs rather than text descriptions alone, cutting revision rounds nearly in half according to both our informal tallies of the year's invoices and email threads. He also flagged that the two shoots where I'd pushed back on an unrealistic prop count, suggesting fewer items shot more carefully instead, had produced noticeably better final images than the earlier, more crowded shoots from spring, and he wants that pushback to keep happening rather than treating my initial brief as fixed.

## Where It Fell Short

Two friction points came up that neither of us had fully named before this call, both of which felt more useful to have out in the open than either of us expected going in. First, turnaround time on the two rush jobs this year, both under 48 hours' notice, produced work Kofi's team was happy with but that I personally felt was below my usual bar, rushed macro focus stacking in particular, and I told him directly that repeated rush requests would eventually mean either a rush premium or a harder no, since quality control on tight technical work genuinely suffers past a certain point no matter how experienced I get at working fast. He was receptive and said the rush requests were mostly avoidable on their end, tied to a launch calendar that keeps slipping internally for reasons above his role, not anything about my work specifically, and he agreed to push back internally on that pattern going forward, framing it as a quality-preservation issue rather than a vendor complaint, which he thought would land better with his own leadership than the alternative. Second, I raised that two invoices this year took over 45 days to get paid despite 30-day terms, which had created real cash flow friction on my end during an otherwise slow month, and asked directly whether that was a process issue on their side or something I should be flagging differently in my own invoicing going forward. Kofi didn't have a clean answer in the moment but said he'd check with their finance team and get back to me, seeming genuinely surprised it had happened twice, which I believed, he's been reliable and responsive in every other part of the relationship over the two years we've worked together, so I don't think this was intentional on anyone's part, more likely a process gap somewhere between his approval and their actual payment run that neither of us had visibility into until this conversation surfaced it directly.

## Looking Ahead to Next Year

Kofi mentioned a possible expansion into a new product line, insulated water bottles, sometime in Q2, which would mean a new set of reflective and thermal-material challenges I haven't shot much of before, closer to the kind of work I've been doing more of with the jewelry clients than to the matte hardware I usually shoot for his brand, and he wants to loop me in early on that rather than treating it as a standard booking once the product samples exist. We also floated the idea of a light retainer arrangement similar to what I've been discussing with a homeware client, see [[186-client-retainer-negotiation]], though Kofi was upfront that his budget approval process makes a retainer harder to justify internally than a per-project invoice, so this stays a loose idea rather than a real proposal for now, something to revisit once the water bottle line is further along. We closed the call by agreeing that even without a retainer, giving each other more lead time on both sides, his launch calendar and my booking calendar, would solve most of this year's friction on its own without needing a bigger structural change to the relationship. Kofi also asked whether I'd be open to a short site visit at their warehouse sometime in Q1, mostly so I could see how the hardware actually gets used in the field before shooting the new bottle line, which struck me as a genuinely useful offer that no other client has made in two years of working together, and one I plan to take him up on.
