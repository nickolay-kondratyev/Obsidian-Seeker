# A Short History of the Club

The club started in 2009 as six people meeting outside a hardware store on Saturday mornings, which is a smaller and less romantic origin than most members probably imagine. There was no clubhouse, no committee, and no name for the first year; riders just called it 'the Saturday group' and word spread slowly through workplaces and the handful of local bike shops willing to put up a flyer. The founding six were mostly commuters who wanted company on longer weekend rides rather than racers looking for competition, and that distinction has quietly shaped the club's culture ever since, for better and occasionally for worse when newer members expecting a racing club find something closer to a social riding group with a strong maintenance and touring streak. By 2011 the group had grown past twenty regular riders and needed some basic structure: a shared calendar, an actual name, and somewhere to store the first set of borrowed marshal vests that kept going missing between rides. The clubhouse arrangement, a converted storage unit behind the old bakery, came about almost by accident when one founding member's cousin offered the space cheaply rather than through any deliberate search, and the club has been there ever since, gradually filling the space with the accumulated clutter that eventually prompted the storage shed project. A proper committee structure followed in 2013, with elected roles for ride captain, treasurer, and events coordinator, mostly copied from the constitution of a nearby running club that one member happened to belong to as well. Membership numbers have moved in waves since then, spiking noticeably after 2020 when a lot of people discovered cycling during a period when many other social activities weren't available, and settling since into a steadier base of around ninety active members, which is roughly where things stand today, comfortably larger than the founding six ever imagined back outside that hardware store.

## Naming the Club

The current name was chosen in 2011 by an informal vote at the pub after someone's suggestion involving a pun on the local reservoir was, thankfully, voted down. Three names made the shortlist, decided by a show of hands among maybe fifteen people who happened to be there that particular evening, which in hindsight was a fairly casual way to settle something that's now printed on jerseys and the clubhouse sign. Nobody has seriously proposed changing it since, though the treasurer occasionally jokes about how much it would cost to rebrand at this point, given how much kit already carries the old name printed or stitched onto it somewhere.

## Growing Pains

Growth hasn't always been smooth. The most difficult period was probably 2017 and 2018, when a rapid influx of new members outpaced the club's ability to onboard them properly, leading to a string of minor incidents on weekly rides: riders getting dropped without anyone noticing, a couple of near-misses at junctions the group hadn't properly marshalled, and at least one genuinely heated argument in the clubhouse about ride pacing that a few longtime older members still bring up occasionally. That period is largely why the club now insists on a structured new-member induction and, more recently, the mentorship model for training new ride leaders described elsewhere in the vault. It was an uncomfortable couple of years at the time, but most long-standing members now consider it the point where the club grew up organisationally rather than just numerically, and the systems built in response are still mostly in use today, adjusted here and there but never fully replaced by anything more elaborate.

## What's Stayed the Same

Despite all the structural changes, a few things have stayed remarkably consistent since the earliest Saturday-morning rides outside the hardware store. The club has never charged more than a modest annual fee, deliberately kept low so cost is never the reason someone doesn't join, even as insurance and event costs have crept up over the years and forced some careful budgeting elsewhere to compensate. The weekly Saturday ride has run every single week without exception since 2009, through snow, through the odd cancelled event, and through at least one memorably wet November where only two people showed up and rode anyway, which has become a small point of pride whenever it comes up in conversation at the pub afterwards. The club has also never had a serious falling-out that split the membership, which committee members who've been around long enough will tell you is more luck than management, given how many small clubs elsewhere have fractured over exactly the kind of pacing and inclusion disagreements this club has mostly managed to talk through rather than let fester. And perhaps most importantly, the culture of turning up for each other, whether that's a marshal standing in the rain for three hours or someone driving out with a spare wheel for a stranded rider, has survived every wave of new membership largely intact, passed on more by example on the road than by anything written down in a handbook.

## Looking Ahead

The next few years will likely bring a proper clubhouse lease renewal negotiation, continued growth in the touring and gravel side of the club, and, if the shed project goes well, a slightly less cluttered back room to plan things from.
