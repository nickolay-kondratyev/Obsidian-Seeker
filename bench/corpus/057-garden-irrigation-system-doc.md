# Garden Irrigation System

Reference document for the full irrigation layout across all four garden zones from [[037-garden-redesign-plan]], built out gradually as each zone got planted and the [[039-garden-irrigation-howto]] techniques were refined through trial and error along the way this season.

## Vegetable Zone

Drip irrigation on a timer, twenty minutes daily through summer, dialed back to every other day once weather cools into September. This zone gets the most attention since it's also the most water-hungry of the four by a meaningful margin, and the one where a missed watering day shows up fastest in visibly wilted leaves.

## Orchard Extension

Deep, infrequent watering rather than daily drip, using a simple soaker hose ring around each young tree's drip line, moved further out as the trees establish and their root systems spread wider over coming seasons. The dwarf pears get their own smaller rings, watered on a slightly different schedule than the established apple tree, since their shallower first-year roots dry out faster than the apple's much deeper, decades-established root system ever does even in a real heat wave.

## Gravel Border and Herb Strip

The gravel border gets no irrigation at all, by design; drought-tolerant perennials were chosen so this zone needs nothing beyond the swale Tomasz recommended. The herb strip, by contrast, is hand-watered most evenings, a small area not worth automating.

## Water Source and Timer

A single hose bib feeds the whole system through the pressure regulator discussed at length in the irrigation how-to, split via a manifold into separate lines for the vegetable and orchard zones, each controlled independently by its own timer setting rather than sharing one schedule between the two, which lets us run different durations for each without touching the other zone's settings.

## Seasonal Adjustments

Spring: full daily schedule as new plantings establish root systems, with extra attention to the dwarf pears during their first season. Summer: peak watering, monitored during any real heat wave, with manual top-ups on the hottest days. Fall: tapering down as growth slows and days shorten, watching the extension office's frost dates to time the last watering. Winter: fully disconnected, drained, and stored per the winterizing steps in the how-to, with the timer battery pulled to avoid corrosion.

## Maintenance Log

Check emitters each spring for clogs, replace the timer battery in early summer, and inspect all tubing for winter cracking before that first spring reconnection each year, noting any repairs made directly in this log so the pattern is visible over time.
