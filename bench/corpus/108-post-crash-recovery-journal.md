July 22. Three weeks since the crash and this is the first day I've felt like writing about it properly rather than just noting physio appointments in my calendar. Came off on the descent past the old quarry, the same stretch that came up in the ride-planning discussion months ago about capping group sizes, though to be fair this had nothing to do with group size and everything to do with a patch of loose gravel I simply didn't see in time. Front wheel washed out on the corner, I went down hard on my left side, and Lotte, who was riding just behind, later said I slid a good four or five metres before stopping against the verge. Broke my collarbone, cleanly according to the hospital, plus a fair amount of road rash down my left arm and hip that looked far worse than it turned out to be once the swelling went down. No surgery needed, thankfully; just a sling, painkillers for the first week, and a lot of enforced sitting still, which is genuinely the part I've found hardest so far, harder even than the collarbone itself.

## The Ride Itself

What I remember most clearly, oddly, isn't the fall but the few seconds right before it: coming into the corner slightly too fast, feeling the front end start to go light, and that strange stretched-out moment where you know something's wrong but haven't yet processed what to do about it. Lotte stopped immediately and stayed with me while Tomasz, who was further back, called for help and flagged down a passing car to guide the ambulance in, since the quarry road doesn't have a clear postcode for satnav purposes and it's easy to miss the turning if you don't already know it. The club's WhatsApp group apparently lit up within minutes of Tomasz's message, and by the time I was in the ambulance three separate people had already offered to drive my bike home and check in on me that evening. I mention this mostly because it's the part of the whole experience that's stuck with me most: not the pain, which faded faster than expected, but how quickly and completely the club rallied around me without anyone having to ask twice. Dana visited the next day with a casserole that fed me for three days, which felt like an oddly practical and deeply kind thing for someone to think of amid everything else going on that week. Priyam has been messaging most days just to check in, nothing heavy, usually a single line asking how the shoulder's doing, which somehow means more than a longer message would, since it doesn't demand a proper reply when I'm too tired to write one.

## The Physical Side

Collarbone fractures apparently heal well in six to eight weeks for most people, though the physio was careful not to promise a specific timeline for me individually. Sleeping has been the hardest part, since I can't lie on my left side at all and I'm apparently a committed side-sleeper who never noticed that fact until it became physically impossible. Propped up on three pillows most nights now, which helps a little, though I still wake up several times wanting to roll over before remembering why I can't.

## What I'm Thinking About

Mostly I've been thinking about how differently this could have gone, which isn't a productive thought exactly but keeps surfacing anyway during the long, boring hours of not being able to do much. A slightly different angle, a car coming the other way on that corner, a slower reaction from Lotte, any of a dozen small variables could have turned a bad but survivable crash into something much worse, and there's not much to do with that thought except sit with it for a while before it eventually loses some of its weight. I've also been thinking, more usefully, about what actually caused this: loose gravel on a corner I've ridden probably fifty times without incident, which suggests either the road surface has degraded recently or I simply got unlucky with debris that happened to wash onto the racing line that particular morning after some rain overnight. Either way, it feels worth mentioning to whoever ends up doing the next scouting pass on that section, since a corner that's caught one rider badly is worth a second look even if it turns out to be nothing more than bad luck on an otherwise fine stretch of road. There's also a smaller, more personal thought I keep circling back to: how much I've missed the actual riding these three weeks, not in an abstract way but specifically, the Saturday group, the particular sound of the group settling into a rhythm on the long flat sections, the small rituals of a normal ride weekend. I hadn't quite realised how much of my week is structurally built around cycling until it was suddenly, temporarily, taken away by six weeks of enforced rest and careful movement. I've caught myself scrolling through old ride photos more than once this week, which feels a bit maudlin to admit but is apparently just what recovering from something like this does to a person's mood on the quieter afternoons when there's nothing much else to occupy the time.

## Next Steps

Follow-up scan in three weeks to check healing progress, then hopefully cleared for gentle turbo sessions after that, well before any real return to the road. Patience, apparently, is the entire plan for now, which has never been my strongest skill, if I'm honest about it, but I suppose there's no shortcut available here worth taking.
