Small system for cancelling subscriptions we've been meaning to cut, after realizing during the last budget review that we were paying for three streaming services nobody had opened in months.

## Step 1: Audit first

Pull the last three months of card statements and list every recurring charge, even the small ones under ten dollars, since those hide easily and add up more than they seem to individually, especially anything billed annually that you'd otherwise forget entirely.

## Step 2: Rate and sort each one

Mark each as "keep," "cancel," or "unsure," then sort the cancel list by effort.

## Step 3: Cancel the easy ones immediately

Do the one-click cancellations same day, right after the audit, while the motivation from seeing the total is still fresh. Waiting even a day drops the follow-through rate noticeably in my experience — momentum matters more than scheduling it "properly" for later, and putting it off past dinner basically guarantees it slips another week.

## Step 4: Batch the phone calls

Group the annoying ones — the "call to cancel" services — into a single Saturday morning block, back to back, so you only psych yourself up once.

## Step 5: Log the savings

Add each cancelled subscription's monthly cost to a running "reclaimed" total in the budget sheet, next to the debt payoff numbers from [[212-debt-payoff-plan]]. Watching that number climb steadily is its own small, quiet motivation to keep going.

## Step 6: Set a re-check reminder

Six months out, an actual calendar reminder, not just a note to memory.

## Step 7: Watch for the win-back offers

A few services will email a discount to try to win you back within a week or two of cancelling. Decide in advance whether you're actually interested, on the audit's terms, not the panic of losing a "deal" — otherwise you'll re-subscribe out of scarcity and undo the exercise before it stuck. We ended up back on one service at half price because the discount made sense against actual usage, and let two others expire unanswered because the honest answer was still "unsure" tipping toward cancel.
