## Before you start

Confirm you have the customer's legal business name and a billing email address before opening the admin panel. Both are required and can't be added later without a support ticket.

## Step 1: create the org

Use the "New Organization" flow from the admin panel, not the customer self-signup form, for any manually onboarded account — the two flows set slightly different defaults, and mixing them up causes small but annoying support headaches later.

## Step 2: set the currency

Confirm the default currency directly; it can't be changed after creation without a manual migration.

## Step 3: invite the first user

Send the invite to the actual billing contact, not a generic shared inbox — invites to shared inboxes get missed constantly. Confirm the invite landed before ending the call, and if it hasn't arrived within a couple of minutes, resend it manually rather than assuming it's just delayed, since delivery failures are common enough that assuming "it'll show up eventually" wastes the customer's time.

## Step 4: walk through the first invoice together

Have the customer create one real draft invoice while still on the call, rather than leaving them to figure it out alone. This catches most confusion immediately.

## Step 5: confirm the webhook test

> [!tip] Sent invoices are immutable
> Editing a sent invoice creates a new draft revision, not an in-place change. Mention this before their first real send — it avoids the "did that not save?" confusion.

## Step 6: schedule a follow-up

Book a fifteen-minute check-in for one week out, not "as needed" or "whenever."

## Common mistakes

Skipping the live first-invoice walkthrough because the call is running long — this is the single biggest predictor of a confused support ticket in the customer's first week, more than any other step in this list. Setting the wrong default currency because nobody asked directly and just guessed from the billing address on file. And inviting a generic shared inbox instead of a real billing contact, which means invite emails get missed or ignored entirely by whoever eventually checks that inbox weeks later, if anyone does at all.
