Planning meeting for the neighborhood canning swap, held at the community hall on a Tuesday evening since Dana's kitchen table proved too small last year once word spread and a dozen more people showed up than expected. Present: Dana, Priyam, Kofi, Lotte, plus two new faces from the building down the street who heard about it through Kofi. The purpose tonight was narrower than a normal ferment club meeting — just logistics for the April swap, not general fermentation chat, though that discipline slipped a couple of times as it always does when Kofi starts talking about his kimchi. Lotte took notes since Dana was running the projector with last year's photos as a reference point for space planning.

## Recap of last year

Last year's swap drew around thirty people into a space meant for maybe twenty, and the table layout became a bottleneck almost immediately, with everyone clustering near the entrance instead of spreading through the room. Jars went fast in the first twenty minutes and slow after, which in hindsight suggests we needed a second wave announcement rather than everything landing on the tables at once. The labeling situation was inconsistent — some people wrote contents and date, others just wrote a cheerful name with no ingredients listed, which caused at least one allergy scare when someone with a shellfish allergy nearly took home a jar of fish sauce-based kimchi without realizing. Feedback afterward, collected informally through the group chat, was mostly positive but repeatedly mentioned wanting more table space, clearer labels, and a water station since the hall got warm with that many people and jars. Priyam also noted that several people left early because there was nowhere to sit and actually taste anything before deciding what to take home, which probably reduced how much got swapped overall compared to a more relaxed pace. Kofi added that parking outside the hall was worse than anticipated, with several attendees circling before giving up and parking three streets over, which likely discouraged people who'd only meant to stop by briefly. Lotte mentioned cleanup took almost two hours because nobody had assigned teardown roles, and the four of them ended up doing most of it themselves while newer attendees had already left. Looking back at the photos, it's obvious the entrance bottleneck was the single biggest problem of the night.

## This year's format

The plan this year splits the hall into three zones instead of one big table: a drop-off and labeling station near the entrance, a tasting area with small cups and spoons in the middle, and the actual swap tables along the back wall, moving traffic through in a rough loop rather than one crowded scrum. Labeling becomes mandatory this time, using the simple format Priyam already drafted for the ferment club — contents, date, salt or sugar percentage if relevant, and any common allergens flagged with a colored sticker system, red for shellfish or fish-based ferments, yellow for dairy, green for everything else. We're adding a water and seltzer station near the tasting area after last year's complaints about the heat, and asking everyone to bring their own cups this time to cut down on cleanup. Kofi volunteered to handle the allergen sticker system since he already has experience labeling from selling at the farmer's market booth a few times last summer, which felt like the right person for the job given how badly the shellfish mix-up could have gone. We also agreed to cap attendance loosely around forty by only promoting it through the existing group chat and word of mouth rather than posting flyers around the neighborhood like last year, which is probably what drove the surprise turnout in the first place. To fix the parking problem Kofi raised, Dana will ask the building manager whether the small overflow lot behind the hall can be opened for the evening, and if not, we'll add a note to the group chat suggesting people carpool or walk from the nearby streets rather than circling for a spot right outside. We're also assigning specific teardown roles this time instead of leaving cleanup to whoever's still standing at the end, with Lotte drafting a short checklist covering tables, chairs, the water station, and trash, so nobody has to improvise at eleven at night like last year. Dana suggested a soft cutoff time of 9pm for new drop-offs, so the last hour can be dedicated to tasting and swapping rather than a fresh wave of jars arriving right as people are trying to wind down.

## Logistics and roles

Dana will book the hall again and confirm the exact room layout with the building manager, ideally getting the three-zone floor plan approved in advance rather than improvising on the day. Priyam owns the labeling system and will print a stack of the sticker sheets plus a laminated instruction card for the drop-off table so volunteers don't have to explain the system from memory to every single person. Lotte is handling the water and seltzer station, including cups, ice, and a small folding table borrowed from her sister. Kofi manages the allergen stickers and will spend the first thirty minutes at drop-off helping people flag their jars correctly before rotating to general floor support. The two new attendees, still getting oriented, offered to help with setup and teardown, which we gratefully accepted given how much furniture needs moving before and after. Dana is also the point of contact for the building manager on parking, and will confirm by the next meeting whether the overflow lot is actually available. Lotte's teardown checklist gets shared in the group chat a week before the swap so people can volunteer for tasks in advance rather than being assigned on the spot, which worked poorly last time. Priyam is responsible for a short verbal reminder at the start of the evening explaining the sticker system, since a printed card alone clearly wasn't enough given the shellfish scare. Kofi will also bring a folding sign for the entrance pointing people toward drop-off first, reinforcing the new traffic flow.

## Open questions

Still unresolved: whether to charge a small fee to cover the hall rental and cups, or keep it fully free and eat the cost between the four of us like last year. Dana leans toward a suggested two-dollar donation jar rather than a mandatory fee, since a hard price point might discourage first-timers from showing up at all. Also unresolved is the exact cap on jars per person, unlimited swapping was chaotic last year but a strict number feels overly bureaucratic for a casual event. Lotte suggested a soft guideline instead, something like "bring roughly what you'd want to take home," posted on the sign-in sheet rather than enforced at the door. There's also lingering disagreement about whether to allow baked goods alongside jars, since a couple of people asked last time whether bread counted, and nobody had a good answer. Priyam is mildly opposed, worried it complicates the sticker system since flour and dairy need their own categories, while Kofi thinks it would draw a friendlier crowd. No decision tonight; we're tabling it until Priyam has time to sketch out whether stickers can reasonably extend to baked goods.
