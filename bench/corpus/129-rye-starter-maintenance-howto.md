Once a rye starter is established, maintenance is simpler than the first two weeks suggest. This covers the routine I settled on for Gustav, my starter from [[123-sourdough-starter-journal]].

## Daily vs weekly feeding

If baking often, feed once daily at room temperature, doubling within five to eight hours. If baking less than twice a week, refrigerate and feed weekly instead — the cold slows activity enough that a full week between feeds won't starve it.

## Ratio

I use 1:1:1 by weight, rye flour, water, starter, unless scaling up before a big bake.

## Signs it's hungry

Watch for a layer of dark liquid on top, called hooch, and a sharper, more acetone-like smell than the usual pleasant tang. Both mean it's overdue for a feed, running low on food for a while, sometimes longer than you'd guess from the jar alone. Neither means it's dead, just neglected, and a fresh feed brings it back within a day or two.

## Reviving after the fridge

Take it out, discard all but a couple tablespoons, and feed at room temperature. Expect a sluggish first rise, sometimes taking twice as long before it's back to strength.

## Before baking

Feed twice in the 24 hours before mixing dough, and use it at peak, roughly doubled and still domed rather than flat or sunken. A starter used past peak gives a denser, less lively loaf, so timing the final feed matters most of all.

## Storage between bakes

Fridge, loosely lidded, feeding weekly is enough for most home bakers between bake days.

## Common mistakes

Using a metal bowl for brief contact is fine, but avoid long soaks in reactive metal. A bigger real mistake is feeding straight from the fridge without letting it warm up first, which slows the whole cycle more than expected. Also common: discarding too little at each feed, which lets the jar creep upward over months until it's suddenly overflowing during a routine feed, usually the one time you're in a hurry. Keep discard proportional and the jar stays predictable week to week.
