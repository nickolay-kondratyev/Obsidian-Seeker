# Tidewatch Manufacturing Plan

This document tracks the manufacturing path for the Tidewatch print run, covering component sourcing, vendor selection, and the rough timeline from approved files to boxes on a shelf. It exists so Dana, Lotte, and Tomasz are working from the same assumptions instead of three separate mental models of what "ready to print" actually means. Anything that changes here should be dated and explained, not silently overwritten, since this doc gets referenced during Kickstarter fulfillment planning long after the decisions were made.

## Component Breakdown

Tidewatch ships with a hex tile board printed on chipboard, four wooden warden pawns per faction color, roughly ninety cardstock cards split across event, storm, and supply decks, a rulebook, a player aid sheet per seat, and the tide and storm token set covered separately in [[277-tidewatch-component-list]]. The board is the most expensive single component because of its size and the die-cut lighthouse insert, so most of our cost-cutting conversations start there. Tomasz has been getting quotes from two vendors: one specializing in card and box print runs, another better suited to chipboard and wooden pieces. Splitting the order across two vendors adds coordination overhead but should meaningfully reduce the standard wood-pawn tooling cost we'd otherwise pay to a single generalist supplier for a run this small.

## Vendor Selection Criteria

We're weighing three factors when choosing between print vendors: minimum order quantity, turnaround time, and willingness to ship a physical proof before committing to the full run. A few hundred copies is small enough that some vendors won't take the order at all, or will only take it at a per-unit cost that erases our margin entirely. Tomasz has ruled out one vendor already because their minimum was nearly double our target print quantity and their sample turnaround was quoted at ten weeks, which would blow past our Kickstarter fulfillment window described in [[292-tidewatch-kickstarter-prep]]. The remaining two candidates both offered physical proofs within three weeks and minimums close to what we need, so the decision now comes down to per-unit pricing at our actual quantity and how their box assembly compares — Lotte specifically wants a vendor who assembles boxes in-house rather than shipping flat components we'd have to assemble ourselves, since none of us has warehouse space for that kind of manual labor at scale, and time is already tight before the planned launch window this spring.

## Timeline

Print files need to be locked by mid-February, physical proof approval by early March, full production run by late March, and fulfillment shipping by May, leaving a buffer before convention commitments. Delays anywhere push everything downstream, so Dana is treating file lock as a hard deadline this time, given how the last prototype slipped by three weeks and nearly missed a demo slot.

## Risks and Open Questions

Component costs could rise if raw material prices shift before the order is placed, particularly for the wooden pawns, and a failed proof such as a color mismatch or warped board would cost two to three weeks to resolve and reprint.
