Annual financial planning session, 2026-01-04, at the kitchen table with the full spreadsheet up on the laptop and last year's printed plan for comparison. Attendees: me, Dana. This is the meeting we take most seriously all year, the one where we actually reset targets rather than just track against existing ones, and we blocked off a full Saturday morning for it with no other plans competing for attention, coffee and pastries included to make it feel less like a chore and more like a ritual worth protecting. We've done this every January since 2023 and it keeps getting more useful as the history accumulates and we can actually compare year over year instead of guessing.

## Year in review

2025 net worth grew from $184,000 to $221,000, a $37,000 increase against a target of $32,000, beating plan mostly because of a stronger-than-expected market and one unplanned bonus in September that we mostly invested rather than spent on the kitchen renovation we'd been eyeing. Savings rate held at 34% of gross income for the full year, slightly above the 30% target we'd set twelve months earlier, and we managed that without feeling especially deprived, which was the real test of whether the number was sustainable rather than a one-time sprint. The debt payoff plan tracked in [[212-debt-payoff-plan]] stayed on schedule, with the car loan balance dropping faster than projected after we redirected subscription-cutting savings toward it starting in November. The one clear miss was the emergency fund, which dipped to two months of expenses in August after the water heater failure and hasn't fully recovered yet, currently sitting closer to two and a half months rather than the three-month target we'd like to maintain going forward into an uncertain year. We also underspent our travel budget by about $600, mostly because a planned trip in October got postponed, and that money quietly rolled into savings without us really deciding it should, which we want to be more intentional about this year rather than letting it happen by default. Our allocation drifted slightly more aggressive than target after the strong year, roughly 82% equities against an 80% target, worth a rebalance early in the new year. Overall 2025 felt like a genuinely good year, even accounting for the emergency fund miss.

## 2026 targets

We set the net worth target at $265,000 by December, a $44,000 increase that assumes market returns closer to the historical average rather than repeating 2025's above-average year, plus continued strong savings carrying most of the weight if returns disappoint. Savings rate target stays at 30% of gross as a floor, with 34% as a stretch goal if bonuses come in as expected in Q3, though we agreed not to plan around the bonus the way we accidentally did last year. Rebuild the emergency fund to a full three months of expenses by April, prioritized ahead of extra debt payments for the next few months specifically to close that gap before anything else gets extra attention, since last year's near-miss with the water heater made clear how thin the buffer actually was in practice. Debt payoff continues on the avalanche schedule with no changes: car loan projected clear by August, student loan by March 2027 per the existing plan, and we're not pulling money from that plan to accelerate the emergency fund rebuild, just directing new savings there instead. We also want to open and fund a taxable brokerage account for the first time this year, once debts are clear and the emergency fund is rebuilt, targeting a modest $200/month starting around September once the runway from earlier priorities allows for it without straining the rest of the budget. Finally, we set a smaller travel target of $2,400 for the year, split roughly across two trips, and agreed to actually spend it this time rather than letting it default back into savings the way 2025's allocation quietly did. We also want to rebalance the portfolio back toward the 80/20 equity-bond split in January, before any new contributions go in, rather than letting the drift from last year compound further while we debate it. Health insurance premiums are expected to rise about 6% at open enrollment this fall, so we're padding the healthcare line in the budget slightly ahead of that increase instead of reacting to it after the fact in October.

## Discussion points

We spent a good chunk of the morning debating whether to increase retirement contributions above the employer match now versus waiting until debts clear, and landed on waiting, since the interest rate gap still favors debt payoff over additional retirement contributions at current rates, a conclusion that matches the reasoning already written up in [[212-debt-payoff-plan]]. We also talked through the early retirement timeline more broadly, given the doubts that came up in journaling this year, and agreed the target date itself doesn't need to move, just that our comfort with the plan needs more deliberate attention through the year rather than being treated as a solved problem we don't revisit until it becomes a source of anxiety again. Dana raised whether we should hire a fee-only advisor for a single consultation just to sanity-check the whole plan from an outside perspective, and we agreed to look into it before the April review, budgeting up to $400 for a one-time session if we find someone reputable and genuinely fee-only rather than commission-based in disguise. We also discussed the house downsizing idea that's been floating around informally for a while, tracked more fully in [[232-downsizing-house-project]], and decided it's worth a real feasibility pass this year rather than staying a background thought we mention occasionally and never act on. Lastly we talked about whether our current asset allocation still matches our risk tolerance given how close we are to the middle of this journey, and agreed to review that specifically alongside the advisor consult rather than deciding anything today without more information in front of us.
