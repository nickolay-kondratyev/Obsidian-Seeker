January is always slow and I know this every year, and yet every year the first empty week in the calendar still manages to make me anxious in a way that the spreadsheet says is completely irrational. Looking back at three years of booking data, January and the first half of February consistently bring in less than a third of what a normal month brings, and it's not really about my business specifically, most of my clients are doing their own planning for the year ahead and simply aren't shooting new catalog content yet. Knowing this pattern exists on paper and actually feeling calm about an empty week on the calendar are two very different things, and this year, even with a genuinely healthy cash buffer built up from a strong Q4, that first blank Monday still hit the same old nerve. I spent an embarrassing amount of time this morning refreshing my inbox for no reason before catching myself and deciding to actually do something useful with the quiet instead of just marinating in the anxiety.

## What I Did With the Time

Instead of doom-scrolling my own calendar, I used the first slow week to catch up on everything that's been on the someday list since roughly October. Reorganized the prop closet, which had become a genuine hazard, half-used seamless paper rolls stacked in a way that guaranteed one would eventually fall on someone. Went through last year's invoices and flagged the three clients who were consistently slow to pay, over thirty days every time, and drafted a note to myself about tightening payment terms for them specifically rather than across the board, since most clients pay on time and I don't want to punish everyone for three people's habits. Also spent two afternoons on the [[187-website-refresh-project]] portfolio curation, which I'd been putting off for months because it always felt like there wasn't enough uninterrupted time during a normal busy month, but a slow January turned out to be exactly the right window for it. By Thursday I'd gone through three years of shoots and shortlisted about forty images for the new portfolio grid, more than I expected to have, which felt like a small win worth noting even if nobody but me will ever know how long that shortlist took to narrow down from the original couple hundred candidates. I also finally labeled and backed up two external drives that had been sitting in a drawer unsorted since the second year of the business, which is exactly the kind of task that never feels urgent until the day a drive fails and it suddenly becomes the most urgent thing in the world.

## A Genuinely Bad Afternoon

Wednesday afternoon was rough though, for what felt like no real reason. I'd been productive all morning and then around two o'clock just hit a wall, staring at the same invoice spreadsheet for twenty minutes without doing anything with it. I think it was less about the work itself and more about the quiet catching up with me after a frantic Q4. I closed the laptop, went for a walk, and came back an hour later feeling mostly normal again.

## Reframing It

By Friday I'd talked myself into a slightly different framing that I want to remember for next January, because I clearly need the reminder every single year around this time. The slow season isn't dead time to be endured, it's the only real maintenance window the business gets all year, since every other month is too busy to touch anything structural without risking a client-facing mistake. The prop closet, the invoice review, the portfolio work, none of that would have happened in April or October, there's simply no slack in those months for anything beyond the actual shoots and the editing queue behind them. I also noticed that framing it this way changed how the empty calendar itself felt to look at, less like a warning sign and more like space I'd actually earned after a hard quarter, which is a genuinely different feeling even though the number of bookings on the page hadn't changed at all between Monday and Friday. Dana mentioned something similar when we talked Thursday, that her own slow season used to feel like something had gone wrong until she started deliberately scheduling her own maintenance tasks into it years ago, on purpose, instead of just letting the emptiness happen to her passively and then scrambling to fill it with busywork she didn't actually care about. I want to try that next year, actually blocking out the maintenance list in December before January arrives, rather than discovering it reactively once the anxiety kicks in and I go looking for something productive to distract myself with. It's a small shift but it would mean going into the slow season with a plan instead of dread, which after three years of the same cycle repeating itself almost identically feels like a genuinely worthwhile thing to finally get right instead of relearning the same lesson every winter like clockwork. Even just writing it down here, in a note I can actually reread come December, feels like more of a commitment than the vague intention I've carried into every January before this one.

## One Thing to Carry Forward

Block the maintenance list in December, before the anxiety shows up uninvited. Treat January as planned downtime, not failure, since the maintenance always matters more than it looks like from the outside. Revisit this note next December and see if it stuck, and if it didn't, figure out honestly why before writing the same resolution again.
