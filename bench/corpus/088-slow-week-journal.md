April 21, 2026. Slow week. I keep telling myself that a slow week is still forward motion as long as I show up, but tonight it genuinely doesn't feel that way, more like I circled the same block of concepts three or four times without landing anywhere new. Work was heavier than usual, two late evenings in a row on an unrelated deadline, so the actual Rust time I got was maybe forty minutes total spread across five days instead of the hour or so most nights I've gotten used to since the study group started back in January. I opened the JSON flattener project twice and closed it again both times without touching a line, which is a specific kind of frustrating because I know exactly what the next step is, I just couldn't summon the focus to sit with it for more than a couple of minutes before my attention drifted somewhere easier, usually back to just scrolling instead of actually writing the recursive walker I already have sketched out in my head.

## What little did happen

Tuesday's study group session still happened, and honestly it was probably the best use of my limited time this week even though I showed up more tired than usual and contributed less than I normally would in the discussion. Heard Dana's update on her parser project finally handling nested expressions correctly, which was genuinely energizing to watch even secondhand, and it reminded me that progress on these things is rarely a smooth line, it's stalls followed by a sudden unlock once the right piece clicks into place after enough banging against it. Kofi mentioned "Effective Rust" again and I actually opened it on my phone during a commute and read through the first handful of items, jotted a few lines into [[085-effective-rust-item-notes]] on the train, which counts as real engagement even if it wasn't sitting at a keyboard writing code. That small amount of reading, oddly, felt more satisfying than the failed attempts to touch the flattener project, probably because it had a defined, small scope I could actually finish in one sitting instead of an open-ended coding session with no clear stopping point. Lotte also messaged the group chat with a screenshot of her file watcher's new config file working end to end, which was a nice small jolt of momentum to see scroll past even on a night when I had nothing of my own to add to the thread, a reminder that other people's steady progress doesn't actually take anything away from mine even when it's tempting to read it that way at the end of a long day.

## The nagging feeling

There's a specific kind of guilt that shows up on weeks like this, a nagging sense that I'm falling behind some imaginary pace I never actually set for myself, comparing my current week against my best weeks instead of against a realistic average across the whole stretch since January. It doesn't help that the study group chat keeps moving even on quiet weeks, other people's small wins scrolling past as a constant, low-grade reminder of everything I didn't get to, whether or not anyone intended it that way.

## Trying to reframe it

I want to write down, mostly for future me reading this back on some other slow week, that a forty-minute week following a string of solid ones is not the same thing as quitting, and it doesn't erase the ownership model finally clicking during the graph traversal exercise back in [[073-ownership-clicked]], or the fact that I can now read a panic backtrace without flinching, both real and durable gains that a bad week doesn't undo just because I didn't add to the pile this particular week. The whole point of doing this in the evenings around a full-time job was always that it would be uneven, some weeks generous with free time and focus, others eaten alive by everything else going on, and expecting a perfectly smooth upward line was never realistic to begin with, more a story I told myself early on before I'd actually lived through a few real slow stretches to test it against. What matters more, probably, is whether I come back to it at all once the heavier week passes, not whether any single week hit some arbitrary bar I set for myself without much thought, especially given that I chose evenings and weekends specifically because they'd stay flexible against everything else competing for the same hours. I've watched people quit these kinds of long personal projects entirely over exactly this feeling, the guilt compounding until picking it back up feels harder than it should, rather than just accepting the slow week for what it is and moving on without much fuss or self-recrimination. The trick, if there is one, seems to be treating the guilt itself as noise rather than signal, something to notice and set aside rather than something that gets to decide whether I open the laptop tomorrow night or not, since acting on it has never once actually produced more code, only more time spent feeling bad about not writing any.

Small win over no win, and no guilt attached either way if the week stays this thin, since that's apparently just how this whole thing goes sometimes and always has, months in.
