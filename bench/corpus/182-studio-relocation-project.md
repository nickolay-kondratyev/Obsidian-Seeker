# Studio Relocation Project

Moving the studio from the current 400 sq ft unit on the east side to a 650 sq ft space closer to the warehouse district. The lease ends June 30, 2026, and the new landlord will let us start build-out in May, so there's roughly a month of overlap. Goal is zero missed shoots during the transition, and ideally no gap in booking availability either, since a couple of clients have already asked about summer dates. This doc tracks the plan, the budget, and the open risks.

## Why Move

The current space has one real problem: ceiling height. At 8 feet, the c-stands can't get boom arms high enough for full-body product shots like shoe displays or mannequin work, and I've turned down two apparel clients this year because of it. The new unit has 11-foot ceilings and a loading dock, which matters more now that furniture and appliance clients are asking for on-location-style setups shot indoors. Rent goes up by about 35%, but the extra space also lets me stop paying for the offsite gear storage unit, which softens the increase. Foot traffic isn't a factor either way since clients book by referral or through the site. The extra footage also means I can keep a second cyc wall permanently set up instead of striking it between bookings, which currently eats close to an hour on furniture days.

## Timeline

May 1: sign new lease, get keys, start measuring for cyc wall and paint. May 4-8: electrician in to add the dedicated 20-amp circuits the strobes need, since the current wiring is original to the building and trips constantly under load. May 11-15: paint, flooring, and the cyc wall build, handled by a contractor Dana referred from a previous job. May 18-22: move non-essential gear and props first, keep the main kit at the old space until the last week to avoid downtime. May 25-29: final move of cameras, lights, and computers, test every outlet and light stand position before the first client shoot. June 1: first booked shoot in the new space, a jewelry client who's been with me for two years and is flexible if anything runs late. June 30: hand back keys to the old unit, final walkthrough with the old landlord for the deposit return. Any slack in the schedule gets absorbed by the two-week overlap rather than by rushing the paint or electrical work, since redoing either later would cost more than the extra rent.

## Budget

Total estimated at $9,400: electrical work $2,100, cyc wall and paint $2,600, flooring $1,800, moving labor $900, signage $600, contingency $1,400. That's on top of the lease deposit, paid from the savings buffer rather than financed, so cash flow stays healthy through the transition. ## Risks and Status

Biggest risk is the electrician falling behind, since the old wiring is a known problem and inspection could turn up something the estimate didn't cover. Second risk is a client shoot landing during the final move week; two are already flagged as flexible, but a third client hasn't confirmed yet.

As of March 2026 the lease is signed and the electrician is booked for the first week of May. Still waiting on flooring samples before locking that contractor in, and Dana is chasing the contact for the cyc wall build separately. No client shoots are currently scheduled during the blackout week of May 25-29, which was the main scheduling risk from the original plan and now looks resolved as long as nothing shifts.
