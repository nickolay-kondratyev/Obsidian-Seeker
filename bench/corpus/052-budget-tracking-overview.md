# Budget Tracking Overview

This document is the single running summary of renovation spend across kitchen, workshop, and garden, maintained alongside monthly reviews with Dana like [[041-budget-review-with-dana]]. It exists mainly so I stop trying to hold three separate budgets in my head at once, since that approach reliably failed within the first month and led to at least one avoidable overspend before we started tracking things properly in one place instead of scattered notes and half-remembered phone calls with contractors and suppliers.

## Overall Structure

Each of the three project areas has its own line-item budget with a fifteen percent contingency built in at the start, tracked separately so an overrun in one area doesn't quietly get absorbed by slack in another without anyone noticing the pattern. Kitchen sits at eighteen thousand dollars total, workshop at roughly twenty-two thousand once the concrete, framing, and electrical are all accounted for, and garden at thirty-two hundred across both planned phases. Dana reviews actuals against these numbers monthly, or sooner if something significant surfaces mid-cycle, like the cast iron drain or the workshop's surveyor requirement, both of which triggered an off-cycle conversation rather than waiting for the next scheduled review to come around on its own.

## Current Status by Project

Kitchen is running about six percent over its original estimate, almost entirely due to the unplanned drain line replacement found during demo, a cost that was always going to come from somewhere given the house's age even if we couldn't have predicted the specific number in advance. Workshop is harder to pin down precisely right now, with the electrical load calculation and surveyor's setback confirmation both still pending, though we're holding a placeholder line five hundred dollars above the original estimate until real numbers land and replace the guess, per Dana's recommendation from the last review. Garden costs shifted somewhat after the landscaper meeting, adding a drainage swale that wasn't in the original plan but roughly offset by savings on the revised orchard tree spacing, netting out close to even against the original thirty-two hundred figure once both changes are considered together rather than in isolation, a small relief given how many other lines have moved the wrong direction lately across the other two budgets.

## Contingency Tracking

Total contingency remaining across all three budgets sits at eleven percent, down from the fifteen we started with, which Dana considers healthy this early into a project with two major structural unknowns, the header beam and the workshop electrical, still not fully resolved on paper. She's flagged nine percent as the point where we'd need a harder conversation about scope.

## Monthly Review Cadence

Standard reviews happen roughly every three weeks with Dana, though we've moved to shorter cycles recently given how much is still unresolved between the workshop permit and the pending surveyor quote, both of which carry enough budget uncertainty that a longer gap between check-ins started to feel genuinely risky rather than just mildly inefficient. Each review updates this document directly, replacing placeholder figures with actuals as they land, and flags anything trending toward the contingency limit early enough that we still have room to make a deliberate trade-off rather than discovering the problem only once it's already too late to do much about it beyond simply absorbing the cost as it stands.
