August 2. Marshaled my first real event today, junction duty on the charity ride route from [[102-charity-ride-fundraiser]], the same course we walked through at the safety review meeting a few weeks back. Up at five, at the clubhouse by half six for the briefing, high-vis vest that still smells faintly of the storage shed, and a radio I was quietly terrified of dropping the entire day. My junction was the left turn off the main road onto the lane by the old mill, the one everyone agreed at the briefing was the trickiest spot on the whole route because of the blind bend and the Saturday farmers market traffic. Kofi was posted with me for the first two hours before rotating to the water stop, which I was grateful for since I'd never actually stood in a road stopping traffic before and having someone beside me who clearly had made the whole thing feel far less alarming than I'd been dreading since signing up weeks ago, half-convinced I'd talk myself out of it entirely.

## The Actual Job

The riders came through in three waves, roughly forty minutes apart, and between waves the junction went from completely quiet to genuinely frantic within about thirty seconds of the lead rider appearing around the bend, which caught me off guard the first time even though I'd been warned about exactly that. Stepping into the lane with the stop sign felt far more intimidating than I expected, standing there watching a car actually slow down and wait, trusting a vest and a bit of laminated cardboard more than it probably should. A delivery van driver in wave two was clearly unhappy about the delay and said so loudly through his window, which rattled me for a minute until Kofi, back for a quick radio check between rotations, reminded me over the handset that a grumpy driver waiting ninety seconds is not actually an emergency, just a mildly annoyed person, and that stuck with me for the rest of the day more than I expected it to. Between waves I radioed in rider counts to the coordinator at the finish, a habit I fumbled twice before getting the format right, numbers first then any incidents, nothing else, exactly as covered at the briefing. By the third wave I'd stopped feeling like I was performing the role and started actually just doing it, which is apparently how everyone says it goes the first time, and which I didn't quite believe until it happened to me around hour three standing in that same spot.

## One Close Call

A cyclist in the second wave came round the bend faster than the pack average, clearly trying to make up time after a mechanical earlier on, and nearly didn't see my stop sign in time before a car was already pulling out from the mill's car park; I stepped further into the road than I probably should have and waved both arms, and it resolved fine, but my hands were shaking for a solid ten minutes afterward in a way the training session never quite prepared me for.

## Why I'd Do It Again

Tired doesn't begin to cover how I feel writing this at nine at night, feet aching in a way that has nothing to do with actual cycling for once, but there's something genuinely satisfying about a day spent making sure two hundred and something riders got through a dangerous junction safely rather than riding through it myself, chasing my own numbers on a route I already know reasonably well. It's a completely different kind of tired than a hard ride produces, less about legs and lungs and much more about the low hum of sustained attention, watching the same bend for six hours and never quite letting your focus drop even in the quiet stretches between waves when nothing at all is happening. I understand now, in a way the marshal briefing meeting only partly got across, why the same names keep showing up on the volunteer list year after year; there's a kind of quiet pride in it that's genuinely different from a good ride result, closer to the feeling after a long shift of honest, useful work than anything resembling a personal athletic achievement, and I hadn't expected that distinction to land as clearly as it did standing at that junction all afternoon. Talking to Kofi at the pub afterward, he mentioned he's marshaled probably thirty events over the years and still gets a version of the same nervous energy at every single briefing, which made me feel considerably less ridiculous about how anxious I'd been about the whole thing beforehand, given how experienced he clearly is at this by now. I signed up for the autumn hill climb's marshal rota before I'd even finished my pint, which surprised me more than anyone else at the table, Priyam included, who has apparently been trying to get me to marshal something for over a year now without success until today, and who now insists on taking full credit for finally wearing me down.

## Feet Up

Legs are somehow more tired than after most rides I've done this year, which nobody warned me about, and I suspect I'll be thinking about that junction, and that one close call, for a while yet. Writing it down now, before sleep blurs the details, mostly so next year's version of me remembers how quickly the nerves passed once the first wave came round the bend.
