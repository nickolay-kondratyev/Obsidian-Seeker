June 14. Legs are still heavy but I wanted to write this down before the details blur, the way long rides always do a week later when you can barely remember which climb was which. Today was the club century, 100 miles from the clubhouse out past the reservoir, up through the two long climbs on the ridge road, and back along the river valley for the last thirty miles, which is flat but exposed and always feels longer than it should once the wind picks up in the afternoon. I signed up for this back in February on a whim, mostly because Priyam kept posting about the training plan in the group chat and I didn't want to be the only one who bailed. Training went in fits and starts through spring, plenty of missed weekday rides when work got busy, but the long Saturday rides mostly held together, and by May I'd done three rides over sixty miles which felt like enough of a base not to embarrass myself. Woke up at half four, which is an hour I otherwise never see, ate two pieces of toast with too much peanut butter, and was at the clubhouse by six for the six-thirty rollout. The parking lot was already full of nervous energy, people double-checking tyre pressure and rummaging through saddle bags for gels they'd definitely already checked twice. Kofi had brought a box of bananas for anyone who forgot food, which several people clearly had.

## The Ride

The first twenty miles felt easy, almost suspiciously so, rolling out in a loose group of maybe thirty riders with the morning still cool and the roads quiet. We hit the first climb around mile twenty-five and the group splintered immediately, with the stronger climbers gapping off the front within the first half mile of the gradient. I settled into a rhythm with a small group including Lotte and a rider from another club I didn't recognise, and we traded pulls up the switchbacks, which made the climb feel more like a shared project than a solo grind. The descent on the far side was fast and a little sketchy where gravel had washed onto the road from recent rain, and I rode the brakes more than I'd have liked, losing contact with the group ahead. Rode alone for a stretch after that, which was fine, actually nice, just me and the road and the tyres, until I caught back on at the second rest stop around mile fifty. That stop is where the day nearly went sideways: my right knee had started aching on the second climb, a dull ache on the inside that I recognised from a bike fit issue two summers ago, and for about ten minutes I genuinely considered calling for the sag wagon. Ate a banana, stretched against a picnic table, adjusted my cleat a half millimetre with the tool in my saddle bag, and decided to give it another ten miles before deciding anything. The knee never fully went away but it also never got worse, which in hindsight was the deciding factor. By mile seventy I'd caught up with Tomasz, who'd flatted twice and was in a foul mood about it, and we rode the river valley section together into a headwind that made the last thirty miles feel every bit as long as I'd feared. Rolled back into the clubhouse parking lot just after four in the afternoon, nine and a half hours after leaving, to a small cluster of people who'd already finished and were eating pizza someone had ordered. Dana was there with a cooler of cold drinks, which felt like a genuinely great thing.

## How It Felt

Honestly, the last thirty miles were rough in a way that no amount of training fully prepares you for, that specific flavour of tired where your hands go numb on the bars and you start doing maths about how many more pedal strokes are left before the parking lot appears. But there was also a stretch around mile eighty, just after the headwind briefly dropped, where everything clicked and I felt like I could ride forever, which is probably the closest I've come to understanding why people sign up for these things over and over again every single season. The knee held up well enough that I don't think it's anything serious, though I'll still mention it to the physio Priyam recommended just in case it flares up again on the next long ride. Mostly I just feel quietly proud of finishing something I genuinely wasn't sure I could finish back in February, when the whole idea still felt like a stretch.

## Notes For Next Time

A few things worth remembering before I forget them entirely: bring more than two gels for anything over eighty miles, the peanut butter toast breakfast was a mistake and sat heavy for the first hour, and the cleat adjustment trick at the rest stop is worth trying earlier next time rather than waiting until the knee is already complaining loudly about it. Also worth flagging for [[096-annual-volunteer-coordination-meeting]] that the mile-fifty rest stop badly needs a second table, since the queue for water backed up onto the road shoulder at one point, which felt like an actual safety issue rather than just a minor inconvenience for tired riders.
