# Reading Notes: Voss, *Concrete and Consent*

Argues curb cuts are the binding constraint on pedestrian access, not shelters or benches. p. 12.

## Core claim

Accessibility audits over-weight amenities and under-weight the actual path of travel to a stop. p. 44.

## Method

Voss surveyed 200 intersections across three cities, scoring crossing distance, curb ramps, surface condition, and signal timing against a five-point rubric. p. 61.

## Curb cuts

"A missing curb cut is a closed door, not an inconvenience." p. 67.

## My reaction

This lines up with what riders told me at Linden Ave — nobody mentioned the shelter, several mentioned the curb unprompted, without me asking about it directly. Strong support for Q3, worth citing in the methods chapter. p. 89.

## Weak spot

Sample skews toward downtown intersections; Voss admits residential corridors are undertested, which is exactly where my two study corridors sit. p. 103.

## Follow-up

Check whether Adeyemi's transit-equity work cites Voss or contradicts the amenity framing. p. 118.
