How to refinish the salvaged upper cabinets we're keeping from the old kitchen, since not everything needed replacing, just a refresh for the new sage and brass scheme.

## Tools Needed

Orbital sander, 120 and 220 grit sandpaper, tack cloth, mineral spirits, bonding primer, a paint sprayer, painter's tape for the surrounding trim we're not touching this round, and a box of wood filler for the relocated hardware holes.

## Step 1: Remove and Label

Take doors off first, numbering each on the hinge edge with tape.

## Step 2: Strip, Sand, and Fill

Wipe every surface with mineral spirits first to cut through decades of kitchen grease sanding alone won't remove. Sand with 120 grit, then 220 for a smooth surface primer will grip. Wipe with tack cloth between grits, since leftover dust shows through paint as texture. Fill old hardware holes with wood filler, sanded flush once cured.

## Step 3: Prime and Paint

Two thin sprayed coats of bonding primer, light sand between coats, then the same matte sage as the new cabinets in thin, even coats.

## Step 4: Reinstall Hardware and Doors

Once cured, about a week given the humidity, reinstall the brushed brass hardware in the relocated holes, then rehang each numbered door on its original hinge location so reveals stay consistent across old and new cabinets side by side.

## Step 5: Final Check

Stand back and check for drips or uneven sheen under good, direct light.

## Lessons Learned

Curing time matters more than the can label suggests; I rehung one door after four days instead of a full week and left a fingerprint near the handle that I had to sand out and respray, losing a weekend to a mistake avoidable just by waiting. The other lesson: matching a sprayed finish to factory cabinets is genuinely hard without a proper spray booth, and ours came close but not perfectly identical under afternoon light, a gap I've made peace with since nobody but me notices it.
