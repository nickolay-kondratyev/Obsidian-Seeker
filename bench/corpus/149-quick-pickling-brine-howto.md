Quick refrigerator pickles are ready in a day, unlike the true ferments in [[130-slow-fermentation-philosophy-article]], and need only a jar, vinegar, and whatever's crowding the crisper drawer.

## What You'll Need

A clean jar with a tight lid, white vinegar or rice vinegar, sugar, salt, and whatever firm vegetables you've got on hand: cucumbers, radishes, carrots, or green beans all work, and mixing two or three together often turns out better than any one alone.

## Step 1: Slice the Vegetables

Cut into even spears or coins so everything pickles at roughly the same rate.

## Step 2: Make the Brine

Combine equal parts vinegar and water with two tablespoons sugar and one tablespoon salt per cup of liquid, then bring just to a simmer until the sugar and salt dissolve. Add any aromatics now: garlic, mustard seed, a dried chili, or peppercorns, all of which infuse fastest while the brine is still hot.

## Step 3: Pack the Jar

Pack vegetables in tightly, standing spears upright where possible, leaving about half an inch of headspace at the top so the brine has somewhere to settle.

## Step 4: Pour and Cool

Pour the hot brine over the vegetables until fully submerged, tap the jar to release air bubbles, then cool uncovered on the counter for about twenty minutes before sealing the lid and moving the jar to the refrigerator.

## Step 5: Wait

Give it at least four hours before tasting, though overnight is noticeably better.

## Troubleshooting

Refrigerator pickles keep about a month sealed, best in the first two weeks before the vegetables soften. If the pickles taste flat, the brine likely needed more salt; give it another day before adjusting. Mushy pickles usually mean the vegetables were cut too thin, or the brine was poured on while still boiling rather than simmering. If the jar smells off rather than sharply vinegary, discard it; a bad batch is rare but not worth risking, especially with garlic, which occasionally grows surface mold if any clove sits above the brine line.
