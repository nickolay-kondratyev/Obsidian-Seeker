## Why automate

Automatic transfers remove the willpower step. If the money moves before you see it in checking, there's no monthly decision to make, no moment of temptation on a tight week.

## What you need first

A checking account for spending, one or more savings or brokerage destinations, and a clear number for each transfer, decided during a proper [[211-budget-review-sync]] rather than picked casually on the spot without checking it against the actual budget first.

## Pick the day

Schedule transfers for the day after payday clears, never the day of.

## Set the transfers

Log into each destination account directly rather than pushing from checking, since pull transfers from savings apps tend to clear faster and fail less often than push transfers initiated from the checking side. Set the exact dollar amount, the recurring cadence, and confirm the first transfer date lands after your next paycheck actually clears, not before.

## Split by goal

Route separate transfers to separate accounts: emergency fund, debt extra-payment, brokerage, each its own line rather than one lump sum you split manually later, which tends to quietly stop.

## Buffer the checking account

Leave a cushion of at least one week's expenses in checking so a transfer never bounces if a bill lands early, since an overdraft fee wipes out weeks of the interest you're trying to earn by saving in the first place.

## Review quarterly

Amounts should shift as income or goals change, not stay frozen forever.

## Common mistakes

Setting the transfer date too close to payday is the most common failure, since a late paycheck deposit or a payroll holiday shift can bounce the transfer and trigger an overdraft fee that undoes the whole point of automating in the first place. Another mistake is automating an amount that's genuinely too aggressive at first: start slightly conservative, confirm it holds for two full months without stress, then increase it gradually rather than setting an ambitious number on day one and immediately needing to walk it back after an uncomfortable month.
