Started a new starter today from scratch instead of asking around the ferment club for a cutting, mostly because I wanted to actually understand the process instead of inheriting someone else's decade-old culture and treating it like a black box. Equal parts whole rye flour and filtered water, fifty grams each, into a clean jar with a loose lid. The kitchen sits around 70F this time of year, a little cool for fast activity, but I'd rather have a slow honest start than force it with a heat mat right away. No bubbles yet, which is expected on day one, and the smell is just wet flour, nothing sour or unpleasant. I labeled the jar with today's date and a rubber band around the current level so I can actually see if it rises instead of guessing. Priyam warned me rye starters can be more aggressive early on than the all-purpose ones she's used, so I'm curious whether that holds. The plan is to feed once a day for now, same ratio, and switch to twice a day once I see real activity — doubling in volume, not just a few stray bubbles on the surface. I'm also going to resist the urge to smell it obsessively every hour, which is what I did with the last attempt two years ago before giving up during a busy month. This time I want a log, not vibes, so every entry below gets a rise measurement against the rubber band and a smell note however unscientific that sounds. If this one survives past the two-week mark I'll consider naming it, though Kofi's insistence that unnamed starters die faster is obviously superstition.

## Day 4

First real signs of life this morning — the level had climbed maybe 20% overnight and there are visible small bubbles scattered through the jar, not just at the surface. Smell has shifted from plain wet flour to something faintly sweet, almost like yogurt, which matches what I'd read to expect around this stage. I fed it again at the same 1:1:1 ratio by weight, rye flour and water each fifty grams, and moved the jar to the top of the fridge where it's a couple degrees warmer than the counter. By evening the rise had mostly deflated back down, which apparently means the yeast population ate through the available food faster than expected, a decent sign even if the smell had gone slightly more acidic and less pleasant. I switched to twice-daily feeding starting tonight since the deflation suggests it can't wait a full 24 hours yet. Wrote all this down because I already forgot exactly when I fed it yesterday and had to reconstruct the timeline from memory, which is precisely the failure mode I'm trying to avoid this time around. Also noticed a thin layer of liquid pooling on top before this evening's feed — hooch, from what I understand, a sign it was hungry rather than a sign anything had gone wrong. Stirred it back in rather than pouring it off, since I'd read conflicting advice about which is better and figured incorporating it couldn't hurt this early on. Tomorrow I want to try the float test for the first time even though I expect it's too early for a reliable result, just to establish a baseline for comparison later in the week when it should actually matter. Priyam texted asking how it was going and I sent her a photo of the bubbles, which she said looked promising for day four, better than her own first rye attempt at the same point. The rubber band trick is working better than expected; I can see at a glance that the level roughly doubled between this morning's feed and now, even without a ruler.

## Day 9

Passing the float test consistently now, a small spoonful dropped in water sits on the surface instead of sinking, and the rise between feeds has become dramatic enough that I switched to a taller jar to stop it climbing over the rim. Smell is properly sour today, closer to plain yogurt gone slightly further, with none of the unpleasant sharp acetone note that apparently signals an unhappy or underfed starter. I did one feed today instead of two since the taller jar buys more headroom and the rise-then-fall cycle seems to be settling into roughly a twelve-hour rhythm. Considering a test bake this weekend even though most guides suggest waiting closer to two weeks for full strength; worst case it's a dense loaf and a data point rather than a wasted effort. Logged the current hydration and feeding schedule in case I want to compare against a second starter later, maybe an all-purpose flour one to see whether Priyam's claim about rye being faster actually holds up under a controlled side-by-side.

## Day 14

Two weeks in and it's genuinely reliable — doubles in about five hours at room temperature, passes the float test every time I check, and the smell has settled into a consistent pleasant tang rather than swinging between stages. Did the test bake I'd been putting off, a simple boule with no other additions, and while the crumb was tighter than I'd like it rose properly and the crust colored well, which I'm counting as a real success for a starter this young. Naming it now, against my earlier skepticism about Kofi's superstition, mostly because typing "the starter" in every entry has gotten tedious. Going with Gustav, no particular reason beyond it sounding sturdy. Next entry will track the switch to a maintenance schedule now that the intensive daily logging phase feels complete.
