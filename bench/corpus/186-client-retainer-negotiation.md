Meeting with Lotte, marketing lead for one of the two homeware clients who together account for close to a quarter of last year's revenue, about converting their ad-hoc booking pattern into a retainer arrangement for the coming year. Held over video call, 45 minutes, Tomasz from their side joined for the second half to weigh in on budget approval. Purpose was to explore whether a retainer made sense for both sides given how much back-and-forth scheduling has cost everyone over the past eighteen months, and if so, to sketch rough terms before drafting anything formal. Nothing was signed today, this was explicitly a scoping conversation, but the tone was positive enough that I expect a follow-up with a real proposal within two weeks.

## Background

Lotte's team has booked with me eleven times over the past year, averaging just under one shoot per month but with a lot of clustering around seasonal catalog drops, meaning some months had three bookings and others had none. Every booking so far has gone through the same ad-hoc quoting process: brief, quote, approval, schedule, which Lotte estimates costs her team close to a week of lead time each cycle because approvals have to go through Tomasz's budget sign-off individually every time. She raised the retainer idea herself, unprompted, in an email last month, saying the finance team would actually prefer a predictable monthly line item over the current pattern of surprise invoices that make quarterly budgeting harder than it needs to be. I'd been quietly hoping for this kind of conversation for a while, since the scheduling churn on my end has been real too, several ad-hoc requests have come in with only four or five days' notice, which is tighter than I'd like for anything beyond the simplest catalog work. Looking back through last year's invoices before the call, I counted three months where I had to turn down other inquiries because a last-minute homeware booking landed on top of an already-full week, and at least one of those turned-away inquiries never came back, which is exactly the kind of quiet revenue loss a retainer could prevent if the scheduling gets more predictable on both sides. I also went in with a rough sense of what day rate floor I'd accept, based on the effective hourly rate across last year's eleven bookings once editing time was factored in, so I wasn't negotiating from a cold start.

## Discussion

We started by walking through last year's booking pattern together, matching it against a rough monthly average, and landed on a proposed retainer of three shoot days per month, with unused days rolling over for one additional month before expiring rather than vanishing entirely, which addressed Lotte's worry about paying for capacity that goes unused during slow seasonal months. Tomasz joined partway through and asked the harder questions I'd expected: what happens if they need a fourth day in a busy month, what the cancellation notice period looks like, and whether the retainer rate would be lower than my current ad-hoc day rate given the volume commitment. On the fourth-day question, I proposed an overage rate at ten percent above the retainer's effective day rate, which felt fair to both of us since it's still a discount off my standalone ad-hoc rate but preserves some incentive for them to plan ahead rather than always defaulting to overage. Cancellation terms took the longest to work through; we settled on a rough framework of five business days' notice for a shoot to be rescheduled without penalty, with anything cancelled inside that window still counting against the month's allotted days, since I can't always backfill a slot that late and the studio time is effectively lost either way. On rate, I held firm that the retainer rate would sit modestly below ad-hoc, not dramatically below, since the value to me isn't just volume, it's the reduced quoting overhead and better forecasting, which is real but not worth a steep discount. Tomasz pushed back gently on that point but didn't push hard, and Lotte visibly seemed relieved the conversation wasn't turning adversarial, she mentioned afterward that a previous vendor relationship had gotten tense over exactly this kind of rate negotiation and she was glad this one hadn't gone that way. We also touched briefly on scope, confirming the retainer covers studio product photography specifically and wouldn't extend automatically to any on-location work, which would still be quoted separately if it comes up, a distinction Tomasz wanted explicitly written into whatever contract eventually gets drafted.

## Open Questions

A few things are still unresolved and need answers before I draft anything formal. First, whether the retainer term should be a rolling twelve months or tied to a fixed calendar year with a renewal checkpoint, Lotte leans toward rolling for flexibility but wants to check with finance. Second, whether editing turnaround time should be written into the retainer terms explicitly or left as an informal expectation the way it's always been, given that formalizing it might actually work against me if a particularly complex batch needs more time than a generic clause would allow. Third, I still need to decide internally whether three retainer days per month is sustainable alongside my other clients without squeezing out the higher-margin one-off jewelry work that I've been trying to protect more of my calendar for, see [[183-year-two-reflections]] for the broader thinking behind that shift. I want to sit with the calendar math for a few days before committing to a number in writing, rather than agreeing to something in the room that I regret once I actually block it out against next year's projected bookings. Fourth, I haven't decided whether to offer this same structure to the other homeware client, since offering it to one and not the other could read as favoritism if word gets around between their teams, which happens more than either company realizes given how small this industry corner is. Fifth, I want to think through the concentration risk if the three allotted days end up being the bulk of a slow month's income, since I don't yet have a clean answer for how much of my calendar I'm comfortable committing to one client relationship.
