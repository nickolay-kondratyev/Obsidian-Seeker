We also wanted to leave enough time at the end to set a date for the next check-in rather than letting it slide the way the last one nearly did, since we'd gone almost seven weeks past the planned interval before finally sitting down together.

## Allocation review

We pulled the current allocation against the target we set back in January and it had drifted more than either of us expected, mostly because the equity portion outperformed the bond sleeve by a wide margin over the last two quarters while we weren't paying close attention. Target was 75/20/5 across equities, bonds, and cash; actual sits closer to 80/16/4 right now, which doesn't sound dramatic until you realize it represents thousands of dollars that have quietly shifted risk profile without either of us deciding that on purpose. Priyam's take, before she dropped off the call, was that an eight-percentage-point drift is right at the edge of "ignore it" and "act on it," and that if we're going to rebalance we should do it through new contributions rather than selling anything, to avoid triggering capital gains in the taxable account unnecessarily. Dana agreed that felt like the lower-friction option, and pointed out it also avoids the psychological friction of selling winners, which she finds harder than it should be. We didn't rebalance today, just flagged it clearly and agreed to route the next two months of contributions disproportionately into bonds until the ratio closes back toward target, which should take roughly that long given our current monthly contribution size and the current gap between actual and target weights. We also briefly discussed whether the target itself still made sense given how far out retirement still is, and agreed to revisit the target allocation formally at the annual planning meeting rather than adjusting it ad hoc tonight.

## Barbell strategy discussion

Spent the bulk of the meeting on whether to adopt the barbell approach — very safe assets on one end, higher-risk higher-growth assets on the other, with comparatively little sitting in the conservative middle — instead of our current fairly conventional index-fund-heavy setup that treats the whole portfolio as one smooth risk gradient. Dana's initial reaction was skeptical, mostly because it means holding a noticeably larger cash and short-term bond position than either of us is used to, and that feels like "wasted" money sitting there earning less than it could elsewhere. I pushed back a little, since the whole point of the barbell is that the safe end isn't meant to grow much at all, it's meant to fund near-term spending and absorb shocks so the risky end never has to be sold at a bad time just to cover an unexpected expense. We went back and forth on this for a while without really landing anywhere, partly because neither of us has actually finished reading the source material carefully enough to argue from specifics rather than vague impressions of what a barbell portfolio is supposed to do. We didn't reach a decision today. Agreed to each read through [[220-barbell-investing-strategy]] properly before the next check-in rather than continuing to debate it from memory and half-remembered podcast summaries, and to each bring one concrete objection or one concrete argument in favor, not just a general feeling either way about risk. Also agreed that if we do end up adopting it, it would only apply to the portion of the portfolio earmarked for the first five years after quitting work, not the whole thing, since that's where sequence risk actually matters most and where a bad early market year could do the most lasting damage to the plan. Dana raised one more point right before Priyam dropped off: that a partial barbell might be easier to sell to her emotionally than an all-or-nothing switch, since it lets her keep most of the portfolio in the familiar setup while testing the idea on a smaller, lower-stakes slice first.
