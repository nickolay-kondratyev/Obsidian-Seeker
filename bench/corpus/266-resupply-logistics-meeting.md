Resupply logistics call for the Sierra High Route scouting phase, focused on the first off-trail practice weekend rather than the full route itself yet. Attendees: Dana, Priyam, Kofi. Goal was to nail down the practice weekend's food plan and confirm the shuttle situation for a point-to-point route rather than our usual loop or out-and-back trips. This is also the first time we're testing the dehydrated recipes from Priyam's cookbook research before committing to them on a real multi-day trip. We're treating this weekend as one of the three off-trail practice trips from the Sierra High Route project plan, so getting the logistics right matters more than it would for a purely casual weekend outing.

## Food Plan

Food plan for the weekend: three of Priyam's new dehydrated recipes from [[265-reading-notes-backcountry-kitchen]], tested at actual trail conditions rather than just at home on the stove where everything works fine regardless. Kofi is skeptical about the trail chili specifically, given Priyam's own notes about it taking nearly double the advertised rehydration time, so we're bringing a backup freeze-dried meal just in case it doesn't work out on trail. Water resupply for this particular route is straightforward, several reliable creek crossings marked on the map, so we're not carrying more than a liter and a half at any point between refills. Shuttle situation is more complicated than usual since this is a point-to-point route rather than a loop; Dana is coordinating a car spot with a friend who hikes the area regularly and has offered to help for gas money. We also talked through what gear from our usual kit doesn't apply to this kind of off-trail practice weekend, mostly deciding to leave the bear canister at home since this particular area doesn't require one this time of year. Priyam is also bringing a small backup stove in case the alcohol stove she wants to test for weight savings turns out to be too finicky in whatever wind we get, since testing two systems back to back seemed smarter than committing blind. Snacks and lunch stay the same as our usual trips, no experimentation there, since this weekend already has enough new variables between the navigation practice and the untested dinners.

## Navigation Practice

Bigger discussion was about treating this weekend as a genuine test rather than just a fun trip, meaning we're deliberately building in navigation challenges instead of just following the easiest visible route the whole time. Kofi proposed picking three waypoints in advance and navigating between them purely by map and compass, no GPS allowed except as an emergency backup, which is exactly the kind of skill practice the Sierra High Route project actually needs from us. Priyam raised a fair concern about safety margin, given this is intentionally harder navigation than we're used to, so we agreed on a hard turnaround time and a clear bailout route back to the car if anyone gets uncomfortable with how the day is going. We also discussed splitting into pairs for part of the day to practice independent navigation decisions rather than always defaulting to whoever's most confident in the group, since the real route will eventually require everyone to trust their own judgment at some point. Dana suggested keeping a shared notebook of what went wrong during navigation, wrong turns, misread contours, anything, so we have concrete examples to review afterward instead of just a vague sense of how the day went overall. Kofi is picking waypoints that are genuinely tricky but forgiving, meaning a wrong turn costs us time rather than putting anyone in real danger, which feels like the right level of difficulty for a first attempt at this kind of practice. We agreed to debrief immediately after finishing each leg between waypoints rather than waiting until the end of the day, since specific memories of a decision point fade fast once you've moved on to the next stretch of terrain. Priyam raised one more idea worth trying: after the trip, redrawing our actual path from memory on a blank map before checking it against the GPS track, a rough test of how well we internalized the terrain rather than just followed a device. Kofi liked this a lot and suggested making it a standard practice for every future off-trail weekend, not just this first one, since it's a cheap way to measure real progress on the skill we're actually trying to build here.

## Decisions

Decisions: three dehydrated meals from the cookbook plus one backup freeze-dried dinner, no bear canister needed for this specific area, and Dana's friend is confirmed for the car shuttle in exchange for gas money. Navigation practice is on: three waypoints picked by Kofi, pairs splitting for part of the day, hard turnaround time of two in the afternoon regardless of progress made toward the final waypoint. Shared notebook idea from Dana is adopted; she's bringing a small waterproof notepad specifically for logging navigation mistakes as they happen rather than trying to reconstruct them from memory afterward at camp. Debrief happens after each leg between waypoints, not just once at the end of the day, so lessons stay fresh and specific rather than blurring together into vague general impressions. Priyam's backup stove and the freeze-dried backup dinner both get packed regardless of how confident we feel about the new gear, since redundancy costs little and buys real peace of mind on a weekend that's already testing several new things at once. Adopting Priyam's blank-map redraw idea as a standard practice for every future off-trail weekend, starting with this one, as a cheap way to track real navigation progress over time rather than just relying on gut feeling. Dana is bringing the shared notebook and will type up the notes afterward so we have a running record across all our practice weekends instead of scattered impressions that fade within a week or two.
