# Tidewatch Rulebook Review Meeting — 2026-02-10

Dana, Kofi, and Priyam met for ninety minutes to do a full read-through of the current rulebook draft ahead of sending it to layout. This was the first time the whole document had been reviewed line by line since the storm mechanic rewrite, and the goal was to catch inconsistencies between what the cards say and what the rulebook says before anything gets locked for print. Tomasz joined for the last twenty minutes to flag print-format constraints. Lotte was unable to attend but sent written notes in advance about page count limits tied to the current budget, which Dana read aloud near the start so the group could keep them in mind while deciding how much detail to keep versus cut from each section.

## Sequencing Fixes

The biggest item was the storm surge sequencing ambiguity that surfaced during the near-loss playtest session Dana wrote up afterward, described in [[283-tidewatch-journal-near-loss]] — surge resolves before the tide marker advances, and the current draft doesn't say this explicitly anywhere, leaving players to infer an order that two different tables inferred two different ways during the same week of sessions. Kofi rewrote the relevant paragraph on the spot, reading it aloud twice to confirm it couldn't be misread even by someone skimming quickly under time pressure mid-game, and the group agreed the storm phase overview diagram needs a small numbered-arrow addition showing the surge-then-tide order visually, not just in text, since several playtesters have said they skim diagrams before reading paragraphs anyway and rarely go back to reread once a session is underway. Priyam volunteered to produce the updated diagram by the end of the week rather than waiting for the next full illustration pass, since it's a small enough fix to slot in around her current token artwork without disrupting the larger schedule. Kofi also asked whether the same ambiguity might exist elsewhere in the storm phase text, and the group agreed to do a targeted second pass specifically hunting for other unstated orderings before the document goes to layout, rather than assuming this was an isolated case that fixing once fully resolves. Dana pointed out that the supply phase has a similar structure to the storm phase and could hide the same class of problem, so Kofi added it to his list for the second pass alongside the storm text already flagged today.

## Structural Reorganization

Kofi has believed for a while that the rulebook's current order — setup, then turn structure, then win and loss conditions, then a components appendix — buries the most-referenced material too deep for a game people consult mid-session, and today's discussion confirmed it more concretely than before. Looking at three sessions' worth of blind playtest notes together, the group noticed that the storm and supply symbol glossary gets flipped to constantly, yet it currently sits on page eleven, after several pages most players never revisit once a session starts. Dana proposed moving a condensed symbol reference to the inside back cover instead, reachable in one flip regardless of where a reader currently has the book open, with the full glossary staying in its existing spot for people who want the longer explanations. Kofi agreed this was worth doing even though it adds a small amount of page count, since [[289-how-to-write-rulebook-faq]] already assumes players will flip to a glossary repeatedly and a slow, deeply buried glossary undermines that whole approach regardless of how clearly each individual entry is written. Priyam noted this changes her inside-cover layout plans and asked for the final symbol list locked before she starts that spread, since reworking finished art costs real time she'd rather spend on the token redesign already in progress this month. The group also discussed whether turn structure should move ahead of setup, since several blind playtesters have said they'd rather understand what a turn looks like before memorizing the setup steps in detail, but Dana pushed back, arguing that setup errors are much harder to recover from mid-session than turn-structure confusion, which usually resolves itself within a round or two once players start playing. Kofi proposed a middle path: keep setup first, but add a short "what a turn looks like" summary box right before the detailed setup steps, giving players a mental model before they start assembling the board, tokens, and decks. Everyone liked this compromise better than either original position, and Priyam agreed it fits naturally into the existing page layout without requiring a full restructure of the surrounding pages, which was the main worry going into the discussion.

## Page Count Tension

Lotte's note flagged that the current draft is running four pages over the budgeted count, and every fix discussed today — the sequencing diagram, the condensed glossary spread — adds rather than removes pages, which nobody fully reconciled during the meeting itself. Dana pushed back gently on cutting the diagram to save space, arguing that a confusing rulebook costs more in lost trust than four pages costs in print budget, referencing the near-loss playtest as evidence that ambiguity has a real downstream cost beyond the page itself. Kofi suggested the setup section could be tightened by pulling repeated reminders that appear in both the setup steps and the components appendix, since duplicating that material was more useful in an earlier draft when the two sections were written independently but now reads as padding once placed side by side. Tomasz, joining near the end, confirmed the print vendor can absorb one or two extra pages without changing the binding cost bracket, which took some of the urgency out of the discussion but didn't fully resolve it, since going further over budget than that would push into a more expensive bracket nobody wants to enter this late in the process. He also mentioned that saddle-stitch binding, still under discussion for the manufacturing plan, has a hard page-count ceiling of its own regardless of budget, and the group should confirm that number before locking any content that assumes room to spare. Dana asked Tomasz to get that exact ceiling from the vendor before the next meeting so the group isn't planning against a guess.
