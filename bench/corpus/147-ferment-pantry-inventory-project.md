Building a simple inventory system for the pantry and ferment shelves, since I've genuinely lost track of what's fermenting, what's canned, and what's simply expired at this point, buried behind newer jars.

## The problem

Found a jar of pickles three years past its label date last week, pushed to the back of the shelf and forgotten entirely. That's the third expired surprise this year alone, and clearly memory isn't a reliable inventory system anymore given how the shelves have grown.

## Approach

A simple spreadsheet, one row per jar batch: contents, date made, expected ready date if fermenting, and shelf location. Nothing fancier than that, since past attempts at elaborate systems always died from being too much effort to actually maintain week over week, and a system nobody updates is worse than no system at all.

## What gets tracked

Active ferments in progress, finished jars in the pantry, and anything in the root cellar setup from [[127-root-cellar-storage-project]]. Not tracking single-use items like fresh produce, only anything jarred or crocked that could realistically sit around long enough to be forgotten.

## Update habit

Adding a new row the moment anything gets started or finished, rather than batching entries later from memory, which is exactly the habit that failed with every previous attempt at tracking this kind of thing consistently.

## Shelf labeling

Physical jars get a small numbered sticker matching the spreadsheet row, so cross-referencing what's actually on the shelf against the spreadsheet takes seconds rather than requiring memory or guesswork about which jar is which, especially for jars that look nearly identical from the outside once labels fade or peel off over time.

## Review schedule

Monthly review, checking for anything past its ready date or nearing an expiration window, flagged in a separate column rather than deleted, so there's a record of what actually got used versus what quietly expired unused on the shelf. This monthly check is also when older, fully consumed rows finally get archived to a second sheet, keeping the active tracking sheet from growing cluttered with jars that no longer physically exist anywhere in the house.

## First cleanup

Before the system even starts, doing one full pantry audit to catch existing expired jars and establish an accurate starting inventory.
