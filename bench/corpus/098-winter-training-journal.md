January 9. Started the structured winter plan today after putting it off for two weeks, mostly out of dread for the turbo trainer sessions rather than any real obstacle. The plan, cribbed loosely from *Legs, Lungs, and Long Roads* and adjusted with a spreadsheet Priyam shared in the group chat, has three easy rides and two structured interval sessions a week through February, building toward the spring century. First session tonight was forty-five minutes on the turbo in the garage, which is cold enough that I could see my breath for the first ten minutes before the effort warmed things up. Did four sets of five-minute intervals at what the plan calls threshold effort, with three minutes easy between each. Legs felt heavier than expected for what should still be early-season fitness, but the numbers on the head unit were roughly where they were at the same point last winter, which is reassuring even if it genuinely didn't feel that way sitting on the trainer in the cold.

## The Plan Itself

Writing this down mostly so I remember it in March when motivation dips, which it always does somewhere around week six. Monday and Wednesday are easy rides, genuinely easy, the kind where you could hold a conversation the whole way without getting short of breath, and I keep having to remind myself that riding slower than feels natural is the actual point rather than a failure of effort. Tuesday and Thursday are the structured sessions, alternating between the threshold intervals I did tonight and a longer set of shorter, harder efforts closer to what the book calls real intensity. Saturday is the long club ride, whatever pace that ends up being depending on who shows up, and Sunday is fully off the bike, which took some getting used to after years of just riding whenever the weather allowed regardless of a plan. The whole thing runs eight weeks, taking me through to early March, at which point I'll reassess based on how the legs feel rather than blindly following the spreadsheet into a plan that's stopped making sense. Kofi is doing a version of the same plan and we've agreed to compare notes every couple of weeks, mostly for accountability since neither of us is great at sticking to a turbo session alone in a cold garage with nobody watching and no crowd to keep either of us honest about the effort.

## Garage Setup

The garage setup needs work before this becomes a habit rather than a chore. No proper lighting beyond one bare bulb, the trainer wobbles slightly on the uneven concrete floor, and there's nowhere to prop a phone or tablet for something to watch during the easy portions. Small fixes but worth doing this weekend rather than letting a bad setup become an excuse to skip sessions once the novelty wears off sometime in February.

## Why Bother

Worth writing down why I'm doing this at all, since motivation always fades faster than intentions in January. Last year's century, documented in [[093-century-ride-journal]], went fine but the last thirty miles hurt more than they should have for someone who'd technically trained all spring, and I want this year to feel different, less like survival and more like the ride I actually planned for. There's also a smaller, more selfish reason: I want to be able to lead one of the harder club rides this year without quietly worrying I'll blow up on the second climb and hold the group up, which happened once two summers ago and still bothers me more than it probably should given nobody else seemed to think much of it at the time. Structured training feels like the honest way to close that gap rather than hoping fitness just shows up on its own the way it sometimes has in past years when the weather cooperated and I happened to ride more by accident than design. There's a financial angle too, if I'm honest: a turbo trainer isn't cheap, and using it properly through winter is the only way to justify having bought it last year instead of just leaving it under a dust sheet like it sat for most of last January and February. Writing the plan down here, rather than leaving it in a spreadsheet nobody but me will ever open again, feels like a small extra bit of accountability, even if it's really just me talking to myself about a decision I've already made and mostly just need to follow through on now that it's written somewhere I'll actually revisit. If nothing else, having it written down in the vault, dated, makes it harder to quietly let the plan slide the way I did two winters ago, when I told myself I'd start structured training and then simply never opened the spreadsheet again after the first week. There's something about a dated entry that feels more binding than a private intention, even though logically nobody is checking up on me and the only consequence of skipping a session is disappointing myself, quietly, in a garage nobody sees.
