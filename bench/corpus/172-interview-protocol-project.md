# Interview Protocol — Tenant and Planner Interviews

The purpose of this project is to design a semi-structured interview protocol that I can use with two distinct groups: tenants in rent-burdened households near the Elm Corridor, and municipal planners who worked on the 2023 zoning update. The protocol needs to hold up across both groups while letting me probe group-specific experiences without forcing a single script onto very different conversations. It feeds directly into [[157-literature-review-project]] and the coding scheme I'm building out in [[167-data-cleaning-project]], so getting the question wording right now saves a lot of re-coding later.

## Goals

The core research question is how displacement risk is perceived differently by the people experiencing it and the people whose policy decisions shape it. I want the tenant interviews to surface lived experience — rent trajectories, moves, informal coping strategies — without asking people to narrate trauma for its own sake. The planner interviews should surface the internal logic of decisions: what tradeoffs were discussed, what data existed at the time, what got dropped from drafts. A secondary goal is triangulating specific dates and building addresses mentioned in the archival record from [[155-reading-notes-curb-cuts]], to check whether planners remember the same sequence of events that the meeting minutes show. I'm deliberately not trying to build a representative sample; six to eight interviews per group is enough for saturation on the themes I already see forming, not for statistical claims.

## Question Guide

Both guides open with a warm-up question about how long the person has lived in or worked on the neighborhood, then move into a rough three-part structure: background, a critical-incident probe, and a forward-looking question.

For tenants: "Walk me through the last time your rent changed — what happened and what did you do." Then: "Has anyone in your building talked about moving? What would make that decision for you?" I'm keeping the language plain and avoiding planning jargon like "displacement" unless the tenant introduces it first.

For planners: "Tell me about a moment during the 2023 update where the discussion got difficult." Then: "What data were you missing that you wish you'd had?" I want to leave room for planners to name internal disagreements.

Both guides end with: "Is there something I should have asked but didn't?" That question has produced the best material in my two pilot interviews so far — one tenant brought up a landlord buyout offer I never would have thought to ask about directly.

## Recruitment Plan

Tenants: recruiting through the housing coalition contact from [[166-meeting-housing-coalition]] and a flyer at the community center, $25 gift card per interview. Planners: direct email using the staff directory, no incentive since it's within their professional role. Target is 6-8 per group, staggered through October so I have room to code between batches rather than transcribing everything at once.
