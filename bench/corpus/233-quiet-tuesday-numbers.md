I did the thing I keep telling myself I don't need to do anymore, which is open three different apps before breakfast to check whether the number changed overnight. It didn't, obviously, because markets don't move much on a random Tuesday, but the checking itself is the tell. Priyam calls this "portfolio peeking" and says it's basically the financial version of checking your phone for notifications that aren't there. She's not wrong. I used to think I'd grow out of it once the number got big enough to feel solid, but there is no number that feels solid, there's just a number that's slightly bigger than yesterday's, and a brain that wants to know if that's still true five minutes later.

What actually happened today: nothing. I made coffee, transferred four hundred dollars into the brokerage account on the usual schedule, and looked at a spreadsheet I've looked at roughly nine hundred times this year. It said the same thing it always says, which is that we are on pace, slowly, unglamorously, for something that doesn't have a firm date attached to it yet. Dana keeps asking me to pick a date, an actual month and year, instead of a range, because ranges let me avoid ever feeling done. She might be right. I've been treating "sometime around 2032" as a comfortable fog to hide in rather than a target to aim at, and today's checking ritual is proof I still haven't fixed that.

## Trying to name the actual number

After lunch I sat down and tried to do what Dana asked, which is turn the fog into a date. I opened the retirement spreadsheet and instead of looking at the current balance, I looked at the projected monthly spend column, the one I usually skim past because it makes the whole project feel smaller and less abstract. If we hold spending at roughly four thousand two hundred a month, including the health insurance premium we'd have to buy on our own, the portfolio needs to clear a specific number I've been avoiding writing down anywhere. I wrote it down today. It's higher than I expected, mostly because I'd been quietly excluding healthcare from my mental math for years, treating it as a rounding error instead of the second-largest line item after housing.

Once I had the number, I did the boring arithmetic: current balance, current contribution rate, a conservative growth assumption, and a spreadsheet formula that spits out a year. The year it spit out was 2033, not the vague "early thirties" I'd been carrying around in my head, but an actual calendar year with a month attached. Seeing it typed out in a cell felt different than saying it out loud to Dana ever has. It's less negotiable somehow, more like a commitment and less like a hope. I texted her a screenshot instead of explaining it, partly because I wasn't sure I could say it without my voice doing something embarrassing.

I also noticed, doing this exercise, how much the number moves if I change one assumption slightly — a percentage point of expected return, six months of extra cushion for the first years, whether we downsize the house the way we've talked about in passing. None of those are decided yet. But at least now there's a specific target I can push those decisions against instead of a mood. Even a rough sensitivity table, three rows scribbled in the margin, made the whole exercise feel less like guesswork and more like something I could actually plan around instead of just worry about in the shower.

## What I'm still avoiding

The part I didn't do today, and probably should, is actually sit down with Dana and go through the number together rather than sending it as a screenshot with a shrug emoji. I keep telling myself I'm protecting her from my anxiety about it, but I think what I'm actually doing is protecting myself from watching her reaction in real time. If she looks worried, I have to hold that in the moment instead of processing it alone first. If she looks relieved, I have to sit with the fact that I was carrying more dread than the situation warranted, which is its own uncomfortable thing to metabolize. Either way it's easier from behind a phone screen, which is a cowardly thing to admit in a private notebook nobody else reads, but there it is. I keep telling myself this weekend, over coffee, no laptop open, just the two of us and the number out loud. We've said that before and not followed through, so I'm writing it here partly to hold myself to it.

## Small good thing

Priyam texted back almost immediately after I mentioned the 2033 number in our group chat, just to say "that's a real date now, congrats," which sounds small typed out but landed better than I expected. It's strange how much weight a friend's casual confirmation can carry when you've spent a year being vague on purpose. She's a few years ahead of us on this path and has said before that naming the date was the moment the whole thing stopped feeling theoretical for her too, so maybe that's just how it goes — not a big dramatic turning point, just a Tuesday where the fog resolves into a number and somebody notices.
