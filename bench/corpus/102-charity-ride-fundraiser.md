# Charity Ride Fundraiser

Every September the club runs a charity ride raising money for the regional trauma unit, in memory of a member's accident a few years back that the club rallied around at the time. This year's target is £6,000, up from £4,200 last year, reflecting both growing club size and a more ambitious route through the hills rather than the flatter loop we've used the past two years. This document tracks the route, fundraising mechanics, and event day logistics as they come together over the summer.

## Route

The new route runs 80 kilometres from the clubhouse out past the reservoir and through the hill villages, roughly following the second half of [[092-gravel-loop-mapping-project]]'s scouting but staying on paved roads throughout, since we need this to be accessible to occasional riders raising money for a good cause rather than only club regulars. Three rest stops are planned, spaced roughly every 25 kilometres, each stocked with water, snacks, and a first-aid kit, staffed by volunteers coordinated through [[096-annual-volunteer-coordination-meeting]]'s new process. The route avoids Route 9 entirely given the mixed-ability field expected, adding about six kilometres compared to the most direct path but removing the one section of the ride that would worry both organisers and participants.

## Fundraising Mechanics

Riders register online through a simple form and commit to a minimum sponsorship of £50, collected through a fundraising page rather than cash on the day, which makes reconciliation far simpler than previous years when Dana spent most of October chasing envelopes of cash and cheques. The club covers insurance and route costs from membership funds so that effectively all sponsorship money reaches the trauma unit directly, which is a point worth emphasising in publicity since donors respond well to knowing overhead isn't eating into their contribution. A small number of club jerseys will be given as thank-you gifts to anyone raising over £150, funded separately from a modest print run budget agreed at committee rather than from the fundraising total itself. Last year's £4,200 came from sixty-one riders; this year's target of £6,000 assumes similar average sponsorship per rider but a larger field of around seventy-five, which feels achievable given how much earlier publicity started this time around compared with previous years' fundraising drives.

## Publicity

Posters going up at the three local bike shops by June, a page on the club website already live, and a push through the newsletter starting in July asking members to share the fundraising link widely with friends, family, and coworkers well beyond the club itself, since the trauma unit benefits most from reach outside our own membership.

## Event Day

Registration opens at 7am at the clubhouse, with a mass rollout at 8am split into two groups by expected pace to avoid the bottleneck that slowed everyone down at the first rest stop two years ago. Marshals are positioned at every road junction along the route and at each rest stop, coordinated by radio where mobile signal is unreliable out past the reservoir. A sag wagon will follow the back of the field, carrying spare parts and space for anyone who needs to abandon partway through, which has happened once or twice in past years without being any kind of crisis. The finish features a small gathering back at the clubhouse with food donated by a local bakery, and a short thank-you speech acknowledging both riders and the volunteers who make the whole day possible.
