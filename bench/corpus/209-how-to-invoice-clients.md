My current invoicing process, tightened up after the late-payment friction with Kofi's team, see [[196-annual-client-review-meeting]], which made me realize the process had gaps I'd been quietly working around.

## Send Promptly

Invoice goes out within 24 hours of final delivery, never bundled with a later job. Delay here signals the invoice isn't a priority, and clients notice that more than they'd ever say directly.

## Clear Terms

30 days net, stated plainly on every invoice, no assuming the client remembers from the contract.

## What's Included

Line items: shoot fee, editing, any rush premium, usage rights covered, expiration if applicable. Spelling out usage rights explicitly on the invoice itself, not just the contract, has cut down on the "can we also use this for X" follow-up questions that used to come in weeks after delivery, once a client's team had already moved on and forgotten what the original scope actually covered.

## The 25-Day Nudge

A short, friendly check-in email at day 25 if unpaid, before it's technically late, not after.

## If It Goes Late

Second email at day 35, direct but polite, asking if there's a processing issue on their end.

## Escalation Point

Past day 45, I call rather than email, and mention it may affect future scheduling priority.

## Recordkeeping

Every invoice logged with sent date, due date, and paid date in the same tracker used for [[193-tax-season-journal]], so nothing depends on memory or digging through an inbox later when tax season or a client dispute actually requires the real dates rather than a rough guess at when something happened.
