# Youth Cycling Program

A pilot programme teaching bike handling and road safety to kids aged eight to twelve, run one Saturday morning a month starting this spring, using the school hall car park for the closed-course sessions.

## Why

Several members have kids who want to ride but have nowhere structured to learn beyond a parent teaching in a driveway on weekends. Dana proposed this after seeing a similar programme at another club, and committee approved a small budget for cones, helmets to lend, and printed handouts on the road rules.

## Format

Each session runs ninety minutes: thirty minutes of closed-course skills like figure-eights and emergency braking in the car park, thirty minutes covering road rules and hand signals, and a final thirty minutes riding a short, quiet loop with adult supervision at a ratio the insurance policy requires of one adult per four children present. Sessions are free, capped at sixteen kids, and require a parent or guardian present throughout rather than a drop-off model.

## Staffing

Four trained adult volunteers per session, drawn from the same volunteer pool discussed at [[096-annual-volunteer-coordination-meeting]]. Priyam is running a short briefing for volunteers before the first session, covering age-appropriate teaching and basic safeguarding guidance.

## Budget

Around £300 for the pilot: cones and chalk for course marking, ten loaner helmets of varying sizes, and printed handouts on road rules. Insurance is covered under the club's existing policy at no extra cost, confirmed in writing by the insurer before anything was announced publicly to members and parents.

## Risks

The biggest risk is volunteer burnout if this proves popular and demand outpaces the four-person staffing model; Priyam wants to cap enrolment rather than stretch volunteers thin in the early months while the format is still being tested. Safeguarding is the other serious concern: every volunteer working with kids needs a background check completed before the first session, which takes a few weeks to process and has already been factored into the timeline below. Weather is a minor risk since the car park sessions can't easily move indoors.

## Timeline

Background checks submitted this week, volunteer briefing in three weeks, and the first pilot session the following Saturday, weather permitting, with a full review after three sessions to decide whether the programme continues into the summer term.
