Gear shakedown for the Wonderland Trail trip, two weeks before departure, held at Dana's apartment with full packs laid out on the living room floor. Attendees: Dana, Priyam, Kofi. Goal was to weigh every item, cut anything unnecessary, and confirm nothing critical is missing before the final week of prep, since this is our last chance to order replacement gear with time to spare. We spread every piece of gear across the floor by category: shelter, sleep system, kitchen, clothing, and miscellaneous, so it was easy to spot duplicates before we even touched the scale. Dana brought a kitchen scale for anything the pack's built-in scale couldn't handle precisely, and Priyam kept a running spreadsheet on a laptop so we had real numbers instead of guesses by the end of the night.

## Pack Weigh-In

Each of us weighed our full pack including food and water for day one, and all three came in heavier than the fourteen-pound base weight target, though Priyam's was closest at fifteen point eight pounds base. Dana's pack was the heaviest at nineteen pounds base, mostly because of a heavier sleeping bag rated for colder temperatures than we're likely to see in September, plus a second book that got cut on the spot. Kofi brought a full-size camp chair that added over a pound on its own; after some debate he agreed to leave it and just sit on his sleeping pad instead, which is what the rest of us do anyway. We went through the shared gear list item by item: tent, stove, fuel, water filter, bear canisters, first aid kit, and repair kit, confirming who's carrying what and that nothing is being duplicated across three packs unnecessarily. Priyam pointed out we were about to carry three separate lighters and two full firestarter kits between us, which is redundant for a trip where fires aren't even allowed above a certain elevation in this park. We also compared trekking poles, since Dana's older pair is noticeably heavier than the carbon poles Priyam and Kofi both use, and she agreed to borrow a spare lighter pair from a friend before departure instead of carrying the heavier ones out of habit. Clothing layers got a similar review; each of us had packed a slightly different mix of base layers, insulation, and rain gear, and we cross-checked against the forecast to make sure nobody was over-packing for cold that probably won't materialize in September.

## Discussion

Long discussion about the first aid kit, since Kofi wanted to bring a much larger kit than Dana and Priyam thought necessary, given that we're never more than a day from a ranger station on this loop. We compromised on a mid-size kit: enough for blisters, minor cuts, an ankle sprain wrap, and altitude or stomach medication, but not the full trauma kit Kofi originally packed, which included supplies more suited to a week deep in true backcountry with no bailout options. Also debated the satellite messenger versus a full satellite phone; the messenger is lighter and cheaper to rent, and since our main risk is injury or weather rather than needing two-way voice communication, the messenger won out as the better tradeoff for this specific trip. Went through the food bags next: five days each of dehydrated dinners, plus snacks and a lunch rotation of tortillas, cheese, and jerky that all three of us have used successfully on past trips without getting sick of it by day four. Water treatment sparked debate too, chemical drops as backup to the filter in case it clogs, which happened to Kofi on a trip in July and cost him half a day of finicky troubleshooting at a creek crossing before he gave up and just used drops instead. We also reviewed the resupply bucket contents together, making sure it has enough food for the back half plus a spare fuel canister, batteries, and a page of trail notes for the second half of the loop in case anyone's memory of the plan gets fuzzy by day six. Priyam raised one more point worth noting: none of us had actually tested the satellite messenger before renting it for the trip, so we agreed to spend an evening this week learning the interface and sending a couple of test messages before we're relying on it somewhere without cell coverage. Kofi also brought up whether we needed microspikes given a colder-than-usual forecast for high elevation, and after some back and forth we decided against it, since the itinerary avoids anything above nine thousand feet where ice would realistically be a concern this time of year.

## Decisions

Final decisions: camp chair stays home, Dana swaps to the lighter sleeping bag and leaves the second book, and the redundant lighters get consolidated down to one per person plus a single shared backup. First aid kit is the mid-size option Kofi will assemble and bring, satellite messenger is being rented from the outdoor shop downtown starting the Wednesday before we leave. Chemical water treatment drops go in as backup to the filter for all three packs, cheap insurance against a repeat of Kofi's July clogging incident at a creek crossing far from any resupply option. Resupply bucket gets finalized and dropped off at the ranger station the weekend before departure, and Dana is confirming the drop-off hours with the station directly since they're limited on weekdays. We also agreed to do one more short shakedown hike together this coming weekend with full packs, mostly to catch any last-minute fit issues with Dana's new sleeping bag before it's too late to exchange it. Kofi is bringing the mid-size first aid kit to that hike too, so we can all see exactly what's in it and know where things are packed in case anyone else needs to grab something quickly on the actual trip. We're skipping microspikes for this trip given the itinerary stays below nine thousand feet, and Priyam is testing the satellite messenger with a couple of practice messages before it becomes our only backup communication out there.

## Final Numbers

After the cuts, projected base weights are roughly twelve pounds for Priyam, thirteen for Kofi, and fourteen for Dana, which is a meaningful improvement from where we started tonight. Full pack weight with five days of food and a liter of water should land around twenty-six to twenty-eight pounds each, which feels reasonable for the terrain and daily mileage we're planning. One open item: Kofi still needs to confirm his new trail shoes are broken in enough by departure, since he's only got about thirty miles on them so far and the goal is at least fifty before a nine-day trip. We're checking in again after this weekend's shakedown hike to confirm those final numbers hold once food and water are actually loaded in. Priyam's spreadsheet will keep tracking these numbers through the shakedown hike and the actual trip, so we have real data for next time instead of relying on memory. Overall a useful evening: cutting nearly two pounds each without giving up anything we actually need.
