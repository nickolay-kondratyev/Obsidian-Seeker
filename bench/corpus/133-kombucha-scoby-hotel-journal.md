The SCOBY hotel has officially outgrown its jar, a problem I created for myself by refusing to throw anything away for the better part of a year. What started as a single scoby from a friend of Kofi's has become six distinct layers stacked in a half-gallon jar of increasingly strong tea, each one a record of a batch that either got scaled up, flavored differently, or just paused when I got too busy to brew. Today's project is finally splitting the hotel into something manageable, giving away what I don't need, and setting up a proper rotation. Priyam's been asking for a scoby for months and this is as good an excuse as any to finally hand one over rather than keep promising and forgetting. I counted six full layers when I tipped the jar out into a clean bowl, plus what looked like two thinner baby scobies that had formed on the surface between batches and never really thickened into anything substantial. The tea itself smelled properly vinegary, sharper than I usually let a batch go, which tracks with how long some of these layers have been sitting untouched. I'm keeping the two thickest, healthiest-looking layers for my own ongoing rotation, splitting one respectable-sized layer for Priyam, and composting the rest, including both thin babies that never developed properly and one older layer that had gone a strange grey color I didn't love the look of. Before composting anything I did a smell check on each layer, since a moldy scoby is identifiable by fuzzy spots rather than normal brown yeast strands, and I wanted to be sure before handing anything to Priyam.

## Splitting the hotel

Splitting itself was straightforward once I had clean hands and a clean cutting board set aside specifically and dedicated just to this — peeling layers apart where they'd naturally separated, which most of them had, and using a clean knife only where two layers had partially fused together near the edges. The two I'm keeping went into a fresh jar with about two cups of the existing hotel tea plus a cup of freshly brewed sweet tea to give them something to actually eat, since strong old tea alone isn't great long-term food even for something as resilient as a scoby. Priyam's portion went into a small jar with a cup of the hotel tea to travel with it, which I'll drop off this week along with basic instructions since her only kombucha experience so far has been drinking my batches, not making her own. I wrote her a short care sheet covering ratios, first-batch timing expectations, and the warning signs of an actual mold problem versus a normal-looking yeast strand, since the last thing either of us wants is her tossing a perfectly fine first batch out of unnecessary panic. The compost pile got what was left, and it's strange how much heavier six months of accumulated scoby layers turn out to be once they're all sitting in a bowl together rather than hidden in a jar I mostly ignored. Cleaned the original hotel jar thoroughly with hot water and vinegar, since a full scoby collection like that tends to leave a slightly sticky residue on the glass that plain soap doesn't fully cut through on the first pass, and I don't want that residue interfering with whatever gets brewed in it next. Kofi warned me once that reused jars with leftover residue can throw off a new batch's flavor slightly, tasting faintly of whatever came before, a small detail I wouldn't have thought to check on my own.

## New rotation plan

Going forward, the plan for the next few months is a stricter rotation: brew regularly enough that the hotel never grows past two active layers, and if I fall behind on brewing for more than a couple weeks, either compost the oldest layer proactively or find someone else who wants a scoby before it becomes another six-layer situation I have to deal with later. I'm also starting a proper log this time, one line per batch with start date, flavor if any, and brew length, kept right next to the jar rather than trusted to memory, which is clearly what let this get so out of hand in the first place. Kofi suggested labeling the jar itself with a dry-erase marker for the current batch's start date, which is a simpler habit than a separate notebook and probably more likely to actually stick given how the notebook idea has failed me with sourdough logging before, more than once, in ways I'd rather not repeat with kombucha too, so I'm trying the marker approach from the very first jar this time.

## Tasting the leftover tea

Tasted a small cup of the strongest hotel tea before dumping the rest, mostly out of curiosity, and it was genuinely too sharp to drink comfortably, more useful as a starter liquid for new batches than as anything resembling a finished beverage worth serving to anyone. That's roughly the amount of vinegar-forward tea I'll keep on hand going forward for starting new batches quickly, rather than the accidental stockpile that built up this time around. Priyam laughed when I described the taste and said it sounded like something you'd use to strip paint rather than drink, which is fair, honestly, and not far from how it actually hit going down. Next hotel split, I'll be more disciplined about not letting it sit this long before dealing with it properly.
