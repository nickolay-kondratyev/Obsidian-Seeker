## Scope

Three themes anchor the chapter: parking and land-use regulation, transit equity and access, and the political economy of eviction. I'm deliberately excluding the broader "smart growth" literature — Priyam warned it would balloon the chapter, and I agree after skimming the Voss anthology last week. Advisor confirmed this scoping call is fine as long as I note the exclusion in the introduction.

## Sources gathered so far

Zotero library sits at 61 sources tagged `lit-review`, split roughly 24 parking/zoning, 19 transit, 18 eviction. Strongest cluster is parking minimums — Adeyemi's book plus four recent working papers give a tight throughline from Shoup-era critique to current reform waves. Transit equity sources skew older, mostly 2008-2015, so I need newer empirical work. Eviction sources are thin on quantitative methodology, heavy on ethnography, which suits the mixed-methods framing in [[152-thesis-project-plan]] but leaves a gap for the data chapter.

## Gaps

No sources yet on informal/paratransit systems, which the committee flagged last spring. Also missing anything post-2024 on parking reform outcomes — most reform literature is aspirational rather than evaluative, since the policies are too new for outcome data.

## Organizing structure

Chapter will run thematic rather than chronological: each section opens with the dominant framework, then traces critiques, then lands on the empirical gap my dissertation fills. Draft outline lives in a separate doc; [[156-committee-meeting-minutes]] has the committee's sign-off from the March meeting. Still deciding whether eviction comes before or after transit, since the two interact more than the outline currently implies.

## Notes on synthesis approach

Rather than summarizing each source in sequence, I'm grouping by claim and citing clusters — this is closer to how Kofi's dissertation handled its lit review, and it reads less like an annotated bibliography. Each subsection needs one paragraph naming the consensus view, one naming dissenters, and one stating where my project sits relative to both. I keep drifting into summary mode when tired, so I flag those paragraphs in comments and revisit them the next morning with fresher eyes, once the argument is easier to see clearly.
