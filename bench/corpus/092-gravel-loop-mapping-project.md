# Gravel Loop Mapping Project

The club has talked for two seasons about a signature gravel loop that starts and ends at the clubhouse, and this winter we're finally scoping it properly. The goal is a 35-45 kilometre route that mixes quiet farm roads with the disused rail bed north of the reservoir, avoiding the busy junction on Route 9 that half the membership complains about every spring. This document tracks scouting rides, surface notes, and the eventual GPX file we'll publish for members.

## Scouting So Far

Dana and Kofi rode the northern half in November and found the rail bed surface is mostly hard-packed gravel, rideable on 32mm tyres without much fuss, though there's a 400 metre stretch near the old signal box that's washed out and needs a short road detour. The southern half hasn't been scouted since the farm roads are muddy this time of year; Priyam wants to wait until March when the ground firms up. We've also had two members email asking whether the loop could connect to [[110-membership-tiers-and-bike-choices]] discussion about gravel bike ownership, since a signature route might nudge more members toward buying one. We've flagged that as a cross-reference for whoever eventually writes the comparison piece, rather than trying to settle the bike-choice question in this scouting log.

## Route Requirements

The route needs to satisfy a few competing constraints that came out of committee discussion. First, it has to be doable by an intermediate rider in under three hours including a coffee stop, which caps total distance around 45 kilometres given the terrain. Second, it should have at least one bailout point roughly halfway, ideally the general store in the next village, so riders who bonk or flat can call for a pickup without walking ten kilometres. Third, we want to avoid any road with a posted speed limit above 60 unless there's a wide shoulder, which rules out most of the direct route along Route 9 and forces the farm-road detour. Finally, the club insurance policy requires any 'official' route to avoid unmarked level crossings, which knocks out the shortcut past the old mill that a few members have ridden informally for years. None of these constraints are dealbreakers on their own, but stacked together they narrow the viable corridor considerably, which is part of why the scouting has taken longer than anyone expected when this project first came up at committee.

## Timeline

Scouting the southern half in March, a full test ride with six volunteers in April, and a public GPX release with the first organised loop ride in May, timed to land before the century ride season really picks up. Each stage has an owner assigned already, so slippage should be visible early rather than discovered the week before the launch ride.

## Open Questions

A few things are still unresolved and will need a decision at the next committee meeting rather than being settled by whoever scouts the route next. Should the loop have an official shorter variant for members on hybrid bikes, cutting the rail bed section and staying on pavement for roughly 25 kilometres total? Tomasz thinks yes, arguing that a gravel-only route quietly excludes a third of the club who don't own the right bike, while Dana worries that maintaining two GPX files and two cue sheets doubles the ongoing admin for not much benefit. There's also the question of formally requesting permission from the rail trust for the rail bed section, since we've been riding it under an informal understanding for years and someone raised the chance that could change if the corridor is ever sold.
