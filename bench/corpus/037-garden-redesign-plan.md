# Garden Redesign Plan

Scope document for reworking the backyard garden around the new workshop footprint. The current beds are a leftover 1980s layout that no longer makes sense once the workshop is built, so this plan replaces roughly two-thirds of the existing planting.

## Current State

The backyard is dominated by an overgrown lawn, a row of aging boxwoods along the fence, and three raised beds that Lotte built years ago but that now sit partly in the workshop's planned footprint. Those beds will need to be relocated or rebuilt entirely before spring planting season, ideally with cedar this time around.

## Goals

The redesign prioritizes food production over ornamental planting, since that's shifted as a household priority over the last two years, with a rough target of covering a meaningful share of summer vegetables from the garden itself. We also want a clear sightline from the kitchen window to the workshop door, and we want to keep at least one mature tree, the old apple in the back corner, since replacing that kind of canopy takes decades nobody here has to spare.

## Zones

The garden splits into four zones: a vegetable zone near the kitchen door, a small orchard extension around the apple tree, a gravel and perennial border along the workshop's south wall, and a narrow herb strip outside the kitchen window. See [[057-garden-irrigation-system-doc]] for irrigation planning per zone.

## Materials

Raised beds will be cedar rather than the pressure-treated lumber Lotte originally used, mostly for food safety reasons given they're growing vegetables. Gravel for the border zone is a locally sourced pea gravel over landscape fabric, and the herb strip gets brick edging salvaged from the old front walkway, cleaned and reset by hand.

## Budget and Phasing

Phase one covers bed relocation and the vegetable zone, targeted for this spring so we don't lose a growing season. Phase two, the orchard extension and gravel border, follows once the workshop structure is closed in, since running equipment near young plantings risks damaging them. Total budget across both phases is roughly thirty-two hundred dollars, modest next to the kitchen and workshop lines but worth tracking, since garden costs creep upward fast once you start pricing specimen plants instead of seed packets.

## Open Questions

Still unresolved: whether the herb strip needs any winter protection given how exposed that side of the house is, and whether Lotte wants her old cedar beds back for her own yard once we replace them, which would save us a disposal trip.
