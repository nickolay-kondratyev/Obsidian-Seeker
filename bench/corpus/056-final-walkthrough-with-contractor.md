Final walkthrough of the kitchen with Kofi, close to nine months after the kickoff meeting where this whole thing started, punch list in hand and a genuine sense of the finish line being visible for the first time in a long while. Present: homeowner, Kofi, and briefly the cabinet supplier's installer who came back to adjust one door that wasn't closing quite flush after settling in over the past couple of weeks since the cabinets went in. We walked the whole room slowly, checking every surface against the punch list Kofi had drawn up from his own final inspection the day before, plus a shorter list of my own that had accumulated from actually living around the space over these last few weeks of near-daily use as things gradually came together toward completion.

## What Passed Clean

Most of the room passed without any notes at all, which was genuinely satisfying to hear after so many months of incremental progress punctuated by unplanned surprises like the drain replacement and the permit back-and-forth. The zellige backsplash grout lines read even and consistent across the whole wall, no visible haze remaining from the grouting process, and the countertop seams, where the two slabs meet near the sink, are essentially invisible unless you know exactly where to look for them under raking light in the late afternoon. Cabinet alignment throughout is solid, doors and drawers all closing with the same even resistance, and the under-cabinet lighting Kofi's electrician installed is wired cleanly to its own switch exactly as specified back in the original renovation plan, with no visible conduit or exposed wiring anywhere along the run beneath the upper cabinets. The floor transition into the dining room, one of my early worries about matching the existing engineered oak, turned out seamless enough that guests who've seen the place recently didn't even register it as a new floor until I pointed it out to them directly over dinner last weekend. The pantry pull-outs Kofi's crew built glide smoothly with no binding anywhere along their tracks, and the sage cabinet finish has held up well against the daily wear of actual cooking, no visible scuffing yet near the range where I'd expected the first signs of wear to show up eventually given how often that spot gets bumped by pans and elbows alike during a normal week of cooking.

## Punch List Items

Four items remain, all minor. First, the cabinet door near the range that wasn't closing flush, which the installer adjusted on the spot today by tweaking the hinge tension slightly, resolved within about ten minutes of actual work once he'd identified which specific hinge was the culprit. Second, a small gap in the caulk line where the backsplash meets the countertop on the far end near the window, cosmetic only but worth fixing before we call this fully done, since it's exactly the kind of small detail that would nag at me every time I noticed it doing dishes at that end of the counter. Third, one of the under-cabinet light fixtures flickers faintly on the coldest setting of its dimmer, which the electrician suspects is a loose connection rather than a faulty fixture itself, scheduled for a quick follow-up visit next week to confirm and fix properly rather than guessing at it today without the right tools on hand. Fourth, and most involved of the four, the range hood makes a faint rattling sound at its highest fan speed that wasn't present during the initial test run a few weeks ago, which Kofi thinks might be a loose duct connection somewhere above the ceiling line rather than anything wrong with the hood unit itself, and he's coming back with his HVAC subcontractor to check it properly rather than opening up finished drywall on a guess about where exactly the rattle is actually originating from. None of the four items is remotely close to a dealbreaker on their own, but I appreciated Kofi treating all four with the same seriousness as if they were, rather than waving off the smaller ones as not worth a follow-up visit given how close we already are to being fully finished with this whole room. He said he'd rather over-deliver on a punch list than leave a client wondering, months later, whether some small nagging thing was ever actually going to get properly addressed or just quietly forgotten once the final invoice cleared.

## Overall Impressions

Standing in the finished room today, it's strange how quickly the disruption of the last nine months has started to recede from memory, replaced by how normal this space already feels to move through daily, as though it had always looked roughly like this rather than having been a construction zone with no floor for weeks at a stretch not that long ago. Kofi mentioned this is a common feeling among clients at final walkthrough, that people adjust to a finished space faster than they expect to, which somehow makes all the disruption feel both more and less real in retrospect than it did while actually living through it day by day. We talked briefly about the workshop's own timeline too, since that project is still ongoing in parallel and clearly benefiting from lessons learned here, particularly around documentation and communication with the permit office, which Kofi said has genuinely smoothed out the workshop process compared to how the kitchen permit went earlier in the year. He also mentioned, half joking, that he's started recommending future clients budget a full contingency conversation into their very first kickoff meeting rather than waiting for the first surprise to force it, which is a lesson our own project apparently helped teach him as much as it taught us along the way. It was a nice reminder that even someone who's done this dozens of times is still learning something new from each individual house, ours apparently included, despite how routine any single job might look from the outside.
