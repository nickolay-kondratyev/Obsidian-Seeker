# Packing for a Multi-Day Club Tour

Written up after our four-day tour last September so nobody has to relearn these lessons the hard way, especially the weight ones.

## Panniers vs Bikepacking Bags

Panniers carry more but add wind drag and weight low on the bike; bikepacking bags keep things lighter and more aerodynamic but force real discipline about what actually comes along for the ride.

## Rule One

If you're unsure, leave it home. Nobody regrets packing light.

## Clothing

One spare kit, not two: you can rinse and dry overnight if the guesthouse has anywhere to hang things, which most do. Pack one warm layer beyond what feels necessary, since mountain evenings drop faster than flatland ones, and a lightweight rain shell even if the forecast looks clear, because forecasts four days out are little better than guesses.

## Tools

Multi-tool, spare tube, and patch kit. Skip the full toolkit; club members can fix most things between them at a rest stop.

## Food and Water

Two bottles minimum, three in hot weather, plus a way to purify water if the route runs remote. Carry snacks for gaps between towns, but don't overpack; most routes pass a shop at least once every couple of hours.

## Weight Target

Aim for under 8kg of gear excluding water. Weigh your loaded bags first.

## What We Got Wrong Last Time

Tomasz packed a full stove setup for a route that passed a cafe every day, dead weight up every climb. Lotte packed two spare kits and never used the second. I forgot a rain shell on day two and rode an afternoon in a bin bag with holes cut for arms, borrowed from a petrol station, which still comes up in conversation. The lesson: pack for the route you're actually riding, not a worst case that rarely happens on a well-supported club tour.
