Notes on *Pricing the Unpriceable*, recommended by another studio owner at a local meetup.

## Core Argument

Creative pricing should anchor to client outcomes, not to hours worked or gear owned.

## Chapter Two

Good case study on a wedding photographer doubling rates by repositioning as an "archive," not a service. Worth stealing the framing, even outside weddings.

## A Useful Line

"You are not selling photographs. You are selling the client's confidence in the outcome."

## Where I Disagree

The book assumes most creative buyers are emotionally driven. Product clients are usually budget-driven and comparing quotes directly, so the "outcome anchoring" pitch lands differently than it would for a wedding client choosing on feeling. Still worth adapting the framing partially.

## Applying It

I've started leading quotes with what the client gets to do with the images, listing use, ads, print, rather than just a shot count and day rate.

## Rating

Four stars. Recommend to Lotte, might reframe how her team briefs vendors too.
