## Drainage and Grading Discussion

Tomasz flagged that the current grade along the workshop's south wall slopes very slightly toward the structure rather than away from it, which is a problem regardless of the garden plan and needs correcting before any gravel goes down, since gravel alone won't fix a grading issue underneath it and will just mask a problem that resurfaces the first heavy storm after installation. He recommended a shallow swale running parallel to the wall, feeding toward a dry well near the property's lowest corner, which adds a modest cost but solves both the garden drainage and the workshop's foundation concern in a single pass rather than treating them as separate problems that happen to sit next to each other. Kofi agreed this was worth doing properly now rather than patching it later once the gravel and plantings are already in place and harder to work around without redoing finished work. We also discussed the landscape fabric under the gravel, with Tomasz pushing back gently on my instinct to skip it, explaining that without it we'd be pulling weeds out of gravel forever, which is a chore he said nobody actually enjoys despite what gardening magazines sometimes imply about the meditative qualities of weeding. He also suggested a slightly coarser gravel grade than what I'd originally picked out from the sample chips, since finer gravel tends to migrate and compact over a season or two, losing the loose, walkable texture we actually wanted for that path in the first place.

## Orchard Extension and the Apple Tree

The apple tree's root zone is larger than I'd assumed, extending roughly to the tree's drip line and somewhat beyond it, which constrains where we can dig for new plantings without risking real damage to roots that have had six decades to establish themselves undisturbed. Tomasz recommended we treat the area directly under the canopy as a no-dig zone entirely, planting shade-tolerant perennials in shallow, small holes rather than anything requiring real excavation, and save the more ambitious orchard additions, like the two dwarf pear trees we'd talked about, for just outside the drip line where root competition is less of a concern for either the new or the established tree. This changes the layout somewhat from what's sketched in [[037-garden-redesign-plan]], shifting the pear trees further from the apple than originally planned, which actually works out better for eventual harvest access anyway, since the original spacing would have made ladder work awkward once all three trees matured and their canopies started competing for the same open ground beneath them each growing season. We also talked briefly about companion planting under the apple, since Tomasz mentioned comfrey and certain alliums can share root space with established trees without much competition, unlike shallow-rooted vegetables that would struggle for both water and light in that particular spot regardless of how carefully we tried to manage it. I liked the idea enough that we're adding a small comfrey patch to the plan, mostly for its reputation as a soil-building plant that also happens to flower attractively enough not to look purely utilitarian sitting under a mature apple tree in full bloom each spring. Tomasz also flagged that the two dwarf pear varieties we'd picked need a compatible pollinator within range to fruit reliably, and recommended checking that against the neighboring yards before finalizing the order, since a solo dwarf pear can sit there for years producing almost nothing at all without one planted nearby.

## Budget Impact

The drainage swale and dry well add roughly six hundred dollars to the original garden budget, not accounted for in the figures tracked in [[052-budget-tracking-overview]], but Tomasz was clear this isn't optional given the grading issue near the workshop wall, so it's going in regardless of how the rest of the phasing shakes out, and Kofi agreed it's cheaper to solve now than to fix water damage against the workshop foundation later. The revised orchard layout, with the pear trees moved further from the apple's drip line, actually saves a small amount compared to the original plan, since the wider spacing means less specialized root-barrier material than we'd budgeted for closer plantings, which Tomasz said would have been necessary to keep the three trees from competing too aggressively underground within just a few years of growth. Net effect on the garden budget is close to a wash, maybe a hundred dollars over the original estimate once both changes are accounted for together rather than considered separately, which feels like an acceptable outcome given how much better the plan is now than what we started with this morning. Tomasz also mentioned his quotes tend to run stable once formalized, unlike some of the surprises we've had elsewhere on this project, which was a small but genuine relief to hear out loud after a stretch of budget conversations that have mostly gone the other direction lately on this project.
