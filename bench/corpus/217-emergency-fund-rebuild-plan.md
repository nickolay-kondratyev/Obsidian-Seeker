# Emergency fund rebuild plan

After the water heater failed in August and drained the fund to two months of expenses, we need a deliberate plan back to three months by April, rather than hoping surplus cash accumulates on its own like last time.

## Current state

The fund holds $6,200 against a three-month target of $9,300, based on average monthly expenses of $3,100. That's a $3,100 gap. The target excludes discretionary spending like dining and travel, since this fund covers true necessities during a job loss, not our normal lifestyle, and we recalculate the base expense figure every January using the prior twelve months of actual spending.

## Rebuild mechanics

Starting this month, $300/month moves automatically to the high-yield account, ahead of the extra $400/month currently going toward the car loan in [[212-debt-payoff-plan]]. That reduces the extra debt payment to $100/month rather than pausing it entirely, balancing two legitimate priorities instead of treating one as strictly more important. We picked $300 specifically because it still leaves the debt payoff moving forward, just more slowly, rather than freezing it completely while the fund rebuilds, which felt too extreme given how close the car loan already is to being cleared.

## Timeline

At $300/month the gap closes in a little over ten months, hitting target around November, later than the April goal from [[216-annual-financial-planning-meeting]]. To hit April instead, half of March's expected tax refund goes straight to the fund.

## Where it lives, and why separate

High-yield savings, currently 4.3% APY, kept fully separate from checking and from the car and home maintenance sinking funds. Separation removes ambiguity about what this money is for. Mixing it with sinking funds invites quietly borrowing from the emergency reserve for a car repair that should really come out of its own line, which happened once in 2024 and took months to notice.

## Trigger conditions

We only touch this for a genuine emergency: job loss, major uncovered medical expense, an urgent home or car repair above $500, not a "great opportunity" investment or a discretionary purchase that merely felt urgent in the moment of wanting it. Every withdrawal gets logged here with the exact date, amount, and a short written reason, no exceptions, even for small amounts that feel too minor to bother recording properly at the time it happens, since the log is what keeps this fund honest.

## Refill rule

Any withdrawal makes refilling the top financial priority again, above debt payoff, until the three-month target is restored, using the same automatic-transfer mechanism described above rather than requiring a fresh decision from us each time it happens.
