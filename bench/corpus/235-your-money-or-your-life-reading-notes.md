Reading *Your Money or Your Life* again, doing the exercises this time instead of skimming past.

## Core idea

Money equals life energy traded for hours worked. Every purchase costs remaining life hours.

## Chapter 3 exercise

Calculated my real hourly wage after commuting, clothes, and decompression time. Lower than salary divided by hours suggests — uncomfortable to see written down.

## Notable quote

> "The point is not to deprive yourself, but to find what enough looks like."

## Where I disagree

The book assumes a single steady income and a linear career, which doesn't match freelance income well. Dana's contract work makes the monthly-tracking table harder to fill in honestly, since some months are zero and others spike.

## Crossover point

The chapter where investment income finally exceeds expenses, plotted against monthly spending on a hand-drawn chart, is the emotional center of the whole book.
