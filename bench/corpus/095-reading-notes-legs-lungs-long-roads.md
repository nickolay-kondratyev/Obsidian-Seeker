Notes on *Legs, Lungs, and Long Roads* by a sports physiologist, borrowed from Kofi.

## Premise

Most amateur cyclists train too hard on easy days, too easy on hard days.

## Key Idea

The book argues for polarised training: roughly 80% of riding at genuinely easy conversational effort, 20% at real intensity, with almost nothing in the tempo middle ground.

## Quote

"Grey-zone riding builds fatigue without building real fitness," page 47.

## Chapter 6 — Recovery

Most useful chapter for me personally, mostly because it gave permission to ride genuinely slowly on recovery days instead of the half-hearted moderate pace I'd defaulted to. The author is blunt that ego is the obstacle, not knowledge.

## My Take

Convincing overall, though the case studies lean on racers rather than people riding for fitness and community. Worth adapting the ratio loosely rather than following it exactly.

## Rating

7/10. Recommend it for anyone doing structured winter training work.
