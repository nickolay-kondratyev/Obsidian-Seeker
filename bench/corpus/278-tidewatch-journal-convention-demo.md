Ran our first public demo table for Tidewatch yesterday, a small local convention, four scheduled slots plus a couple of walk-ups the organizer squeezed in between panels. I went in expecting mostly polite curiosity, the kind of interest that comes from a nicely painted box on a table full of other prototypes, and honestly got a lot more than that. Three of the four scheduled groups made it to a genuine ending, win or lose, inside the forty-five minute slot, which is the first time I've seen that consistency outside our own regular Tuesday group. Kofi came along to help run the second table so we could take two groups at once during the busiest afternoon block, and comparing notes with him afterward was almost as useful as the sessions themselves. We followed [[299-how-to-run-convention-demo-table]] fairly closely for setup and teaching, and it held up well even with two tables running the teach simultaneously a few feet apart, competing with the noise of a busy hall around us. I'm already thinking about tweaking that guide before the next one.

## Session Log

Kept a rough log through the day since two tables running in parallel made it easy to lose track of which group did what. Here's the breakdown by slot.

| Slot | Players | Outcome | Notes |
|------|---------|---------|-------|
| 11:00 | 3 | Win, round 9 | Confused by wind tokens early, clicked fast after round 3 |
| 12:15 | 4 | Loss, round 7 | Storm outran evacuation, felt fair per table |
| 13:30 | 2 | Win, round 11 | First walk-up pair, slower but engaged throughout |
| 14:45 | 4 | Loss, round 8 | One player quietly directed others, old captain problem resurfaced |
| 16:00 | 3 | Win, round 10 | Best session of the day, asked to buy a copy on the spot |

The 14:45 slot is the one that's stuck with me since packing up; the captain problem showing up again, in a group of total strangers who'd never met each other before sitting down, tells me it's not just a quirk of our regular playtesters knowing the game too well. It also means the fix has to work on a cold table with zero shared history, not just on our Tuesday group who've internalized four months of house etiquette about not backseat-playing for each other. The 12:15 loss is worth a second look too: that group felt the loss was fair even though the storm outran them by only one space, which suggests the tension curve on a close loss lands the way we want for a first-time cold audience.

## Reactions

Across the day, the most repeated comment was some version of "this feels more tense than I expected from the box art," which I'm choosing to take as a compliment even though it says something about the cover reading to a cold audience. The 16:00 group's request to buy a copy on the spot was the best moment of the day, and I had to explain, a little sheepishly, that there's no product yet, just a Kickstarter coming next year, which visibly deflated them.

## The Captain Problem, Again

Watching a total stranger fall into the same directing-everyone-else pattern we've been trying to design out since the earliest prototypes was the low point of an otherwise great day. It happened in the 14:45 slot: one player, clearly an experienced board gamer, worked out the wind-token economy two rounds faster than his table and started quietly telling the others what to do, phrased as suggestions but landing as instructions nobody felt able to override. The group still lost, which is almost the more interesting data point, since even with someone doing the "optimal" thing, the storm deck's variance beat them anyway; but the other three players visibly checked out for the last two rounds once they realized they weren't really making decisions anymore, just executing someone else's plan.

What struck me most was how fast it happened, inside maybe three rounds, well before anyone had a chance to build the kind of table rapport that might let a quieter player push back on the loudest one's suggestions. Kofi, watching from the next table over between his own group's turns, said it reminded him of the exact same pattern from our very first internal prototype sessions over a year ago, before we'd even split roles into four asymmetric wardens instead of one shared hand of cards. That's a little discouraging to hear this late in development, honestly, since I'd assumed the asymmetric-role redesign had mostly solved it, and it's a reminder that a fix that works on a group who already trusts each other doesn't automatically transfer to four strangers meeting for the first time at a convention table.

That's worth flagging directly for [[273-tidewatch-design-journal-storm-mechanic]] since the wind-token visibility rules might need another look, maybe hiding token counts from other players entirely rather than leaving them face-up on the table where anyone can do the math for everyone else. I want to test a fully-hidden-token variant with the next convention group we run, comparing directly against today's face-up baseline, and see whether hiding the numbers actually slows the captain-pattern down or just makes the loud player guess out loud instead, which might be worse rather than better for the quieter players at the table.
