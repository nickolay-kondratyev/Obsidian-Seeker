How to plan a group ride route that's actually safe for thirty riders of mixed ability, not just fast on paper for whoever mapped it alone on a Tuesday evening.

## What You'll Need

A route-mapping tool with elevation and surface data, a printed or offline copy for anyone without signal, knowledge of the group's actual pace range rather than the fastest rider's, and, ideally, someone who's physically ridden the route recently.

## Step 1: Set the Pace Target

Plan around the slowest regular rider expected to show up, not the group average.

## Step 2: Draft and Ride the Route

Sketch two or three candidate lines, favoring quieter roads over the shortest distance, checking each against recent incident notes from [[106-route-safety-review-meeting]] before picking one. Then ride the draft yourself, at the pace you're planning for rather than solo and fast, since a blind junction or a gate that's since been locked rarely shows up on a map, only on the road under your wheels.

## Step 3: Place Rest Stops

Space stops every fifteen to twenty miles, near water and a toilet, and away from any junction flagged as risky where the route allows it.

## Step 4: Write the Route Notes

Flag every hazard by mile marker: gravel, blind bends, level crossings, livestock gates, and any junction that scattered the group last time it was ridden, so nobody's relying purely on memory to catch it again this time around.

## Step 5: Brief the Group

Share the route sheet two days ahead, and confirm the sweep rider before anyone clips in.

## Common Mistakes

The most common mistake is planning for the group's best day rather than its worst, packing in more climbing than the turnout can manage, which strings the group out until the back half rides alone. A close second is trusting a mapping tool's road classification without checking it, since roads marked as quiet lanes turn out to be a farm's delivery route, invisible until you're watching traffic nobody warned you about. Skipping the pre-ride is costliest, the only step that reliably catches surprises no dataset ever captures.
