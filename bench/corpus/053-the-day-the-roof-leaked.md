It rained hard overnight, the kind of steady, driving rain that goes on for hours rather than the quick spring showers we've had most of this season, and I woke up to a dark water stain spreading across the dining room ceiling directly beneath where the old kitchen used to share a roofline before the renovation started shifting things around above it. My first thought, half-asleep and thoroughly unhelpful, was that the kitchen work had somehow caused this, even though the roof itself hasn't been touched at all during the renovation and there's no obvious reason the two would be connected beyond simple proximity and bad timing on a morning I really didn't need one more thing to worry about. Priyam heard me swearing quietly at the ceiling and came down to look, and between the two of us we managed to convince ourselves it was probably just condensation before deciding that was wishful thinking rather than an actual diagnosis, since condensation doesn't usually produce a stain shaped exactly like a slow, active drip pattern with a clear point of origin near the chimney flashing. I called Kofi first out of habit, even though this genuinely isn't his scope, and he was decent enough to talk through it with me for a few minutes anyway before firmly redirecting me to an actual roofer, since flashing and shingle work is well outside what a kitchen and workshop contractor should reasonably be diagnosing over the phone from someone else's ladder.

## The Roofer's Visit

The roofer came out same-day, which felt like genuine luck given how the week had already started, and spent about forty minutes on the roof with a flashlight and a bucket of soapy water, checking the chimney flashing methodically before finding what he was looking for: a section of flashing around the chimney that had lifted slightly, probably from years of freeze-thaw cycling working the old sealant loose bit by bit rather than any single dramatic event, exactly the kind of slow, cumulative failure that's easy to miss until a storm finally routes enough water through the gap to show up as a visible stain somewhere inside the house. He explained that this kind of flashing failure is extremely common on houses this age, especially around masonry chimneys where the roofing material and the brick expand and contract at different rates over decades of seasonal cycling, gradually working any seal loose no matter how well it was originally installed back when the chimney was first built or last resealed. The actual fix was straightforward: strip the old sealant, clean the joint thoroughly, and reseal with a modern flexible product rated for exactly this kind of expansion and contraction, a repair he completed within the hour rather than the multi-day ordeal I'd been quietly bracing myself for on the drive back from dropping Priyam at work earlier that morning. He also checked the rest of the roof while he was up there, since we were already paying for the visit, and flagged a few shingles on the north slope showing early wear that aren't urgent yet but are worth watching over the next year or two, adding them to a mental list of future projects that already feels uncomfortably long for a house we've owned less than two years at this point. Total cost for today's fix came in at just under three hundred dollars, a real relief compared to what I'd been imagining on the drive back from dropping Priyam at work, half convinced we were looking at a full roof replacement before the roofer even climbed his ladder.

## What This Taught Me

The house keeps finding new ways to remind me it's sixty-plus years old regardless of how much attention we're currently paying to the kitchen and workshop specifically, and today felt like a useful, if unwelcome, lesson that renovation focus doesn't pause the rest of the house's slow decline in the background while we're busy elsewhere. I think I'd unconsciously assumed that because we were actively renovating, the house was somehow protected from other, unrelated failures during this stretch, which is obviously not how any of this actually works once you say it out loud, but the assumption was there anyway, quietly, until this morning's ceiling stain corrected it rather bluntly and without much warning. It's a strange kind of tunnel vision, in hindsight, focusing so intensely on two specific rooms that the other fifty-odd years of accumulated wear everywhere else in the house genuinely slipped my mind for a while. Dana joked, when I mentioned it during our next budget call, that every homeowner she's worked with hits some version of this moment, usually right when they've convinced themselves the worst surprises are all behind them for good.

## Adding to the List

Roof gets a proper full inspection scheduled for this fall, before winter weather makes any further problems both harder to diagnose and more expensive to fix, and I'm adding a standing reminder to check the attic for water stains every few months going forward, rather than only noticing a problem once it's already visible on a finished ceiling downstairs, which feels like exactly the kind of reactive habit this house has been patiently teaching me to break since we first moved in. I'm also going to ask the roofer for a rough quote on the north slope shingles now, while it's fresh, rather than waiting until they actually fail and become an emergency call during some future storm.
