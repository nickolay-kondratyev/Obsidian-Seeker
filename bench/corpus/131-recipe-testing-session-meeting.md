Ferment Club side session, just Dana and Priyam, testing bread hydration. Sunday afternoon, four test loaves.

## Agenda

Compare four hydration levels on the same base recipe to settle the debate about whether higher hydration is worth the extra handling difficulty for a home baker without a mixer.

## Method

Same starter, same flour, same bake time and same oven setup throughout, varying only water percentage: 68%, 72%, 76%, and 80%, each loaf shaped identically before baking side by side on the same tray at once.

## Results

| Hydration | Handling difficulty | Oven spring | Crumb openness | Crust |
|-----------|---------------------|-------------|-----------------|-------|
| 68% | Easy | Moderate | Tight | Good |
| 72% | Manageable | Good | Even | Good |
| 76% | Sticky | Very good | Open | Excellent |
| 80% | Difficult | Flattened | Very open, uneven | Blistered |

## Discussion

72% came out as the clear favorite, offering most of the open crumb benefit of 76% without the sticky-dough struggle that made shaping the 80% loaf genuinely frustrating for Dana. Priyam thinks the 80% loaf's flattened spring came from underproofing relative to how fast that wetter dough moved, not hydration itself, and wants a rerun with shorter bulk time before ruling it out completely. They also agreed the 76% loaf's crust color looked the best of the four, likely from extra surface steam.

## Next steps

Rerun the 80% loaf with adjusted proof timing, and isolate the crust-steam variable separately from hydration entirely, next time around, before drawing final conclusions.
