# Kitchen Renovation Plan

This document tracks the full scope of the kitchen renovation for the 1962 bungalow, from demolition through final walkthrough. It exists as the single source of truth so that decisions made in meetings, like [[031-kitchen-contractor-kickoff]], don't get lost between the contractor, the cabinet supplier, and us. The kitchen is roughly 180 square feet, galley-adjacent to a small breakfast nook that we're folding into the main footprint. Target completion is eleven weeks from permit approval, though everyone involved expects at least one surprise behind the plaster.

## Scope of Work

Demolition covers the existing laminate counters, the 1970s-era cabinets that replaced the originals, and the vinyl flooring down to subfloor. We are not touching the exterior walls, which keeps us on the lighter permit track rather than triggering a full structural review. Plumbing moves are limited to the dishwasher line and a potential island sink relocation still pending a quote. Electrical work adds four new outlets to meet code, relocates the range hood vent through the roof instead of the side wall, and adds under-cabinet lighting on a separate switch. The pantry gets a full rebuild since the old one was really just a closet with wire shelving, and we want proper pull-out drawers instead. Flooring will be a wide-plank engineered oak to match what's already down in the adjoining dining room, avoiding an awkward transition strip in the doorway.

## Materials and Finishes

Cabinets are shaker-style in a matte sage green, ordered from the supplier discussed in the kickoff meeting, with brushed brass hardware. Countertops are quartz in a soft cream with light veining, chosen partly because it doesn't need sealing like the butcher block we originally considered. Backsplash is a handmade zellige tile in an off-white glaze, laid in a simple running bond pattern rather than anything busier, since the room already has a lot going on with the cabinet color and the exposed brick chimney breast. Flooring, as noted above, is engineered oak in a natural finish, chosen to match the adjoining dining room boards already down. Hardware finishes are brushed brass throughout, including the faucet, cabinet pulls, and the pendant lights over what will eventually be the island. We're avoiding polished chrome entirely since it clashed badly in the showroom mockup against the sage cabinets, and matte black felt too heavy for a room this size with such a low ceiling and limited natural light in winter.

## Budget Summary

Total budget for the kitchen is set at eighteen thousand dollars, tracked in detail in [[052-budget-tracking-overview]]. Cabinets and countertops make up the largest single line item at roughly seven thousand, followed by appliances at around thirty-eight hundred. Contingency is held at fifteen percent given the age of the house and the near certainty of finding something unpleasant behind the plaster once demo starts.

## Timeline

Demolition starts the second week after permit approval and should take three to four days given the modest scope. Rough plumbing and electrical follow immediately, running in parallel where trades don't conflict, roughly a week and a half combined. Drywall patching and painting come next, about four days including drying time, since the old plaster needs more skim coating than typical drywall. Flooring is a single week once walls are cured. Cabinet installation follows flooring so toe kicks sit flush, three to five days depending on on-site adjustment once the cabinets arrive. Countertop templating happens after cabinets are set, with fabrication adding one to two weeks, mostly outside our control. Final trim and punch list work wraps the project, budgeted at three days before the walkthrough.
