# Reviewing Studio Insurance Coverage

Current general liability and equipment policy hasn't been reviewed since the business started three years ago, and the equipment schedule attached to it still lists gear I sold off two years back while missing several thousand dollars of newer purchases, which means I'm both overpaying on retired items and underinsured on current ones simultaneously. Prompted this review before the relocation, since moving is exactly the moment an outdated policy causes real problems, and I'd rather catch the gap now than discover it during an actual claim.

## Why Now

Beyond the outdated equipment schedule, the relocation itself changes the risk profile meaningfully: new location, different building, and for the first time actual walk-in foot traffic from the print shop referral partnership if that moves forward. General liability needs to reflect the new address before the move, not weeks after, since a gap in coverage during a physical move is exactly when something is statistically most likely to go wrong. Full equipment schedule rebuild against current inventory, updated liability limits reflecting slightly higher revenue than when the original policy was written, and confirmation that the policy covers off-site shoots for the on-location furniture clients, which I've never actually verified explicitly and have been assuming rather than confirming outright with the broker.

## Broker Conversation and Cost

Scheduled a call with the broker for next week, bringing a full current equipment list with purchase dates and values, plus last year's revenue figure for the liability limit discussion, rather than showing up underprepared and letting the broker's default recommendations set the numbers instead of my own actual situation. Rough estimate from a quick online quote tool suggests the updated policy could run 10-20% higher than current premiums given the equipment increase and new address, though the broker call should sharpen that considerably before committing to any specific number, and I'm not locking anything in until I have real figures instead of a rough tool-generated estimate. Worth also asking whether bundling the equipment and liability policies with a single carrier would save anything over keeping them split the way they currently are, something I've never actually compared directly since the original policy was set up entirely on the broker's initial recommendation years ago without much pushback from me at the time, back when I honestly didn't know enough to ask better questions.

## Timeline

Want this fully sorted and the new policy active before the [[182-studio-relocation-project]] move date, not after, so there's no coverage gap during the transition when equipment is most exposed sitting in a moving truck. Realistically that means finalizing everything within three weeks, since underwriting on a revised equipment schedule can take several business days on its own.

## Open Question

Still deciding whether business interruption coverage is worth adding given how seasonal the business already is, need the broker's input on whether it's actually priced sensibly for a studio this size, given that a slow month already looks a lot like an interruption on paper even without anything actually going wrong. Also unclear whether the print shop partnership, if it moves forward with actual foot traffic through the new studio, would push liability into a different underwriting category entirely, worth raising directly with the broker before assuming the current quote already accounts for that possibility, since guessing wrong here could mean a real coverage gap discovered only after something has already gone wrong.
