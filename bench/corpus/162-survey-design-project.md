The household survey is the backbone of the fieldwork chapter, so I want the instrument locked well before the March window opens. Goal is a 15-20 minute survey covering commute patterns, housing tenure, and displacement risk, administered door-to-door and online in two transit corridors. This doc tracks the instrument draft, sampling logic, and pretesting schedule so I stop rewriting the same decisions in three different notebooks. It's also the version I'll hand to the IRB coordinator once the pretest round is done.

## Goals

The survey needs to answer three research questions from [[152-thesis-project-plan]]: how transit access correlates with rent burden, whether recent arrivals differ from long-term residents in mode choice, and how households describe their own displacement risk in their own words. I'm deliberately keeping the instrument short — under 20 minutes — because pilot conversations with Priyam suggested longer surveys get abandoned at the door. A secondary goal is comparability with the 2019 regional mobility survey, so a handful of questions are lifted nearly verbatim from that instrument to allow trend comparison. Lotte pointed out that keeping wording identical matters more than I'd assumed, since small phrasing shifts can move mode-choice responses by a few points.

## Draft Questions

Section A covers household composition and tenure (own, rent, informal arrangement) plus length of residence, since displacement questions only make sense with a baseline. Section B is commute diary style: mode for the last work or school trip, typical trip time, and a satisfaction rating on a 5-point scale, borrowed loosely from the format in [[159-how-to-analyze-census-tracts]] cross-tab work I did in the fall. Section C asks directly about rent changes in the last two years, any notices received, and whether the household has considered moving because of cost or commute burden — this is the section I expect needs the most pretesting. Section D is four open-ended prompts asking residents to describe their neighborhood in their own terms, meant to feed the qualitative coding pass rather than the regression models. I'm still deciding whether to include an income bracket question at all, given nonresponse rates on income items in the pilot. One option is to ask for a bracket rather than a figure, which pushed completion up when Priyam tested it informally with three neighbors, though that sample is too small to lean on.

## Sampling Plan

Stratified by block group along the two corridors, targeting 40 completed surveys per corridor split evenly between renters and owners where the tract composition allows it. Recruitment mixes intercept surveys near transit stops with a smaller door-to-door push in adjacent blocks, aiming for 120 completes total by the end of April, with a floor of 30 per corridor even if recruitment lags.

## Pretesting and Timeline

Pretest round one is eight cognitive interviews in February, walking volunteers through the draft question by question and flagging anything that gets misread. Kofi has agreed to help recruit two of those from his building. Round two is a soft-launch of 15 live surveys in early March to check timing and drop-off before the full run. Full fieldwork is scheduled for late March through April, overlapping with [[153-fieldwork-journal-transit-survey]] logging, with a hard stop before finals week so coding can start in May. Advisor sign-off on the instrument is needed by February 20th — I'll bring the pretested draft to the next check-in, along with a one-page memo summarizing what changed between the cognitive-interview draft and the soft-launch version, since the committee will want that paper trail later.
