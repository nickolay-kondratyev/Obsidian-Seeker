November through mid-December was the busiest stretch of the year by a wide margin, seven bookings in five weeks, most of them holiday catalog refreshes clients wanted live before their own Black Friday campaigns launched, and I said yes to every single one without doing the capacity math I now know I should have done before the first booking landed on the calendar. Looking back now, in the relative quiet of January, it's obvious there was no real buffer anywhere, one shoot ran into the next with barely a day to reset the studio, let alone rest. I don't regret taking the bookings, the revenue was genuinely excellent, but the way I structured the calendar is exactly the pattern that led into the burnout stretch I wrote about back in [[198-burnout-recovery-journal]].

> [!warning] Pattern noticed mid-November
> Zero buffer days between five consecutive bookings. This is the same shape as the stretch that led to burnout last winter. Noted here in real time rather than only realizing it in hindsight this time.

## What Actually Held Up

The bookkeeping habit held, somewhat to my own surprise given how little spare time there was for anything beyond the actual shoots and editing queue during those five weeks, and I'm genuinely proud that it survived a stretch this demanding without needing to be rescued afterward the way it would have in an earlier year of running this business. Sunday evening categorizing kept happening even during the worst week, admittedly rushed and abbreviated some weeks compared to a normal pace, but it happened consistently enough that January's tax prep wasn't the nightmare it could have been if I'd let five weeks of receipts and invoices pile up untouched the way I might have in an earlier year of running this business. The backup system also held up under real pressure for the first time since I rebuilt it properly, see [[197-backup-workflow-project]], nightly local backups ran every single night without me manually triggering anything, and the cloud sync kept pace even during the two heaviest shooting days back to back, which was genuinely reassuring to watch actually work as designed rather than just trusting it blindly and hoping nothing broke during the single most demanding stretch it had faced since going live months earlier. Priyam also held up remarkably well physically, better than I did honestly, and specifically credited the equipment reorganization from back in [[181-monthly-gear-check-in]] for making the fast turnarounds between bookings noticeably less chaotic than they would have been a year earlier with the old, disorganized prop closet still in place.

## What Didn't Hold Up

Sleep was the first thing to go, predictably, several nights under six hours during the worst two weeks. Communication with Priyam got noticeably terser than usual, less the easy back-and-forth we normally have and more clipped, purely logistical exchanges, which Priyam actually mentioned directly on the second-to-last shoot day, gently but clearly, and I appreciated the honesty even though it stung a little in the moment. Meals also slipped badly, more takeout in five weeks than the rest of the year combined, which I noticed but didn't actually fix at the time.

## Doing It Differently Next Year

Concretely, I want to cap any single stretch at four bookings without at least one full buffer day inserted somewhere in the middle, even during peak holiday season when the temptation to say yes to absolutely everything is strongest and the revenue argument for doing so is genuinely the most persuasive it ever gets all year. I also want to raise rush-adjacent holiday pricing specifically for this particular window, not as a punishment for clients booking during a naturally busy season, but as a real, honest reflection of what a five-week sprint with zero slack actually costs me physically and mentally, a cost that current pricing doesn't currently account for at all in any explicit way, and that gap between real cost and actual price charged is worth closing deliberately rather than continuing to just absorb it quietly every single December the way I have for each of the last several years running this business more or less the same way. Concretely, I'm planning to block November and early December on the calendar right now, in January, while I still remember exactly how this stretch felt, rather than waiting until October when the same optimistic capacity assumptions from every previous year will inevitably start creeping back in, quietly and persuasively, the way they always seem to. I also want to tell returning clients explicitly, ahead of time, that holiday bookings after a certain date in November carry a rush premium by default, rather than negotiating it case by case under pressure in the moment, since that case-by-case negotiation is exactly where I've historically caved and just said yes anyway despite my own better judgment telling me not to. I'm also going to talk to Priyam explicitly about what a sustainable holiday schedule looks like from their side too, rather than only planning around my own capacity and assuming theirs will simply stretch to match whatever mine does, which isn't fair to either of us and clearly showed in how terse things got between us by the second week of December this year.

## One Thing I'm Proud Of

Despite everything above, every single deliverable this year went out on time and every client seemed genuinely happy with the final work, which after a stretch like that is not nothing, whatever the personal cost turned out to be behind the scenes, and worth actually writing down here so I remember it later.
