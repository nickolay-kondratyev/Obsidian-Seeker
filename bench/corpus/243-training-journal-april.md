Six weeks out from the Wonderland Trail and I finally started an actual training block instead of just hoping the fitness from last summer carried over. Today was a loaded hike up the fire road behind the reservoir, thirty-five pounds in the pack, mostly water jugs and a sandbag I keep for exactly this purpose. It took fifty-eight minutes to the top, which is slower than I wanted, but the last time I did this climb unloaded it still took forty-two, so the delta isn't as bad as it felt at the time. My calves were done by the descent, which tracks, since downhill under load is the thing I've neglected most this year, always training the climb and forgetting the equally punishing way back. Priyam texted asking if I wanted to do the same loop together next weekend, and I said yes before thinking about how sore I'll be by Wednesday. Kofi has been doing stair repeats at the parking garage near his office, twenty minutes at lunch three times a week, and swears it's made more difference than anything else in past training cycles. I should probably add some kind of ankle stability work, since the trail runners I'm switching to for this trip have way less structure than the boots I trained in last year, and my right ankle has always been the weak link on uneven ground. For now the plan is two loaded hikes a week plus one easy recovery walk, building pack weight by two pounds every week and a half until I'm at the actual trip weight of about thirty-two pounds with food.

The Saturday session is the one I care most about, since it's the closest analogue to an actual trail day, steady climbing with real weight rather than a short steep grind. Comparing to last week, gain is up about fifteen percent and pack weight is up almost twenty percent, which is roughly the progression I mapped out before the block started, so at least the plan is holding for now. Heart rate during the Saturday climb averaged one hundred fifty-two, which is higher than I'd like for a sustained effort, and it's a reminder that aerobic base still needs work even though the strength side feels like it's coming along fine. I want next week's long session to hit twelve kilometers with at least six hundred meters of gain, which means finding a route with more actual elevation than the fire road, since that one tops out around five hundred fifty no matter how many laps I do. There's a ridge trail forty minutes away that Dana mentioned has closer to eight hundred meters over a similar distance, so that's probably where Saturday's session should move to if the weather cooperates. Recovery has felt reasonable so far, nothing beyond normal soreness, though I'm watching my right knee closely since it's historically the first thing to complain when volume ramps up too fast in a short window.

## Gear Notes

Gear notes from today: the pack's hip belt is still not quite dialed in, digging into my left hip after about forty minutes no matter how I adjust the straps. I think it might just be the wrong size for my frame, so I'm going to swap it at the shop this week rather than keep fighting it through the whole training block. The trail runners performed fine on the loaded descent, no hot spots, though I noticed the tread is already showing wear after less than fifty miles, which is faster than expected for shoes rated for this kind of use. Trekking poles made a real difference on the way down, taking enough load off my knees that I actually felt fine by the bottom instead of the usual grinding ache. I also tried the new water filter for the first time on this hike, filling a bottle straight from the reservoir overflow, and it cleared a liter in under a minute, which is faster than the old squeeze filter I retired last year.
