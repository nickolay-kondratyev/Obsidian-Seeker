Sunday planning talk, 2026-05-11. Dana and me, no agenda beyond one open question.

## The question

Should extra cash go toward paying down the mortgage faster, or into the taxable brokerage account once debts and the emergency fund are both fully sorted later this year.

## The numbers

Mortgage rate is 5.2%, fixed. Expected long-run market return, conservatively, sits around 6-7% real. The math favors investing on paper, though the gap is narrower than either of us expected going in, closer than the popular finance-forum wisdom on this topic usually suggests.

## The counterargument

Dana pointed out the math ignores certainty: a guaranteed 5.2% return versus a volatile 6-7% average that could badly underperform during exactly the years we need the money most, especially near an early retirement date when sequence-of-returns risk bites hardest and a bad early stretch does outsized damage to the whole plan regardless of long-run averages.

## Where we landed

Split it: 70% to the brokerage account, 30% extra toward the mortgage principal, revisited annually alongside [[216-annual-financial-planning-meeting]] rather than treated as a permanent, unquestioned rule locked in today. Neither of us fully got our preferred outcome, which is usually a decent sign that a compromise is actually fair rather than one side quietly folding. Dana gets the comfort of visibly shrinking the mortgage balance each quarter; I get to keep most of the expected-return upside, and we both agreed that's reasonable given how differently we weigh certainty against growth.

## Next step

Update the automatic transfers once the emergency fund hits target, tracked in [[217-emergency-fund-rebuild-plan]], and set a calendar reminder for next May to revisit this split honestly.
