# Group Ride Hand Signals

Written for new members after a couple of close calls where someone simply didn't know what a signal meant.

## Why This Matters

At speed, in a tight group, you often can't shout a warning fast enough for it to be useful. Hand signals pass information backward through the group almost instantly, rider to rider, without anyone needing to turn around.

## Rule One

Signal early, signal clearly, and pass it back to the rider behind you.

## Hazards

Point down at the road surface for potholes, glass, or debris, on whichever side the hazard sits. A flat palm waved behind your back means slow down. An arm swept fully behind your back means stop entirely, reserved for genuine emergencies like a junction opening unexpectedly ahead of the group.

## Turns

A straight arm pointed in the direction of travel means turning that way ahead. Simple, but easy to forget under pressure.

## Traffic

A flat hand held behind your back, palm facing back, warns of a car overtaking from behind. Some clubs just shout "car back"; we use both, since wind noise can swallow a shouted warning on a fast descent.

## Practice

New members run through every signal at the spring clinic before their first ride.

## Common Mistakes

The most common mistake is signalling too late, right as you're already swerving around the hazard rather than a bike length or two ahead. The second is signalling and forgetting to hold the position long enough to register; flicking a finger down for half a second isn't enough in a fast group. The third, and probably the most avoidable, is not passing the signal back through the group at all, assuming someone further back will spot the hazard themselves, which defeats the entire point.
