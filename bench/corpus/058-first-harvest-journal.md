Picked the first real tomatoes today, a small bowl of them, mostly the early-ripening variety I planted specifically for this, still warm from the sun when I brought them inside and set them on the counter next to the sink where the light from the window catches them best in the late afternoon. It's strange how much weight a handful of tomatoes can carry after a season like this one, watching the beds go from bare cedar frames and churned dirt to something that's actually producing food a household can eat, on a timeline that's felt both impossibly slow in the day-to-day watering and unbelievably fast now that I'm holding the actual result of it in my hands this evening. Priyam came out to the garden when I called, and we stood there for a minute just looking at the plants together, comparing this year's chaotic, slightly overplanted beds against the tidy diagram in [[037-garden-redesign-plan]] that started this whole project, which bears almost no resemblance to how the actual beds look now, sprawling and wild in a way the original sketch never quite captured.

## What Came In Today

Six tomatoes from three different plants, ripening at slightly different rates as expected given how staggered the varieties were chosen to be, plus a modest handful of the earliest bush beans, tender enough to eat raw straight off the vine, which I did, standing in the garden, before a single one made it anywhere near the kitchen where they were technically supposed to go. The peppers are still green and a couple of weeks out from anything worth picking, but they're sizing up well along the sunnier south edge, and the herb strip Lotte's cuttings filled in is now genuinely productive too, enough basil already that I made a small batch of pesto this afternoon using nothing but what came out of our own dirt, which felt disproportionately satisfying for something that, in the end, is just a jar of blended leaves and oil sitting in the refrigerator door. The marigolds along the bed edges are also fully in bloom now, bright enough that they've become a small daily landmark on the way out to check everything each morning before work, and I've genuinely started looking forward to that short walk more than I expected to when I planted them almost as an afterthought back in spring. The squirrel situation, worth a note here too, seems to have settled down considerably since the chicken wire went in weeks ago, though I still catch one eyeing the tomato bed suspiciously most mornings from the fence line, apparently biding its time for an opportunity that, so far at least, hasn't actually come.

## Dinner Tonight

Simple tomato salad, the beans blanched quickly and tossed with the fresh pesto, nothing complicated, letting everything speak mostly for itself rather than burying it under a heavier sauce or preparation that would have made the actual vegetables beside the point of the whole meal. Priyam added a bit of good olive oil and flaky salt at the table, and that was genuinely the whole meal, simple enough that it barely felt like cooking at all.

## A Longer Thought

There's something about growing your own food, even in this genuinely modest quantity, that reframes the whole idea of a renovation for me tonight in a way I didn't fully expect going into any of this a year ago. The kitchen and workshop are both, in their own ways, about improving how we live in this house day to day, but the garden feels different, more like closing a small, particular loop between the house and the actual ground it sits on, something the kitchen counters and workshop bench, for all their genuine usefulness, don't quite manage to do in the same direct way a tomato pulled warm off the vine and eaten within the hour somehow does. I think about the newspaper we found behind the kitchen wall during demo sometimes, that faded scrap from 1962, and wonder whether whoever built this house originally kept any kind of garden here too, whether tomatoes grew in roughly this same patch of ground sixty-some years before Lotte ever handed over her cuttings, before any of us who currently live here were even born to wonder about it. There's no way to actually know, of course, no photograph or record I've found that answers the question directly, but it's a nice thought to sit with anyway on an evening like tonight, eating something we grew ourselves in a yard that's clearly been feeding people, in one form or another, for a very long time before we ever showed up to add our own small chapter to it. The renovation has mostly been about permits and budgets and surprises behind old plaster, practical and sometimes stressful in ways that don't leave much room for this kind of reflection in the middle of an actual workday, but tonight, holding a bowl of tomatoes we grew from seed just a few months ago, all of that faded into the background for a little while, replaced by something quieter and more satisfying than any finished cabinet or sealed roof seam has managed to feel on its own so far.

## Tomorrow

Check on the peppers, water everything by hand since it's been dry for a few days now, and maybe finally write down the pesto recipe properly before I inevitably forget the exact proportions I used tonight, since I never do measure these things carefully the first time around and always regret it a little the second time I try to recreate something good.
