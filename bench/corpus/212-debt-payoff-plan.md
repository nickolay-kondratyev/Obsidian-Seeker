# Debt payoff plan

Goal: clear the remaining car loan and the last chunk of student loan by the end of 2027, without pausing retirement contributions below the employer match. Two debts, one avalanche order, one shared spreadsheet Dana and I both check weekly. This doc tracks the plan, not just the numbers, so future us remembers why we chose this order, and so we have something concrete to point back to the next time either of us is tempted to switch to snowball order because it feels faster. It doesn't; the math below explains why.

## Current balances

Car loan: $8,400 remaining at 5.9%, minimum payment $310/month, 22 months left at minimum pace. Student loan: $11,200 remaining at 4.1%, minimum payment $180/month. Total minimum monthly debt service is $490. We're currently putting an extra $400/month toward the car loan since it carries the higher rate, following avalanche order rather than snowball, even though the student loan balance is larger and would feel more satisfying to watch shrink. The extra $400 comes from the $700/month we freed up after renegotiating our internet and phone plans in November, plus trimming the subscription list down to three services instead of seven. Neither of us misses the streaming bundle we dropped, and the phone plan switch alone saved $45/month with no coverage difference we've noticed on either carrier.

## Strategy

Avalanche, not snowball, because the rate gap is wide enough (5.9% vs 4.1%) that the interest saved is worth more than the psychological win of closing the smaller student loan first. Once the car loan is gone, projected around August 2026 at current extra-payment pace, the full $710/month (minimum plus extra) redirects to the student loan, which should then close by roughly March 2027. After both are gone, that $710 becomes additional investing capacity rather than lifestyle inflation, feeding directly into the [[217-emergency-fund-rebuild-plan]] and then the taxable brokerage account. We reviewed the option of refinancing the car loan but the credit union quote wasn't meaningfully better than our current rate, so we dropped it. We also briefly considered pausing the extra payments to build a bigger cash cushion first, but decided the emergency fund at three months of expenses was enough of a floor to keep the debt payoff moving at full speed without feeling reckless about it. We revisit that judgment call every quarter, and so far neither of us has felt the urge to slow down, even during the transmission recall scare that briefly worried us both.

## Milestones

Car loan under $5,000 by June 2026. Car loan fully paid by August 2026. Student loan under $6,000 by December 2026. Both loans paid off by March 2027, roughly five months ahead of the original end-of-2027 target if the extra-payment pace holds. We'll post an update to this doc at each milestone rather than waiting for the final payoff to celebrate.

## Risks and review cadence

Job loss or a major repair bill would pull the extra $400 back to the emergency fund first, debt payoff second. We've agreed that's the right order even though it slows this plan down. A rate increase on any variable-rate balance isn't a factor here since both loans are fixed. We check the spreadsheet every Sunday during the [[211-budget-review-sync]] and do a fuller review quarterly, adjusting the extra-payment amount if income changes, a bonus lands, or an unexpected expense eats into the buffer we've built for exactly this kind of plan. Quarterly reviews also compare actual payoff pace against the projection above, and we've adjusted the extra amount twice already this year, once up after a small raise and once down after the water heater failed.
