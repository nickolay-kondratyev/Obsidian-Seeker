Reading *Charge What It's Worth*, Kofi's recommendation, ahead of our pricing conversation for launch tiers.

## Core thesis

Pricing should track value delivered, not cost to build or competitor prices. Obvious, rarely practiced honestly.

## Notes on tiering

The author argues three tiers beat five: fewer decisions for the buyer, cleaner internal roadmap prioritization. Our current draft has four, worth revisiting given this chapter's argument.

## A useful framing

"Price is a signal of who you're for." Relevant to our freelancer debate.

## Where I disagree

The book dismisses usage-based pricing as confusing to buyers, too sweeping given how well it fits invoicing volume. Ledgerline customers already think in invoice counts, so per-invoice tiers might actually be clearer, not murkier, than the author assumes.

## Relevant to Ledgerline

Chapter six's annual-discount math informs the pricing page rewrite [[018-lotte-design-journal]] mentions. Worth a joint read, since our discount percentage was picked arbitrarily.

## Rating

Three stars. Chapter six earns it; everywhere else runs thin and repetitive.
