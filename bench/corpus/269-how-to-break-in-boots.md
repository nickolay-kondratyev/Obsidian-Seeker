The schedule I actually follow before trusting new boots on a trip, built after learning the hard way that a good store fit doesn't guarantee a good trail fit.

## Week One: Around the House

Wear them indoors, an hour or two an evening, with the exact socks you'll hike in. This is purely about the boot learning your foot's shape before any real weight or distance gets involved.

## Week Two: Short Walks

Neighborhood walks, thirty to sixty minutes, no pack yet, just breaking in the flex points.

## Week Three: Day Hikes

Move to real trail, four to six miles, still no pack weight added yet. This is where actual hot spots tend to show up for the first time, spots that indoor walking and flat sidewalks never reveal no matter how many hours you log in them. Note anywhere the boot rubs, even lightly, and address it before adding weight on top.

## Watch for Hot Spots

Any spot that's warm or pink after a hike gets taped preemptively on the next outing, before it becomes an actual blister worth worrying about.

## Week Four: Loaded Miles

Add your actual pack weight now, same day-hike distances at first. Loaded weight changes pressure points completely, especially across the top of the foot and around the ankle, so don't skip straight here from week one hoping to save time.

## The Fifty-Mile Rule

I don't trust boots on a trip until fifty real trail miles are logged.

## If Something's Still Wrong

If hot spots persist past week three even with taping, the boot may be the wrong shape for your foot entirely, not just unbroken. Don't push through it hoping four more weeks fixes a structural mismatch; that's a sign to return them while you still can rather than discover the same problem on day two of an actual trip.
