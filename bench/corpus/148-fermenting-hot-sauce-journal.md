April 9. Started my first lacto-fermented hot sauce batch today, using the glut of red jalapenos and two mystery pepper plants that turned out unexpectedly fierce this year, going by the one I bit into by accident while harvesting and immediately regretted. Two percent brine by weight of the peppers, garlic, and a chopped carrot for a little sweetness, all packed under a fermentation weight in the big crock Kofi lent me from the pantry inventory listed in [[147-ferment-pantry-inventory-project]] after his own crock finally filled up with this year's kimchi. The kitchen smelled sharply of garlic and chili within about an hour of packing the jar, a smell Priyam says she can already tell apart from every other ferment currently going in the house, which is apparently four at once right now if you count the sourdough. No visible bubbling yet, which I'm told is normal on day one, though I'll admit I checked twice already out of pure impatience rather than any real expectation of seeing something.

## The Setup

Recipe-wise I kept it deliberately simple for a first attempt: two pounds of peppers, four cloves of garlic, one carrot, and a two percent salt brine by total weight, no starter culture and no whey, just wild fermentation from whatever's naturally present on the peppers and in the kitchen air. Kofi was skeptical I'd get reliable results without a starter, citing a batch of his own that stalled out and eventually molded two summers ago, but Lotte pointed out that hot sauce ferments tend to be more forgiving than most because of the natural acidity and capsaicin already present, both of which apparently discourage a lot of the less friendly bacteria that ruin milder ferments before they get going properly. I weighed everything on the kitchen scale rather than eyeballing it, a habit I picked up watching the proofing box build in [[124-proofing-box-temp-control-howto]], since salt percentage seems to matter more here than in almost anything else I've fermented so far, and getting it wrong in either direction apparently ruins a batch in very different, equally annoying ways. Labeled the jar with today's date and the exact recipe, mostly so a good batch is actually repeatable rather than a happy accident I can't reconstruct in August when the peppers come in again. I also set the weight a little heavier than usual after reading that floating pepper matter is the single most common cause of a failed chili ferment, more so than salt ratio itself, and I'd rather over-correct on the weight than end up scraping mold off the surface a week from now and starting over from scratch with a smaller, sadder batch of peppers.

## Day 3

Real bubbling now, small steady streams rising off the carrot chunks, and the brine has gone properly cloudy in the way every fermentation guide promised it eventually would, a genuinely satisfying thing to see after two days of nothing but garlic smell and impatience. Burped the jar twice today, a small hiss escaping both times, which Kofi says is a good early sign fermentation is actually underway. The weight is holding everything submerged, no floaters yet, and the kitchen smell has shifted slightly from raw garlic toward something a touch more sour.

## Day 10

The color has shifted from bright red to a duller, more brownish orange, which apparently happens to most chili ferments and isn't a sign anything's gone wrong, just the natural result of the fermentation process breaking down the original pigments over time regardless of pepper variety. Tasted a tiny spoonful straight from the jar today, mostly out of curiosity, and it's already properly sour under the heat, closer to a good vinegar-based sauce than raw chili at this point, though clearly still a little rough and unbalanced compared to what I'm hoping the final blended sauce will taste like once everything settles further. No mold, no off smells, just a strong vinegary tang layered under what's genuinely a serious amount of heat from those mystery peppers, more than I expected given how mild the jalapenos alone would have been on their own without them mixed in. I'm blending in ten more days, per the loose plan I sketched out at the start, unless something about the smell or color changes drastically between now and then and makes me reconsider that timeline entirely. Priyam wants a milder batch next round using only the jalapenos, no mystery peppers involved at all, after tasting today's spoonful and visibly regretting the decision to sample it straight off a spoon rather than diluted into something more forgiving first, coughing dramatically for a solid minute afterward while insisting she was fine the whole time. The carrot has mostly softened and lost its bright orange color, which the fermentation guide I've been following says is expected and even a decent sign that the sugars have been properly consumed rather than sitting around feeding something less desirable. Checked the pH strip I finally remembered to buy after last week's trip to the hardware store, and it's reading solidly acidic already, comfortably below the threshold most guides list as safe for long-term storage, which is reassuring given how casually I approached the whole no-starter decision back on day one.

## Notes for the Blend

Planning to blend with a splash of the reserved brine rather than fresh vinegar, strain through a fine mesh for a smoother texture, and bottle in the small swing-top jars from the pantry inventory rather than anything larger, since this batch is modest and I'd rather not waste any of it sitting half-used in the back of the fridge for months.
