Annual volunteer coordination meeting, held in the clubhouse back room on a wet Saturday morning with coffee and the good biscuits Lotte brought. This meeting happens every January to plan marshal coverage, ride leader rotas, and event volunteering for the year ahead, and it's usually the best-attended committee meeting of the year because people actually want to be there rather than being dragged along. Present: the volunteer coordinator, both ride captains, the events lead, and about a dozen regular volunteers who showed up specifically for this one. Priyam chaired, Dana took minutes, and the whole thing ran about ninety minutes including a tea break halfway through, which nobody complained about given how much ground there was to cover before spring.

## Coverage Review

We went through last year's marshal coverage event by event, and the picture is decent but uneven. The spring century had thirteen marshals for eight rest and junction points, which felt about right, while the autumn hill climb only had four volunteers for a course that really needed six, leaving two junctions unmarshalled for part of the afternoon. Kofi, who organised that event, admitted he'd left the volunteer ask too late, posting in the group chat only ten days before rather than the usual six weeks. The consensus was that events need a named volunteer coordinator role separate from the event organiser, so nobody is trying to plan logistics and chase down marshals at the same time. Tomasz volunteered to take on exactly that role for the coming season, on the condition that event organisers give him at least eight weeks' notice for anything requiring more than four marshals. There was also discussion of whether marshals should get any kind of recognition, since it's unpaid and sometimes thankless work standing at a junction in the rain for three hours. We agreed on a simple thing: marshal names get read out and thanked at the post-event social, and anyone who marshals three or more events in a year gets a free club jersey at year end. Nobody thought this would fully solve the recruitment problem on its own, but everyone agreed that some visible acknowledgement was overdue and cheap enough to just do rather than debate further.

## Ride Leader Rota

The bigger discussion was the ride leader rota for weekly club rides, since we currently rely on five people to cover fifty-two Saturdays and it's starting to show. Dana has led something like eighteen rides this year alone, which is generous but not sustainable, and she said plainly that she'll need to step back next year unless more people are trained up. The room agreed this is the most urgent volunteering gap in the club right now, more urgent than event marshalling, because a cancelled weekly ride affects far more members than an under-marshalled one-off event. Priyam proposed a mentorship model: any member who's ridden with the club for at least a year and wants to lead can shadow an experienced leader for two rides, then lead their third ride with an experienced leader riding sweep at the back rather than out front. Six people in the room put their names down on the spot, which was more than anyone expected and genuinely lifted the mood. Lotte raised a fair concern about consistency: if ten different people are leading rides with ten different styles, some riders might get a stricter no-drop pace one week and a faster, looser pace the next, which could be confusing for newer members trying to figure out which group suits them. We agreed to write a short one-page guide covering pace expectations, regroup points, and what to do if someone flats or falls behind, so new leaders have a consistent baseline without it turning into a rigid rulebook nobody wants to follow. Tomasz will draft that guide by the end of the month and circulate it for comment before it's treated as final. The hope is that a short, practical document will actually get read and used, unlike some of the longer policy documents the club has produced in the past that mostly sit unread in a shared folder nobody opens. Dana added that whatever gets written should also cover the wet-weather route substitutions discussed at [[091-tuesday-ride-planning-huddle]], so new leaders aren't left guessing on a rainy Saturday morning.

## Event Volunteering for the Year

We mapped out the full calendar of events needing volunteers: the spring century in June, the youth program open day in July connected to [[107-youth-cycling-program]], the charity ride fundraiser in September, and the autumn hill climb in October. Each event now has a named volunteer coordinator reporting to Tomasz rather than juggling it themselves, and each has a target headcount set based on last year's actual attendance rather than optimistic guesses, which was a recurring problem in past planning. The spring century needs fourteen marshals given expected growth in entries, the youth open day needs eight adults for supervision ratios that the insurance policy actually requires rather than just recommends, the charity ride needs ten split across marshalling and the finish-line refreshment table, and the hill climb needs six at minimum, with eight as the comfortable target given last year's shortfall. Recruitment will start eight weeks before each event rather than the ad hoc timing that's caused problems before, using a shared sign-up sheet pinned in the group chat and also mentioned at the weekly rides so it reaches members who don't check the chat closely. Dana suggested a standing reminder posted every Monday during the recruitment window, which the room liked enough to adopt as a default rather than something to reconsider event by event. It was a small change but it closes off the excuse of forgetting to post the ask, which has quietly killed more than one volunteer drive in past years without anyone quite noticing until it was too late to fix.

## Decisions and Actions

> [!tip] Headline outcome
> Tomasz is now the standing volunteer coordinator for events, and six new ride leader candidates are lined up for mentorship starting in February.

Action items agreed before everyone headed out into the rain: Tomasz drafts the ride leader guide by month end; Priyam pairs the six new candidates with mentors within two weeks; each event now needs a named volunteer coordinator confirmed before it goes on the public calendar; and Dana sets up the standing Monday recruitment reminder starting with the spring century campaign. Everyone left with a clear sense of who owns what, which hasn't always been true after past versions of this meeting. Priyam closed by noting that this was the most concrete this meeting has felt in years, with names attached to deadlines rather than vague good intentions that quietly evaporate by February. Dana will circulate typed-up minutes with the full action list by Wednesday, and the volunteer sign-up sheet for the spring century goes live on the club website the same week, well ahead of the eight-week recruitment window everyone agreed on.
