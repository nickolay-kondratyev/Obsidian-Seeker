# Reading Notes: *Designing for the Whole Table*

Chapter on shared-goal games. Argues traps stem from information asymmetry, not difficulty. Relevant to [[273-tidewatch-design-journal-storm-mechanic]].

## Alpha Player Problem

p. 34: one loud player solving the puzzle kills engagement fast. Matches our early captain-role complaints.

## Fixing It

The book's fix: give each player exclusive information nobody else can see. p. 41-47 has three case studies, none board-heavy, but the principle transfers to our warden roles well.

## Failure States

p. 58: losing should feel earned, not random. Echoes Kofi's storm-deck complaint.

## Quote to Keep

"A cooperative game where one player is right and everyone else is a spectator isn't cooperative, it's a lecture." p. 39. Worth pinning near the top of [[280-tidewatch-cooperative-design-article]] as a framing line for the whole piece.

## Applying It

Cross-reference this against our wind-token economy; the exclusive-information principle maps onto what the storm deck already does, and it's worth checking whether roles hide enough from each other.

## Rating

Four stars, genuinely useful, though a little repetitive after chapter three drags on.
