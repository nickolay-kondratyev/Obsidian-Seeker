Tuesday, April 14. Cold for April, and windy at the stop, which I did not plan for well enough — I brought a clipboard and no way to weigh it down, and lost two consent forms to a gust before 8am. Lesson noted for next week: binder clips, or better, a clipboard with a built-in cover. I set up at the Linden Ave stop on the eastbound corridor, the one with the newer shelter but the cracked curb ramp that Voss's framework would flag immediately (see [[155-reading-notes-curb-cuts]]). Planned window was 7:00 to 10:00, the morning peak, since that's when the audit showed the biggest gap between scheduled and observed boardings. I recruited Lotte to help with intercepts since two people can cover both directions of the platform without missing riders, and she'd done intercept work before for a class project so I didn't have to explain the whole protocol from scratch. We used the short-form survey — six questions, maybe ninety seconds per rider if they're willing — trip purpose, how they got to the stop, any difficulty getting to the stop, alternatives considered, disability status (self-report, optional), and a five-point scale on stop condition. Compensation was a $5 gift card, which I'd budgeted for forty completions today, in hindsight optimistic for a three-hour window at one stop. By 7:30 I'd already second-guessed the site selection twice — foot traffic felt thinner than the ridership counts suggested, though that might just be the cold keeping people in cars instead of walking the way they would in warmer weather.

## What we heard

By the end of the window we had thirty-one completed surveys, twenty-six clean enough to use and five with enough missing data that I'll probably drop them rather than guess. The refusal rate was rough — I counted forty-plus people who saw the clipboard, made eye contact, and kept walking, which is its own kind of data I'm not systematically capturing and probably should be. Of the completions, eleven mentioned some difficulty reaching the stop, and the specifics were more useful than the audit alone would have told me: three people flagged the missing curb cut on the north corner specifically, not the shelter or the bench, which tracks with what Voss argues about curb cuts being the binding constraint rather than amenities. One rider, an older man with a rolling cart, said flatly that he times his trips around which crossing has a working signal, and that the intersection two blocks south is faster but he avoids it because the countdown timer is too short for him to cross safely — that's exactly the kind of qualitative detail my third research question is built to catch, and it didn't show up anywhere in the 311 complaint data I pulled last month. Around 9am a Metro supervisor stopped by, curious what we were doing with a clipboard at her stop, and I explained the study; she was more helpful than I expected, mentioning that this stop had been flagged internally for a shelter upgrade next year but nothing about the curb cut, which is its own small finding about what agencies notice versus what riders notice. Lotte caught a pattern I'd missed — nearly everyone who mentioned difficulty was traveling with something, a cart, a stroller, a bag of groceries — worth checking against the survey data once it's coded, since "traveling with an object" isn't a variable I built into the instrument and I may need to add a follow-up question for the next round. A woman waiting for the 42 also volunteered, unprompted, that she'd stopped taking the bus twice a week after the shelter roof started leaking last winter — worth flagging for the survey's open-comment field going forward.

## Wrapping up

Packed up around 10:15, later than planned because a rider wanted to talk for a while after finishing the survey — not usable data exactly, more just someone glad to be asked. Field notes app crashed once and I lost about ten minutes of typed observations, which is annoying but not catastrophic since I still had the paper forms. Biggest procedural takeaway: three hours at one stop is too long for one person to sustain full attention, refusal-tracking degrades noticeably after the first ninety minutes, and I was clearly less consistent about logging refusals in the second half of the window. Need to either shorten sessions to two hours or bring a third person to rotate. Lotte thinks two hours is closer to the real limit, and given how tired we both were by the end I'm inclined to agree with her rather than push for a longer window next time just to hit a higher completion count. Worth mentioning to Priyam too, since the survey design assumed three-hour windows across all six planned sites and that assumption now looks shaky.
