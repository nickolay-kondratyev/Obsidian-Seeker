Planning document for the Sierra High Route, a cross-country traverse through the Sierra Nevada, tentatively planned for next August as our biggest trip yet. Roughly one hundred ninety-five miles, mostly off-trail, connecting a chain of high passes rather than following a maintained trail corridor for most of its length. Priyam floated this half-jokingly after the JMT trip but it's become a real plan over the past month of research and route study, and now it's the thing all three of us keep bringing up unprompted.

## Scope and Approach

This is a significant step up from anything we've done before: sustained off-trail travel, class two and three scrambling on several passes, and route-finding that depends on map and compass skills rather than following a worn path. We're planning to split it into two long trips instead of one continuous eighteen-day push, doing the northern half one August and the southern half the following year, since none of us can realistically take eighteen consecutive days off work right now. The northern half alone is around one hundred miles with roughly forty thousand feet of elevation gain, which is more sustained climbing than any trip we've attempted, including the Wonderland Trail loop. This year is a preparation and scouting year for us.

## Skills Gap

Skills gap analysis: none of us has significant class three scrambling experience, so we're planning a mountaineering skills course this spring covering exposed scrambling, basic rope work, and glacier travel basics even though the route itself doesn't cross a glacier. Navigation is the other big gap; we've always relied on maintained trails and GPS apps, but off-trail travel through granite basins requires reading contour lines and terrain features in a way none of us have real practice with yet. Kofi is taking a weekend navigation course in the spring specifically to build this skill, and we're planning to do at least three off-trail weekend trips this year practicing route-finding before attempting anything at Sierra High Route scale and commitment. Physical conditioning also needs to go up a level from our usual training; sustained days above eleven thousand feet with a loaded pack demand a different kind of fitness than the lower-elevation multi-day trips we've done so far. We're also researching permit requirements, since some wilderness areas on this route use quota systems that fill up months ahead, so we need to lock in dates earlier than usual.

## Gear Needs

Gear needs reassessment too: an ice axe for early-season snow on some passes, possibly microspikes depending on timing, and a lighter overall kit given the sustained climbing and elevation. Bear canisters are required for the entire route, adding real weight we'll need to plan around carefully given everything else we're already carrying.

## Next Steps

Next steps: book the mountaineering skills course for April, plan three off-trail practice weekends starting in May, and research permit windows for both halves of the route so we can apply as early as the system allows. See [[248-jmt-trip-report-journal]] for the trip that sparked this idea, and [[259-how-to-train-for-elevation-gain]] for the training approach we're adapting for a higher-elevation, more technical objective than anything we've attempted before. This is a multi-year project realistically, and that's fine; some trips are worth the longer runway. We're treating the preparation itself as part of the appeal rather than an obstacle to get through, since the skills we're building will carry over into every trip we do after this one, not just the route itself. First off-trail practice weekend is tentatively booked for the third weekend in May, weather permitting.
