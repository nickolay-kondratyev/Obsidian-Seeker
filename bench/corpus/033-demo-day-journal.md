Demo started today and the kitchen is now, officially, a room with no floor and half a wall. I woke up early, mostly out of nerves, and made coffee in the makeshift setup we rigged in the garage last week: a hot plate, an electric kettle, and a folding table that's already collecting sawdust. Kofi's crew arrived at eight sharp with a dumpster that barely fit down the driveway, and by nine the old laminate counters were already gone, hauled out in three pieces that used to be one continuous slab. I hadn't realized how much I'd stopped seeing the kitchen as it actually was until it was gone; the 1970s cabinets, the dated hardware, all of it had become invisible background over years of living with it. Watching it come apart was strangely emotional, not because I'll miss any of it but because the house suddenly feels unfinished and a little vulnerable, walls exposed in ways they haven't been probably since the place was built. Priyam came by at lunch to check the plumbing before the crew closed anything up, and he found exactly what we feared: the drain line under the sink is cast iron, original to the house, and it's starting to flake rust into the water. That's not part of the current scope, technically, but it's not something we can responsibly leave either, so we're going to have to make a call this week about extending the plumbing work. It's the first real surprise of the project and I suspect it won't be the last, given the house's age. Still, there's something satisfying about seeing physical progress after months of planning documents and supplier calls.

## Finding the Past

By early afternoon the crew had the old flooring up too, down to subfloor, and underneath we found a layer of what looks like the original 1962 linoleum, a faded checkerboard pattern in mint and cream that nobody in the neighborhood would remember. I took photos before they hauled it out, partly out of curiosity and partly because it felt worth documenting; there's something grounding about seeing a physical trace of the house's first version, laid by someone whose name I'll never know, sixty-some years before anyone worried about zellige tile or matte sage cabinets. Behind the wall where the old upper cabinets used to hang, we also found a section of newspaper someone had used as insulation backing, dated from the spring of 1962, mostly illegible from water staining but with a legible ad for a hardware store that clearly no longer exists. Kofi said this kind of thing turns up in maybe one out of every four bungalow kitchens he demos, which made me feel a little better about how unprepared I felt for it, and he mentioned finding a full glass milk bottle wedged behind a stud in another job last year, which is a bar I clearly won't be clearing today. The crew wrapped for the day around four, leaving the room in a state that's hard to describe as anything but a shell: exposed studs, subfloor, and a single bare bulb hanging where the light fixture used to be, casting long shadows across the plywood the moment the sun dropped behind the neighbor's fence. Tomorrow they start the framing changes for the new pantry wall, and Priyam is coming back with an actual quote for the drain line replacement so we can decide whether to fold it into the current budget or treat it as its own smaller project entirely separate from the kitchen scope we originally signed off on, which would mean pulling a second permit but might actually be the more honest way to handle it given how unrelated the two jobs really are.

## Tomorrow

Framing for the pantry wall starts, and I'm hoping to be around when Priyam brings the drain quote so we can decide on the spot rather than let it drag into another week of scheduling calls. I also want to ask Kofi whether the newspaper find is worth keeping; there's a version of me that wants to frame a scrap of it once it's dry, as a small piece of the house's history to hang somewhere in the finished kitchen, maybe near the window where the light hits best in the afternoon. I should also call the plumbing supply place about the cast iron fitting before Priyam arrives, so we're not stuck waiting another day on a part that might need to be special-ordered given how old the existing pipe run actually is.
