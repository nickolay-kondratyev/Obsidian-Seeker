# Print Proof Day

February 3, 2026. The physical print proof arrived today, and I've been staring at the box on my kitchen table for twenty minutes instead of opening it, which is a ridiculous way to behave about cardboard. Tomasz drove it over himself rather than waiting for me to pick it up from the print vendor, which I appreciated more than I said out loud. We've been working toward this exact moment since the manufacturing plan meeting back in the fall, and there's a specific kind of nervousness that comes from finally seeing something you've only known as a PDF turned into an object you can hold and get slightly annoyed at if a corner dents wrong.

I opened it slowly, the way you'd open something fragile even though it's a board game and not glass. The box lid fit better than expected; Priyam's cover art reads clean at print size, which isn't guaranteed, since art that looks great on screen can go muddy once it's printed on cardstock under factory lighting. The lighthouse in the storm held its detail. The tide gauge board unfolded with a satisfying snap and the registration was close to perfect, just a faint hairline shift on the coastline border that I doubt anyone but Priyam would ever notice.

The components themselves are where things get more interesting, and where I spent most of the afternoon. The tide tokens feel lighter than the working prototype set, which used scavenged tokens from an old game none of us play anymore. The storm cards have a matte finish that doesn't glare under lamp light, which matters for something we'll be demoing at a convention table under bad fluorescents.

## First Full Setup

I laid every component out on the table exactly the way Kofi described in the setup diagram, just to see if a stranger following only the box art could get through setup without opening the rulebook, and mostly they could, with one exception around the keeper meeples that took me a minute to sort out on my own.

The four keeper meeples are supposed to be color-coded to match the player boards, but in this proof run, two of the colors read almost identically under warm indoor light: the "warden blue" and the "storm-grey" meeples looked like the same piece from across the table until I held them side by side near the window. That's exactly the kind of thing you never catch on a monitor, where the colors are calibrated and consistent, but that becomes a real problem the first time someone tries to set up the game in a dim room at a friend's kitchen table, which is where most of our actual target players will be playing it. I texted Priyam a photo immediately, along with Dana, since this affects both the physical color separation and the underlying palette choice, and got a reply within the hour agreeing it needed fixing before the full print run, not after. Small thing, but exactly the kind of small thing a proof run exists to catch, and exactly why the whole team insisted on paying for one instead of trusting the digital preview alone, no matter how good the calibration on Priyam's monitor supposedly is.

The rulebook itself printed cleanly, no smudging, no misaligned page numbers, and the FAQ insert Kofi wrote sits nicely folded into the box lid where we planned for it to go. I read through the full setup sequence cover to cover for the first time as a finished object rather than a stapled draft, and it held up better than I expected; the diagrams Kofi and Priyam collaborated on translate well at the smaller printed size, though the storm-track legend on page six is small enough that I'd want reading glasses in low light, which is worth flagging even if nobody wants to redesign that page again this late.

## Calling It In

I texted the whole group tonight: the box, the boards, and the card stock all pass. The meeple color issue is the one real blocker, and everyone agreed within an hour that it needs a fix before the full run goes to press. I also flagged the page-six legend as a nice-to-have, not a blocker, since reprinting the entire rulebook layout this close to launch would push the timeline further than anyone wants. Lotte asked whether the color fix affects the Kickstarter photography timeline at all, and Tomasz thinks it can be resolved with the vendor in under a week without touching the print date, since it is a token-mold color swap rather than a full reprint. I'm relieved, honestly. Holding the finished object made the whole project feel real in a way the spreadsheets never quite managed, and I keep picking the box back up just to look at it again. There's something almost embarrassing about how much a slightly heavier cardboard box can move you, but here we are, two years into a project that started as a scribbled storm-track idea on a napkin.
